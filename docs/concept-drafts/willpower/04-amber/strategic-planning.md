# Willpower / Amber — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** Planning and executing multi-step commitments — how the player structures, sequences, and sustains obligations across time.
> **Why this axis for this module:** Duty-will at Amber requires planning WITHIN the code, not planning to optimise the code. The strategic-planning modality reveals whether the player can structure their obligations effectively (healthy) or whether they use planning to bypass duty (golden-addiction) or cannot plan at all (dark-allergy).

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Order's Campaign"
- **Core mechanic:** The player plans and executes a multi-step campaign on behalf of their order. Each step is an obligation; the campaign is a sustained commitment. Planning must serve the duty, not replace it. The game tests whether the player can structure obligations effectively while maintaining the vow that binds them.
- **Duration:** 5-12 minutes per session (campaign complexity scales with demonstrated capacity)
- **Internal progression:** The Mission Brief → The Duty-Map → The Execution → The Unexpected → The Campaign Complete

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**What this modality uniquely presents:** The PLANNING dimension of duty-will — how the player structures their obligations across time. Unlike deterministic (which measures raw holding), this modality measures whether the player can ORGANISE their duty effectively without using planning as a substitute for genuine commitment.

**How this differs from other modalities:** Unlike scenario-choice (which measures single-decision quality), this modality measures multi-step commitment planning. Unlike embodied-somatic (which measures physical holding), this modality measures cognitive structuring of obligations.

**What it uniquely surfaces:** Golden-addiction (elaborate planning without underlying duty-will), dark-allergy (cannot plan; impulsive execution), and the quality of the player's relationship to obligation-structure.

**Successful integration:** The player plans effectively within the code — structuring obligations clearly, executing them sequentially, adapting when unexpected events arise — without using planning to bypass or rationalise away the duty.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Order's Campaign: a mission-planning interface where the player receives a campaign brief from their order and must plan and execute it. The campaign has multiple steps, each representing an obligation. Aesthetic: Amber-stage military order — maps, mission briefs, duty-rosters, the structure of organised obligation.

### Interaction
- **The Mission Brief (1-5):** Simple 3-step campaign. "Your order requires: secure the gate, deliver the message, return to post." Tests basic obligation-sequencing.
- **The Duty-Map (5-15):** Player plans the campaign before executing. "Map your obligations. What comes first? What depends on what?" Tests planning-within-the-code.
- **The Execution (15-30):** Execute the planned campaign. Tests whether the plan holds under execution pressure.
- **The Unexpected (30-50):** Mid-campaign disruption. "The gate is already secured. Your plan must adapt. But the duty remains." Tests adaptive commitment.
- **The Campaign Complete (50+):** Full integration: planning + execution + adaptation + sustained commitment simultaneously.

### Feedback
- Effective planning → "The campaign is mapped. Each obligation is clear. The order is served."
- Over-planning → "The plan is elaborate. But the first step is simple. Can you take it without the full plan?"
- No planning → "The campaign begins without a map. The duty is real. But without structure, how will you hold it?"
- Plan-as-bypass → "You planned to optimise the outcome. But the order gave you a duty, not an optimisation problem. What does the duty require?"
- Adaptive commitment → "The unexpected arrived. The plan changed. The duty held. This is mastery."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Campaign length: 3 steps → 5 steps → 8 steps → 12 steps
- Planning complexity: simple sequence → branching → conditional → multi-constraint
- Disruption frequency: none → occasional → frequent → continuous
- Obligation hierarchy: single duty → competing duties → duty-portfolio

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The Mission Brief | 1-5 | Simple 3-step obligation sequence |
| The Duty-Map | 5-15 | Planning within the code |
| The Execution | 15-30 | Plan-execution under pressure |
| The Unexpected | 30-50 | Adaptive commitment |
| The Campaign Complete | 50+ | Full planning-duty integration |

---

## 4. Item Pool

- **Campaign briefs:** Multi-step obligation sequences of varying complexity — 25+
- **Planning tasks:** Obligation-sequencing and dependency-mapping challenges — 20+
- **Execution tasks:** Plan-execution under varying pressure — 20+
- **Disruption scenarios:** Mid-campaign unexpected events requiring adaptation — 15+
- **Duty-portfolio tasks:** Managing multiple simultaneous obligations — 10+

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input types:** Tap (step selection); drag (obligation sequencing); hold (commitment confirmation); swipe (plan navigation)
- **Timing:** Planning time tracked; execution latency measured; adaptation speed recorded; abandonment patterns detected
- **NPC/AI:** Adaptive campaign complexity; disruption-event generation; plan-quality assessment; shadow-signal accumulator; duty-portfolio management
- **LLM:** Campaign narrative; disruption event generation; plan-quality assessment for complex cases
- **State persistence:** Full campaign history; planning patterns; execution quality; adaptation responses; abandonment rates; cross-session campaign continuity; drive/shadow signal accumulation; checkpoint position
