# Willpower / Amber — Social-Cooperative Game Concept

> **Axis:** Shared obligation — how the player holds commitments within a group, coordinates through shared duty, and maintains their vow when others break theirs.
> **Why this axis for this module:** Duty-will at Amber is fundamentally GROUP-EMBEDDED. The code is the group's code; the vow is witnessed by the group; the obligation is to the collective. The social-cooperative modality reveals whether the player's duty-will is genuinely relational or merely individual.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Brotherhood's Oath"
- **Core mechanic:** The player and NPC companions take a shared oath and must hold it together. Each member's commitment affects the group's capacity. The player must maintain their own vow while supporting others in maintaining theirs — and must decide what to do when a companion breaks their oath.
- **Duration:** 5-12 minutes per session (group complexity scales with demonstrated capacity)
- **Internal progression:** The Shared Oath → The Group Watch → The Companion's Breach → The Code Enforced → The Brotherhood Holds

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**What this modality uniquely presents:** The RELATIONAL dimension of duty-will — how commitment functions within a group. Unlike deterministic (which measures individual holding), this modality measures whether the player's duty-will is genuinely embedded in shared obligation or merely performed for the group.

**How this differs from other modalities:** Unlike scenario-choice (which measures individual decision quality), this modality measures group-embedded commitment. Unlike embodied-somatic (which measures physical holding), this modality measures the social texture of obligation.

**What it uniquely surfaces:** The vow-slave enforces the code on others. The oath-breaker cannot coordinate through shared obligations. The premature strategist dismisses the group code as irrational. The code-clinger cannot form new group obligations outside the existing code.

**Successful integration:** The player holds their own vow, supports companions in holding theirs, responds appropriately when companions breach, and maintains the group's shared commitment without either enforcing rigidly or abandoning collectively.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Brotherhood: a group-interface where the player and 2-3 NPC companions take a shared oath and must hold it through a series of challenges. Each companion has their own commitment-style (one tends toward vow-slavery, one toward oath-breaking, one toward code-clinging). The player must navigate the group's shared obligation. Aesthetic: Amber-stage brotherhood — guild hall, shared oath, the weight of collective commitment.

### Interaction
- **The Shared Oath (1-5):** Group takes a simple shared commitment. "We will hold the hall until dawn." Tests basic group-obligation formation.
- **The Group Watch (5-15):** Group maintains the commitment together. Tests coordination through shared duty.
- **The Companion's Breach (15-30):** One companion breaks the oath. "Aldric has left his post. What do you do?" Tests response to others' commitment-failure.
- **The Code Enforced (30-50):** The group must decide whether to enforce the code on the breacher. Tests the player's relationship to collective obligation-enforcement.
- **The Brotherhood Holds (50+):** Full integration: individual vow + group coordination + breach-response + code-enforcement simultaneously.

### Feedback
- Group oath held → "The brotherhood holds. Each member's word is the group's strength. The hall stands."
- Companion breach ignored → "Aldric left. You held. But the group's oath was broken. What does the brotherhood mean if one member's breach is ignored?"
- Companion breach enforced rigidly → "Aldric was punished. The code was enforced. But was this justice or rigidity? The brotherhood asks."
- Group oath abandoned → "The hall was left. The oath was broken together. Notice: was this a sovereign group decision or collective impulse?"
- New group obligation formed → "A new oath is offered. The brotherhood can grow. Can you hold a new commitment alongside the old?"

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Group size: 2 companions → 3 companions → 4 companions
- Companion commitment-styles: simple → complex → conflicting
- Breach frequency: rare → occasional → frequent
- Code-enforcement complexity: clear → ambiguous → contested
- New obligation complexity: simple → competing → identity-level

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The Shared Oath | 1-5 | Simple group commitment formation |
| The Group Watch | 5-15 | Group coordination through shared duty |
| The Companion's Breach | 15-30 | Response to others' commitment-failure |
| The Code Enforced | 30-50 | Collective obligation-enforcement |
| The Brotherhood Holds | 50+ | Full group duty-will integration |

## 4. Item Pool

- **Shared oath scenarios:** Group commitment formation of varying complexity — 20+
- **Group watch tasks:** Coordinated commitment maintenance — 20+
- **Companion breach scenarios:** Responses to others' commitment-failure — 20+
- **Code-enforcement scenarios:** Collective obligation-enforcement decisions — 15+
- **New obligation scenarios:** Group commitment expansion — 10+

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input types:** Tap (choice selection); hold (commitment confirmation); swipe (companion interaction)
- **Timing:** Response latency to companion breach tracked; enforcement decision time measured; group coordination quality assessed
- **NPC/AI:** Companion commitment-style simulation; breach-event generation; group-dynamic modelling; shadow-signal accumulator; collective obligation tracking
- **LLM:** Companion dialogue; breach-event narration; enforcement-consequence delivery; group-dynamic adaptation
- **State persistence:** Full group oath history; companion interaction patterns; breach-response quality; enforcement decisions; cross-session group commitment continuity; drive/shadow signal accumulation; checkpoint position
