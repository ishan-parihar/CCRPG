# Willpower / Green — Scenario-Choice Game Concept

> **Axis:** Commitment dilemmas where multiple valid goals compete.  **Why this axis for this module:** Pluralistic will is forged in the crucible of genuine dilemma — scenarios where no single answer is correct force the player to demonstrate authentic pluralistic commitment.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Name:** The Council of Causes  
**Core loop:** Player inhabits a community leader navigating commitment dilemmas — allocating limited resources, time, and energy across competing legitimate causes. Each choice reveals shadow-structure through what is chosen, refused, deferred, or abandoned.  
**Session length:** 5–10 minutes per dilemma arc.  
**Infinite checkpoint:** Yes — each dilemma resolution is a natural save point.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst → Experience → Integration:**  
- *Catalyst:* Dilemmas present genuinely competing goods — not good-vs-evil but good-vs-good. Community garden vs. youth mentorship. Personal creative project vs. collective infrastructure.  
- *Experience:* Player feels the weight of pluralistic commitment — the impossibility of doing everything, the necessity of choosing without dismissing.  
- *Integration:* Successful navigation demonstrates that commitment can be genuine even when partial — that "not now" is not "never."

**Shadow surfacing:**  
- DA: Choosing "defer all" repeatedly, requesting more information indefinitely, cycling between options without landing.  
- DAll: Choosing solo-execution paths consistently, dismissing collaborative options, framing group-process as inefficient.  
- GA: Choosing "transcend the dilemma" options that avoid concrete commitment ("the universe will provide").  
- GAll: Refusing to prioritise when stakes demand it, insisting all causes receive equal attention despite asymmetric urgency.

## 3. Game Design

**Mechanics:**  
- Branching dilemma trees with 4–6 response options per node, each mapping to drive×shadow coordinates.  
- No "correct" answer — but patterns across dilemmas reveal shadow-structure.  
- Consequences unfold over multiple sessions: choices made in round 1 create the context for round 5.  
- Community NPCs reflect back the player's commitment pattern (diegetic feedback without breaking immersion).  
- Collaborative options require "trust investment" — delayed payoff but multiplied impact.

**Difficulty staircase:**  
- Floor: Binary dilemmas with clear stakes and generous timelines.  
- Ceiling: Multi-stakeholder dilemmas with time pressure, incomplete information, and cascading consequences.

**Drive probing:**  
- Agency: Does the player maintain personal vision within community demands?  
- Communion: Does the player genuinely consider others' stakes or perform consideration?  
- Eros: Does the player reach toward synthesis or collapse into either/or?  
- Agape: Does the player honour existing commitments or chase novelty?

## 4. Item Pool

| Dilemma Category | Example | Shadow Targeted |
|---|---|---|
| Resource scarcity | Three worthy projects, funding for one | DA (paralysis) / GAll (equal-split) |
| Personal-vs-collective | Your art practice vs. community need | DAll (solo choice) / Communion |
| Urgent-vs-important | Crisis response vs. long-term building | DA (overwhelm) / Agency |
| Trust-required | Collaborative path with uncertain partners | DAll (refusal) / Communion |
| Transcendence escape | "Step back from all of it" option | GA (bypass) |
| Hierarchy invitation | "One cause IS more urgent — can you name it?" | GAll (refusal to prioritise) |

**Adaptive generation:** Dilemma frequency and type shift based on detected shadow-pattern. Healing dilemmas are introduced once shadow is confirmed — scenarios that make the healthy response feel natural.

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Metrics captured:** Choice-latency per dilemma, option-cycling count, collaborative-path selection rate, defer-rate, prioritisation willingness, consequence-awareness (references to prior choices).  
- **Scoring:** Pattern-based across 10+ dilemmas; no single choice is scored in isolation.  
- **Narrative engine:** Branching consequence system with 3-session memory; NPC relationship tracking.  
- **State persistence:** Full choice history; consequence chains; NPC relationship states.  
- **Cross-line hooks:** Moral line (ethical reasoning in choices), Emotional line (empathic consideration), Interpersonal line (NPC relationship quality).
