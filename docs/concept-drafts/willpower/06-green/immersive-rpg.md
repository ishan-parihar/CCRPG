# Willpower / Green — Immersive-RPG Game Concept

> **Axis:** Ecological pluralistic will — whether collaborative commitment appears spontaneously in free-play.  **Why this axis for this module:** The deepest test of pluralistic will is whether it emerges naturally when no explicit prompt demands it — free-play reveals internalised capacity vs. performed compliance.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Name:** The Living Village  
**Core loop:** Player inhabits a pluralistic community as a free agent — no explicit goals are assigned. Multiple ongoing community projects, relationships, and personal pursuits exist simultaneously. The game observes what the player commits to, how they balance, and whether collaborative will emerges organically.  
**Session length:** 5–15 minutes of open exploration per checkpoint.  
**Infinite checkpoint:** Yes — autosave at any natural pause.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst → Experience → Integration:**  
- *Catalyst:* The village presents ongoing needs, opportunities, and invitations without forcing engagement. Multiple NPCs pursue their own goals; the player can join, initiate, observe, or withdraw.  
- *Experience:* Player experiences the felt-sense of pluralistic will (or its absence) — the natural pull toward one thing, the guilt of neglecting another, the joy of genuine collaboration.  
- *Integration:* Over sessions, the village reflects the player's commitment-pattern back through NPC responses, project states, and community health — all diegetic, never diagnostic.

**Shadow surfacing:**  
- DA: Wandering between projects without sustained engagement, starting many things and finishing none, visible overwhelm in navigation patterns.  
- DAll: Pursuing only solo projects, ignoring community invitations, optimising personal progression while village needs go unmet.  
- GA: Performing "presence" without commitment — visiting projects but never investing, claiming to "hold space" while contributing nothing concrete.  
- GAll: Engaging all projects at identical shallow depth, refusing to deepen any one relationship or commitment over others.

## 3. Game Design

**Mechanics:**  
- Open-world village with 5–8 ongoing community projects at various stages.  
- NPCs have autonomous goals and will progress (or stagnate) with or without player involvement.  
- No quest markers or explicit objectives — player discovers needs through exploration and relationship.  
- Commitment is measured by sustained engagement over sessions, not single interactions.  
- Collaborative projects require repeated coordinated effort with NPCs across multiple sessions.  
- Personal projects (art, building, cultivation) exist alongside community ones — balance is player-determined.

**Difficulty staircase:**  
- Floor: Village is small (3 projects, 5 NPCs), needs are clear, pace is gentle.  
- Ceiling: Village is complex (8 projects, 12 NPCs), needs are subtle, competing urgencies emerge, collaborative projects require precise coordination.

**Drive probing:**  
- Agency: Does the player initiate personal projects and maintain them?  
- Communion: Does the player join collaborative efforts and sustain them across sessions?  
- Eros: Does the player reach toward more complex commitments as capacity grows?  
- Agape: Does the player return to neglected commitments and relationships?

## 4. Item Pool

| Ecological Element | Function | Shadow Targeted |
|---|---|---|
| Neglected project | Community project visibly declining without attention | DA (overwhelm) / Agape (return) |
| Solo opportunity | High-reward personal project requiring isolation | DAll (lone-achiever bait) |
| "Wander" affordance | Beautiful but purposeless exploration paths | GA (presence without commitment) |
| Equal-need moment | Multiple projects at identical urgency simultaneously | GAll (flat engagement) |
| Collaborative invitation | NPC explicitly invites sustained partnership | Communion (genuine joining) |
| Long-arc project | Commitment requiring 5+ sessions of sustained effort | Agency (sovereign persistence) |

**Adaptive generation:** Village state evolves based on player's commitment-pattern. Neglected areas visibly decline (diegetic feedback). Sustained commitments visibly flourish. NPCs respond relationally to player's presence or absence.

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Metrics captured:** Session-to-session engagement continuity, project-depth (interactions per project), collaborative-vs-solo ratio, initiation rate, return-to-neglected rate, commitment-duration distribution, NPC relationship depth.  
- **Scoring:** Ecological — no per-action score. Pattern emerges across 5+ sessions. Composite of commitment-depth × breadth × collaborative engagement × sustainability.  
- **World engine:** Persistent village state; autonomous NPC goal-pursuit; project progression/decay systems.  
- **State persistence:** Full village state across sessions; NPC memory; project histories.  
- **Cross-line hooks:** All lines observable in free-play — Interpersonal (NPC relationships), Emotional (affect in engagement), Cognitive (planning behaviour), Moral (fairness in resource allocation).
