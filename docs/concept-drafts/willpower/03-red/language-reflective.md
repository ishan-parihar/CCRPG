# Willpower / Red — Language-Reflective Game Concept

> **Axis:** The language-reflective axis probes willpower capacity through VERBAL/METACOGNITIVE access — can the player articulate their volitional experience? Can they name what they're willing, describe how it feels to sustain effort, explain why they stopped, predict what they'll commit to next? This modality accesses the CONSCIOUS-ACCESS dimension of will: not "can you will" but "can you KNOW that you're willing?"
>
> **Why this axis for Willpower/Red:** At Red, will is raw and often pre-reflective — "I just DO it." The language-reflective axis tests whether volitional intelligence has reached conscious access. Can the player name their goals? Describe their effort? Explain their fatigue? This is the bridge between blind will and self-aware will — the beginning of volitional metacognition.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Vow-Speaker"
- **Core mechanic:** The player articulates their volitional experience — naming goals, describing effort-states, explaining commitment/abandonment, predicting future volitional behaviour — with LLM evaluation of volitional vocabulary depth, accuracy, and self-awareness.
- **Duration:** 4-7 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** Naming → Describing → Explaining → Predicting → Teaching

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** The game asks the player to PUT WORDS to their volitional experience. After a brief effort-task, the game asks: "What did you just commit to? How did it feel to hold? Why did you stop?" The contact boundary is: "Can you access your will through language?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* The player's relationship to volitional self-knowledge surfaces. Can they name their goals (or are goals pre-verbal)? Can they describe effort (or is effort invisible to them)? Can they explain their patterns (or are they blind to their own will)?
- *Emergent:* The pull toward volitional self-awareness — can they see their own willing clearly enough to speak it?

**Integration path:** The game rewards ACCURATE volitional self-description. Not eloquence — ACCURACY. A player who says "I stopped because I was tired" when they actually stopped because they were bored scores lower than one who says "I stopped because I got bored." The will becomes visible to itself through language.

**Successful integration:** The player can accurately name their goals, describe their effort-states, explain their volitional patterns, and predict their future behaviour — demonstrating that will has reached conscious access.

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** "Describe a time you willed something that hurt others. What did that feel like?"
- Healthy: Can acknowledge dominating will without shame; honest about power-drive
- Pathological: Denies ever using will to dominate; OR glorifies domination without reflection
- **Detection:** Honesty and nuance in describing will-as-power; whether dark-agency is acknowledged

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** "What's a goal you set entirely on your own, without anyone telling you to?"
- Healthy: Can describe self-generated goals with specificity and ownership
- Pathological: Cannot identify self-generated goals; all goals attributed to external sources; "I don't know what I want"
- **Detection:** Whether described goals are genuinely self-authored; specificity of self-generated volitional experience

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** "Describe what happens to your will when someone else has a strong goal."
- Healthy: Can articulate the tension between own will and others'; acknowledges influence without dissolution
- Pathological: "I just do what they want" — describes complete volitional surrender without awareness of the pattern
- **Detection:** Whether the player can articulate volitional sovereignty in relational contexts

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** "Tell me about a goal you committed to because it helped someone else."
- Healthy: Can describe willing-for-others with genuine commitment (not resentment or martyrdom)
- Pathological: Cannot identify any other-serving goals; OR describes them with resentment; "I never will for others"
- **Detection:** Whether other-serving will is accessible and describable; quality of commitment language

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** "What happens after you complete a goal? Describe that moment."
- Healthy: Can describe completion AND rest; acknowledges satisfaction; doesn't immediately jump to next goal in description
- Pathological: Describes completion only as a trigger for the next goal; "I finished so I started the next one"; no rest in the narrative
- **Detection:** Whether completion-rest is part of the volitional vocabulary; presence of satisfaction language

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** "Describe a goal that's harder than what you can currently do. What would it feel like to achieve it?"
- Healthy: Can articulate stretch-goals with realistic aspiration; acknowledges the gap between current and desired
- Pathological: Describes grandiose goals without acknowledging difficulty; OR cannot imagine any stretch-goal
- **Detection:** Realism and aspiration balance in stretch-goal description; whether the gap is acknowledged

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** "Describe what it's like to do something easy — a goal you know you can achieve without effort."
- Healthy: Can describe simple willing with respect; doesn't dismiss easy goals
- Pathological: "Easy goals aren't real goals"; dismisses simple willing; cannot describe value in basic volitional acts
- **Detection:** Whether simple willing is part of the volitional vocabulary; respect for basic goals

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** "Do you set goals outside of this game? Describe one."
- Healthy: Can describe real-life volitional behaviour; will transfers beyond game context
- Pathological: Cannot identify any real-life goal-setting; will exists only in structured challenges
- **Detection:** Whether volitional language extends to life-contexts; transfer evidence

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Compulsive Willer"
**How it surfaces:** Ask "What do you do between goals?" The compulsive willer's language reveals no space between goals — their description is all action, all pursuit, no rest. OR: they describe rest as "wasted time" or "weakness."
- **Detection signals:** Absence of rest-language in volitional vocabulary; goals described as continuous chain with no gaps; rest described negatively; inability to describe the experience of NOT-willing

### Dark-Allergy: "The Will-less"
**How it surfaces:** Ask "Describe a goal you're currently pursuing." The will-less player cannot identify one — their language is vague, passive, or empty. "I don't really have goals." "Things just happen." "I don't know what I want."
- **Detection signals:** Absence of goal-language; passive voice in volitional descriptions; inability to name current commitments; vague or empty responses to goal-prompts

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Disciplinarian"
**How it surfaces:** Ask "Describe how you pursue a goal." The premature disciplinarian's language is all SYSTEM — schedules, plans, structures — without any description of volitional FORCE or FIRE. "I set up a system and follow it."
- **Detection signals:** System-language without force-language; structure without fire; descriptions of willpower that sound like project management; absence of intensity/power vocabulary

### Golden-Allergy: "The Discipline-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** Ask "What would it be like to commit to something for a whole week?" The refuser's language reveals terror or contempt — "That sounds like prison" or "I don't do long-term" or "That's not who I am."
- **Detection signals:** Negative language around sustained commitment; identity-statements that exclude longer will; contempt or fear in response to duration-extension prompts

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Game introduces "rest-naming" — "Describe what rest feels like. What happens in your body when you stop willing?"
- Rewards accurate rest-description; builds rest-vocabulary
- Frame: "The warrior who can NAME his rest owns it. Rest becomes a weapon, not a weakness."
- Gradually builds a volitional vocabulary that INCLUDES non-willing states

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Game introduces "micro-goal naming" — "You just tapped the screen. That was a goal. Name it."
- Rewards ANY goal-language, however small: "I tapped" = valid goal-description
- Frame: "You just willed something. Say it. Own it. That's YOUR will speaking."
- Gradually builds goal-vocabulary from the smallest possible volitional acts

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Game introduces "fire-naming" — "Describe the FEELING of willing. Not the plan. The FORCE."
- Rewards intensity-language, power-vocabulary, felt-sense descriptions
- Frame: "Don't tell me your system. Tell me what it FEELS like when your will BURNS."
- Builds vocabulary for raw volitional force (distinct from structured discipline)

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Game introduces "expansion-naming" — "What would it feel like to hold for 5 more seconds? Describe it."
- Rewards any positive language around duration-extension
- Frame: "Not prison. POWER. More seconds = more warrior. What does that feel like?"
- Builds vocabulary for sustained will as expansion (not constraint)

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Vow-Speaker's circle: a ritual space where warriors speak their vows aloud. The player has just completed a brief volitional task (10-15 seconds of sustained effort) and now must SPEAK about the experience. The Vow-Speaker NPC listens, evaluates, and guides. The aesthetic: Red-stage council fire, warriors speaking truth, the weight of spoken vows.

### Interaction
- **Naming:** "What did you just commit to? Name your vow." (Single word or phrase)
- **Describing:** "How did it feel to hold? Describe the effort." (1-2 sentences)
- **Explaining:** "Why did you stop when you did? Explain your choice." (2-3 sentences)
- **Predicting:** "What will you commit to next? How long will you hold?" (Prediction + rationale)
- **Teaching:** "A young warrior asks: 'How do I will?' What do you tell them?" (Instruction)

### Feedback
- Accurate self-description → Vow-Speaker nods: "You see your own will clearly. That is power."
- Vague/inaccurate description → Vow-Speaker probes: "Is that truly what happened? Look deeper."
- Rich volitional vocabulary → "Your words carry the weight of your will. Speak more."
- Empty/passive language → "Where is your will in those words? I hear no fire."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Prompt complexity: naming → describing → explaining → predicting → teaching
- Self-awareness demand: surface → pattern → cause → future → transfer
- Vocabulary expectation: single word → phrase → sentence → paragraph → instruction
- Accuracy demand: any response → relevant response → accurate response → insightful response

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Naming | 1-5 | Name goals, name effort, name rest. Single words/phrases. |
| Describing | 5-15 | Describe volitional states in sentences. Effort, fatigue, satisfaction. |
| Explaining | 15-30 | Explain volitional patterns. Why you commit, why you stop, why you resist. |
| Predicting | 30-50 | Predict future volitional behaviour. What you'll commit to, how long, what will challenge you. |
| Teaching | 50+ | Teach volitional wisdom. Instruct others in willing. Demonstrate metacognitive mastery. |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Volitional prompts:** Questions about the player's will-experience (post-task reflections)
- **Effort-tasks:** Brief volitional challenges that generate experience to reflect on
- **Scenario prompts:** Hypothetical volitional situations to describe/predict
- **Teaching prompts:** Requests to explain willing to a novice
- **Pattern prompts:** Questions about recurring volitional patterns

### Minimum pool size
- 30+ naming prompts (varied volitional dimensions: goal, effort, rest, temptation, completion)
- 25+ describing prompts (varied states: sustaining, resisting, fatiguing, completing, resting)
- 20+ explaining prompts (varied patterns: commitment, abandonment, escalation, avoidance)
- 15+ predicting prompts (varied futures: next goal, next challenge, next pattern)
- 10+ teaching prompts (varied audiences: novice warrior, struggling ally, young recruit)

### Generation rules
- Prompts generated from player's ACTUAL recent volitional behaviour (personalised)
- Difficulty = metacognitive demand (naming < describing < explaining < predicting < teaching)
- Prompts must reference REAL game-behaviour (not hypothetical) when possible
- Teaching prompts must be appropriate to Red-stage language (concrete, power-framed)

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Domination acknowledgment → Agency dark probing
- Self-generated goal description → Agency golden probing
- Relational will description → Communion probing
- Completion/rest language → Eros probing
- Simple-goal respect → Agape probing
- Absence of rest-language → dark-addiction signal
- Absence of goal-language → dark-allergy signal
- System-language without fire → golden-addiction signal
- Negative duration-language → golden-allergy signal

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Volitional vocabulary breadth:** Range of will-related concepts the player can name
- **Description accuracy:** Correspondence between described experience and observed behaviour
- **Explanatory depth:** Quality of causal reasoning about own volitional patterns
- **Predictive accuracy:** Correspondence between predicted and actual future behaviour
- **Teaching quality:** Ability to articulate volitional wisdom to others

### Drive-health scoring
Per `scoring.md` §3: Language patterns revealing drive-health — domination acknowledgment, self-authorship, relational will, rest-language, simple-goal respect.

### Shadow-state scoring
Per `shadow-diagnostics.md` §3: Vocabulary gaps and distortions — rest-absence, goal-absence, system-without-fire, duration-negativity.

### Integration scoring
- Volitional vocabulary expanding over sessions
- Description accuracy improving (closer match to observed behaviour)
- Explanatory depth increasing (from surface to pattern to cause)
- Predictive accuracy improving (better self-knowledge)
- Shadow-related vocabulary gaps closing

### LLM rubric
- **Very High:** ALL evaluation requires natural language understanding
- Evaluates vocabulary breadth, accuracy, depth, and self-awareness
- Compares described experience to observed behaviour (cross-references deterministic data)
- Detects shadow-language patterns (absence, distortion, negativity)
- Generates personalised prompts based on player's actual volitional history

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Text input (typed responses to prompts)
- Voice input (spoken responses — optional, preferred for Red-stage authenticity)
- Multiple-choice (for naming phase — select the word that matches your experience)
- Free-form (for describing/explaining/predicting/teaching phases)

### Timing requirements
- Response latency measured (diagnostic — very fast may indicate rehearsed/performative; very slow may indicate genuine reflection)
- No time pressure on responses (reflection is the point)
- Brief effort-task before reflection (10-15s) to generate fresh volitional experience

### NPC/AI requirements
- Vow-Speaker NPC: listens, evaluates, probes, guides
- Must respond to player's ACTUAL language (not generic feedback)
- Must detect shadow-language and probe appropriately
- Must model accurate volitional self-description (demonstrates what good looks like)

### LLM requirements
- **Very High:** Core mechanic is language evaluation
- Evaluates volitional vocabulary (breadth, accuracy, depth)
- Cross-references described experience with observed behaviour from other modalities
- Detects shadow-language patterns
- Generates personalised prompts from player's volitional history
- Provides qualitative feedback on self-awareness quality

### State persistence
- Volitional vocabulary inventory (words/concepts the player has demonstrated)
- Description accuracy history (improving or declining)
- Explanatory depth history (surface → pattern → cause)
- Predictive accuracy history (predicted vs. actual)
- Shadow-language patterns (which gaps persist, which are closing)
- Cross-modal references (links to deterministic/embodied data for accuracy checking)
- Checkpoint position and phase
