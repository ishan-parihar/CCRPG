# Interpersonal / Red — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** The strategic-planning axis probes interpersonal intelligence through MULTI-STEP RELATIONAL PLANNING — sequencing interpersonal actions across time, managing multiple relationships simultaneously, and anticipating others' responses to planned moves.
>
> **Why this axis for Interpersonal/Red:** At Red, interpersonal planning is 2-step and TRANSACTIONAL: "First I'll ally with X to defeat Y, THEN I'll take X's share." The player can plan 2 relational moves ahead but cannot yet manage complex alliance networks. This axis isolates the PLANNING component of interpersonal intelligence.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The War-Planner"
- **Core mechanic:** The player plans interpersonal sequences — which alliances to form when, how to position relationships for future advantage, and how to manage relational resources across a multi-step campaign.
- **Duration:** 4-7 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** 2-Move Plans → 3-Move Plans → Alliance Portfolios → Adaptive Plans → Contested Alliances

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "You need X's help to defeat Y. But X wants something from Z. And Z is your enemy. Plan your moves." The contact boundary is: "Can you THINK AHEAD in relational space?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* Do they plan at all (or just react to each relationship)? Do they over-plan (golden-addiction)? Refuse to plan because "others are unpredictable" (dark-allergy)? Plan only domination sequences (dark-addiction)?
- *Emergent:* Can they hold one more relational move in mind? Can they anticipate others' counter-moves?

**Integration path:** Rewards EXECUTED relational plans (not just conceived). A simple 2-move alliance plan that works scores higher than a complex 5-move plan that collapses.

**Successful integration:** The player conceives and executes 2-3 step interpersonal plans appropriate to Red capacity.

---

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Plan suggested by an NPC advisor vs. player's own plan.
- Healthy: Evaluates suggestions; uses good ones
- Pathological: Rejects all relational advice; "I plan alone"
- **Detection:** Rejection rate independent of suggestion quality

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Open relational planning — "Design your alliance strategy for this campaign."
- Healthy: Generates relational plans with confidence
- Pathological: Cannot plan relationships without template; "Tell me who to ally with"
- **Detection:** Self-generated relational plan quality

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Joint planning — player and ally must coordinate their relational strategies.
- Healthy: Integrates ally's relational needs into own plan
- Pathological: Abandons own relational strategy to match ally's; "Whatever you think is best"
- **Detection:** Whether joint plans reflect player's relational sovereignty

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Teaching an NPC to plan their own alliances.
- Healthy: Scaffolds the NPC's relational planning
- Pathological: Plans for them; cannot let others develop relational strategy
- **Detection:** Genuine scaffolding vs. imposition

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Mastered 2-move plans vs. advancing to 3-move.
- Healthy: Enjoys clean execution; advances when ready
- Pathological: Demands longer relational sequences immediately; "This is too simple"
- **Detection:** Satisfaction at current plan-complexity

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Plans requiring trust of unproven allies.
- Healthy: Attempts with appropriate caution
- Pathological: Claims complex alliance networks without demonstration
- **Detection:** Gap between planned and executed relational complexity

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Simple 1-move relational "plans" (barely planning).
- Healthy: Executes with full commitment
- Pathological: Dismisses; "That's not real planning"
- **Detection:** Engagement quality on below-level plans

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Relational planning appearing in free-play.
- Healthy: Plans alliances spontaneously in other contexts
- Pathological: Relational planning exists only in structured challenges
- **Detection:** Spontaneous relational planning outside task contexts

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Dominator"
**How it surfaces:** All relational plans are DOMINATION SEQUENCES — "ally with X to crush Y, then dominate X." Every plan ends with the player on top of everyone.
- **Detection signals:** All plans terminate in player-dominance; cannot plan mutual-benefit outcomes; "alliance" in their plans always means "temporary tool"; every plan includes betrayal of allies

### Dark-Allergy: "The Isolate"
**How it surfaces:** Cannot plan relationally because they refuse to MODEL others as agents. "I can't predict what they'll do." Plans are solo-only; others are treated as environmental obstacles, not agents.
- **Detection signals:** Solo-only plans; refuses to include others as agents; "Others are unpredictable"; cannot model what allies or enemies will DO in response to moves

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Diplomat"
**How it surfaces:** Over-complex relational plans for simple situations. Plans a 5-alliance network when a single ally would suffice. Elaborate "win-win" architectures that collapse under execution.
- **Detection signals:** Over-complex plans for simple problems; elaborate alliance networks that fail; cannot choose the simple relational path; plans that sound sophisticated but don't execute

### Golden-Allergy: "The Loyalty-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** Plans that NEVER include sustained alliance. Every plan is a one-shot transaction. Cannot plan a relationship that extends beyond the immediate task.
- **Detection signals:** All plans are single-interaction; cannot plan sustained alliances; "After this, we're done"; no multi-session relational planning

---

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Introduce plans where MUTUAL BENEFIT produces more power than domination
- Frame: "The war-chief who plans for his war-band's strength has a war-band. The one who plans to dominate them has enemies."
- Reward mutual-benefit plans over domination-sequences

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Introduce PREDICTABLE NPCs (their responses are 100% consistent; modelling them is easy)
- Frame: "This one ALWAYS helps when you offer food. Plan around that. It's reliable."
- Start with perfectly predictable agents; build modelling confidence

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Reward MINIMUM-ALLIANCE solutions (fewest relationships needed to achieve the goal)
- Frame: "One ally. One plan. Done. That's efficiency."
- Penalise unnecessary relational complexity

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Introduce MULTI-SESSION plans where sustained alliance produces compounding rewards
- Frame: "Keep them for 3 rounds. Each round, they give more. Loyalty compounds."
- Make sustained relational planning produce escalating returns

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The War-Planner: a campaign map where alliances are MOVES. The player must plan which relationships to form, maintain, or end across a sequence of challenges. Each challenge requires different relational configurations. The aesthetic: Red-stage war-table, alliance tokens, the campaign map of relationships.

### Interaction
- **2-Move Plans:** "Ally with X to defeat Y." Simple sequential relational moves. (1-5)
- **3-Move Plans:** "Ally with X, use X to reach Z, then trade with Z." Longer sequences. (5-15)
- **Alliance Portfolios:** "You can maintain 3 alliances. Which 3? Resources are limited." (15-30)
- **Adaptive Plans:** Mid-campaign, an ally betrays. Revise the relational plan. (30-50)
- **Contested Alliances:** Enemy is also forming alliances. Anticipate and counter their relational strategy. (50+)

### Feedback
- Plan executed → "Your alliances held. Each relationship served its moment in the campaign."
- Plan collapsed → "The alliance broke. What did you miss about their intent?"
- Over-complex → "Three alliances would have done it. You planned seven. Simpler wins wars."
- Domination-only → "You planned to crush them all. But who fights for a chief who betrays everyone?"

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Plan length: 2 → 3 → 4 moves; Alliance slots: unlimited → 3 → 2
- NPC predictability: perfectly predictable → mostly → partially → adversarial
- Adaptation demand: static → changing → contested

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| 2-Move Plans | 1-5 | Simple sequential alliances; predictable NPCs |
| 3-Move Plans | 5-15 | Longer sequences; some unpredictability |
| Alliance Portfolios | 15-30 | Limited slots; must choose which relationships to maintain |
| Adaptive Plans | 30-50 | Betrayals and changes; must revise |
| Contested Alliances | 50+ | Adversarial relational strategy; anticipation required |

---

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Campaign scenarios:** Multi-step situations requiring planned alliances
- **Alliance configurations:** Different NPC combinations with varied strengths/costs
- **Betrayal triggers:** Mid-plan changes requiring relational revision
- **Enemy strategies:** Adversarial alliance patterns to counter

### Minimum pool size
- 30+ 2-move, 25+ 3-move, 20+ portfolio, 15+ adaptive, 10+ contested

### Drive/shadow mapping
- All-domination plans → dark-addiction; solo-only plans → dark-allergy
- Over-complex plans → golden-addiction; single-interaction-only → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Plan completion:** % of relational plans successfully executed
- **Agent modelling:** Accuracy of predictions about others' responses
- **Portfolio management:** Quality of alliance selection and maintenance
- **Adaptation speed:** How quickly relational plans revise after disruption

### LLM rubric
- **Medium:** Scenario generation, plan evaluation, NPC response generation. Core plan-execution scoring algorithmic.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Drag-and-drop (arrange alliance tokens on campaign map); tap-to-select (choose allies); sequence input (order relational moves)

### Timing requirements
- No millisecond precision; planning phase is untimed; execution phase has generous windows

### NPC/AI requirements
- NPCs with defined response patterns (predictable at early levels, less so at advanced)
- Enemy AI that forms counter-alliances at advanced levels
- NPCs that remember past interactions and adjust behaviour

### LLM requirements
- **Medium:** Scenario generation, NPC response narration, plan evaluation. Core scoring algorithmic.

### State persistence
- Plan completion history; agent-modelling accuracy; portfolio management patterns; adaptation speed; domination/isolation/over-complexity/commitment-avoidance patterns; drive/shadow signals; fatigue state; checkpoint position
