# Interpersonal / Red — Scenario-Choice Game Concept

> **Axis:** The scenario-choice axis probes interpersonal intelligence through RELATIONAL DILEMMAS — situations where the player must choose between competing interpersonal options, each with different costs and benefits to self and others. At Red, these are TRANSACTIONAL dilemmas: betray or stay loyal? Dominate or coordinate? Trust or defend?
>
> **Why this axis for Interpersonal/Red:** At Red, interpersonal dilemmas are about POWER and TRUST in their rawest form. "Do I betray my ally for a bigger reward? Do I trust this stranger? Do I share my resources or hoard them?" The scenario-choice axis tests interpersonal WISDOM — not just reading ability but DECISION quality in relational contexts.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The War-Chief's Dilemma"
- **Core mechanic:** The player faces interpersonal dilemmas — situations with multiple relational options, each revealing different drive-health and shadow patterns. No option is "correct" — the PATTERN of choices across many dilemmas reveals the player's interpersonal structure.
- **Duration:** 3-6 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** Binary Trust → Weighted Alliances → Competing Loyalties → Betrayal Dilemmas → The War-Chief's Judgement

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "Your ally offers help — but at what cost? A stranger approaches — friend or foe? Two allies need you — who do you choose?" The contact boundary is: "How do you DECIDE in relational contexts?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* The player's relational decision-making reveals their interpersonal structure. Do they always dominate? Always isolate? Always perform sophistication? Always refuse commitment?
- *Emergent:* Can they make contextually appropriate relational decisions? Can they hold competing relational needs?

**Integration path:** Rewards CONTEXTUAL WISDOM — decisions that match the situation rather than rigid patterns. A player who ALWAYS betrays or ALWAYS trusts is showing shadow, not capacity.

**Successful integration:** The player makes contextually appropriate interpersonal decisions — trusting when trust is warranted, defending when defence is warranted, committing when commitment serves, and exiting when exit is wise.

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Dilemma where domination is ONE option among several.
- Healthy: Chooses domination only when contextually appropriate
- Pathological: ALWAYS chooses the dominance option regardless of context
- **Detection:** Domination-choice rate across varied contexts (>80% = dark-addiction)

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Dilemma requiring the player to ACT FIRST (initiate a relational move without knowing the outcome).
- Healthy: Initiates with appropriate confidence
- Pathological: Cannot choose without certainty; defers all relational initiative
- **Detection:** Initiative rate in ambiguous relational contexts

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Dilemma where self-sacrifice is ONE option.
- Healthy: Sacrifices only when the alliance genuinely benefits
- Pathological: ALWAYS sacrifices; cannot choose self-interest even when appropriate
- **Detection:** Self-sacrifice rate across contexts (>80% = communion dark)

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Dilemma requiring sharing information/resources with an ally.
- Healthy: Shares when mutual benefit is clear
- Pathological: Cannot share; hoards all advantage even when sharing would produce more
- **Detection:** Sharing rate in clear mutual-benefit contexts

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Dilemma with a "safe" option and a "risky but higher-reward" relational option.
- Healthy: Chooses risk level appropriate to demonstrated capacity
- Pathological: Always chooses maximum relational risk; "Go big or go home"
- **Detection:** Risk-selection pattern relative to demonstrated capacity

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Dilemma requiring trust of a NEW ally (not a proven one).
- Healthy: Extends measured trust with appropriate caution
- Pathological: Claims to trust without basis; "I trust everyone" (performed, not genuine)
- **Detection:** Trust-extension quality in novel relational contexts

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Simple relational dilemma (obvious best choice).
- Healthy: Engages fully; chooses well
- Pathological: Dismisses; "This is too easy"; makes careless choices
- **Detection:** Engagement quality on simple relational dilemmas

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Whether relational decision-making appears in non-dilemma contexts.
- Healthy: Makes interpersonal decisions spontaneously in free-play
- Pathological: Relational decision-making exists only in structured dilemmas
- **Detection:** Spontaneous relational decision quality

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Dominator"
**How it surfaces:** Across 10 varied dilemmas, the Dominator ALWAYS chooses the option that maximises their power over others — regardless of whether it's contextually wise.
- **Detection signals:** Domination-choice >80% across varied contexts; cannot choose cooperation even when it produces more power; "I'd rather have less and be in charge"

### Dark-Allergy: "The Isolate"
**How it surfaces:** Across 10 varied dilemmas, the Isolate ALWAYS chooses the option that minimises relational engagement — exits, refuses, withdraws.
- **Detection signals:** Isolation-choice >80%; cannot choose engagement even when isolation is clearly worse; "I'd rather lose alone than win with someone"

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Diplomat"
**How it surfaces:** The Premature Diplomat chooses the "most sophisticated" option every time — the one that SOUNDS like advanced interpersonal reasoning — regardless of whether it fits the situation.
- **Detection signals:** Always-sophisticated choices; cannot choose the simple/direct option even when it's clearly best; "win-win" language applied to zero-sum situations

### Golden-Allergy: "The Loyalty-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** Across dilemmas involving commitment, the Loyalty-Refuser ALWAYS chooses the option that preserves maximum exit-freedom — even when commitment would produce clearly superior outcomes.
- **Detection signals:** Commitment-avoidance >80%; cannot choose loyalty even when betrayal-risk is zero; "I don't want to be tied down"

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Present dilemmas where cooperation produces 3× the power of domination
- Frame: "The war-chief who coordinates gets a war-band. The one who dominates gets slaves who flee at the first chance."
- Make the POWER argument for cooperation (Red-resonant)

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Present dilemmas where isolation produces ZERO reward and minimal engagement produces SOME
- Frame: "You lose nothing by looking. Just look. What do they offer?"
- Start with zero-risk engagement options; celebrate any relational choice

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Present dilemmas where the SIMPLE option is clearly best and the sophisticated option fails
- Frame: "Sometimes the answer is just 'yes' or 'no.' Not everything needs a speech."
- Reward directness; penalise unnecessary complexity

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Present dilemmas where commitment produces UNIQUE rewards unavailable to the uncommitted
- Frame: "The war-band that stays together gets the fortress. The lone wolf gets the scraps."
- Make commitment produce measurably superior outcomes over time

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The War-Chief's Dilemma: the player sits in judgement. Situations arise requiring interpersonal decisions. Allies petition, strangers arrive, enemies offer truces. Each decision reveals the player's relational structure. The aesthetic: Red-stage throne room, petitioners approaching, the weight of relational power.

### Interaction
- **Binary Trust:** Simple trust/distrust decisions with clear stakes. (1-5)
- **Weighted Alliances:** Choose between allies with different strengths/costs. (5-15)
- **Competing Loyalties:** Two allies need you; you can only help one. (15-30)
- **Betrayal Dilemmas:** Betray for large gain or stay loyal for smaller but reliable gain. (30-50)
- **The War-Chief's Judgement:** Complex multi-party relational decisions with cascading consequences. (50+)

### Feedback
- Contextually wise choice → "The war-chief sees clearly. Your choice serves the moment."
- Rigid pattern detected → "You chose the same way again. Is this wisdom, or habit?"
- Shadow-revealing choice → (no explicit feedback — the game adapts difficulty/content)
- Integrated choice → "You weighed all paths and chose true. This is the war-chief's art."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Options: 2 → 3 → 4; Stakes: low → moderate → high
- Ambiguity: clear best-option → no clear best → all options have significant costs
- Consequences: immediate → delayed → cascading across future dilemmas
- Relational complexity: 1 other → 2 others → 3+ others affected

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Binary Trust | 1-5 | 2 options, clear stakes, 1 other affected |
| Weighted Alliances | 5-15 | 3 options, moderate stakes, trade-offs |
| Competing Loyalties | 15-30 | 2+ allies, cannot satisfy all |
| Betrayal Dilemmas | 30-50 | High-stakes trust/betray with consequences |
| The War-Chief's Judgement | 50+ | Multi-party, cascading, ambiguous |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Trust dilemmas:** Trust/distrust decisions with varied risk profiles
- **Alliance dilemmas:** Choose between competing alliance options
- **Loyalty dilemmas:** Commitment vs. freedom trade-offs
- **Betrayal dilemmas:** Short-term gain vs. long-term relational cost
- **Multi-party dilemmas:** Complex decisions affecting multiple relationships

### Minimum pool size
- 25+ trust, 20+ alliance, 20+ loyalty, 15+ betrayal, 10+ multi-party

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Always-dominate → dark-addiction; always-isolate → dark-allergy
- Always-sophisticated → golden-addiction; always-uncommitted → golden-allergy
- Choice patterns across 10+ dilemmas reveal shadow structure

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Contextual appropriateness:** Do choices match the situation (not rigid patterns)?
- **Consequence awareness:** Do choices account for downstream effects?
- **Relational complexity handling:** Can they manage multi-party decisions?
- **Pattern flexibility:** Variety in choice-patterns across different contexts

### LLM rubric
- **High:** Scenario generation, consequence narration, pattern detection across choices. LLM evaluates contextual appropriateness of choices and detects rigid patterns.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Tap-to-select (choose between options); optional free-text justification for advanced phases

### Timing requirements
- Decision time tracked (very fast = possibly impulsive; very slow = possibly paralysed)
- No time pressure imposed; natural decision speed is diagnostic

### NPC/AI requirements
- NPCs with consistent personalities across dilemmas (player can learn their patterns)
- Consequences must be VISIBLE (player sees what happened because of their choice)
- Multi-session NPCs who remember past decisions

### LLM requirements
- **High:** Scenario generation, consequence narration, pattern analysis across sessions. Detects rigid choice-patterns and shadow signals.

### State persistence
- Choice history across all dilemmas; pattern analysis (domination rate, isolation rate, sophistication rate, commitment-avoidance rate); consequence history; NPC relationship states; drive/shadow signals; fatigue state; checkpoint position
