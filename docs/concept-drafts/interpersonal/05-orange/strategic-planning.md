# Interpersonal / Orange — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** Planning relational life — structuring relationships across time, managing social commitments.  **Why this axis for this module:** Orange contractual coordination requires temporal architecture — the capacity to plan, schedule, prioritise, and sustain multiple relational commitments without collapse or exploitation.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Title:** Relational Architect  
**Core loop:** Player manages a network of relationships across simulated weeks. Each relationship has needs, contracts, and decay rates. Player allocates time, energy, and attention — planning who to invest in, when to renegotiate, and how to balance competing relational demands.  
**Infinite checkpoint:** Each simulated week is a planning cycle (4–6 minutes). State saves after each week. Long-term consequences unfold across 8+ weeks.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Shadow provocation by design:**
- DA surfaces when player allocates relational time purely by ROI — investing only in high-value connections, neglecting relationships without strategic payoff
- DAll surfaces when player under-allocates relational time globally — leaving most relationships in decay, planning solo activities over relational ones
- GA surfaces when player over-invests in depth with select relationships without establishing sustainable contracts — burnout through premature intimacy
- GAll surfaces when player maintains all relationships at identical professional distance — no differentiation, no deepening, pure maintenance

**Drive probing:** Agency in deliberate prioritisation. Communion in balanced investment across the network. Eros in planning for relational growth/deepening. Agape in maintaining existing commitments with care.

## 3. Game Design

**Mechanics:** Network map shows 6–12 relationships with visible health bars, contract status, and need indicators. Each week: player allocates 10 time-units across relationships. Relationships have different maintenance costs, growth potential, and decay rates. Contracts can be renegotiated (costs 2 time-units). New relationships can be initiated (costs 3 time-units).  
**Difficulty staircase:** Early weeks: 6 relationships, clear needs, generous time budget. Mid: 9 relationships, conflicting schedules, reduced time. Late: 12 relationships, unexpected crises, contract violations by NPCs requiring response, time scarcity.  
**Feedback:** Network health dashboard — overall relational vitality, individual relationship trajectories, contract compliance rates. No explicit shadow labelling.  
**Progression:** Network grows. Relationships develop histories. Planning decisions compound — early neglect creates later crises. Early over-investment creates dependency.

## 4. Item Pool

- 12 relationship archetypes (mentor, peer, subordinate, friend, intimate, collaborator, neighbour, family, acquaintance, rival-turned-ally, high-maintenance, low-maintenance)
- 20 event modifiers per simulated week (crisis, opportunity, betrayal, gift, request, boundary-test)
- 8 contract templates (maintenance, growth, renegotiation, dissolution, initiation)
- 6 network configurations of increasing complexity
- Adaptive events: system generates events targeting detected shadow patterns

## 5. Technical Requirements

- Network simulation engine: relationship health, decay rates, contract states, need cycles
- Time-allocation interface: drag-and-drop or tap-to-assign time-units
- Consequence propagation: decisions compound across 8+ week windows
- Shadow-signal extraction: allocation patterns analysed for drive×shadow signatures
- No LLM required — deterministic simulation with pre-authored events
- Dashboard visualisation: network graph with health indicators and trend lines
