# Interpersonal / Orange — Immersive-RPG Game Concept

> **Axis:** Ecological relating — whether contractual coordination appears spontaneously in free-play.  **Why this axis for this module:** The ultimate test of Orange interpersonal capacity is whether it emerges naturally in an open world — not when prompted, but when the player freely navigates a relational ecology without explicit instructions to "make contracts."

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Title:** The Free Market of Souls  
**Core loop:** Open-world RPG segment where the player navigates a social ecology — a trading town, guild hall, or collaborative settlement. NPCs have needs, offers, and relational styles. No explicit instructions to form contracts. The game observes whether the player spontaneously establishes clear expectations, negotiates roles, maintains boundaries, and builds mutual-benefit relationships — or falls into shadow patterns.  
**Infinite checkpoint:** Continuous autosave. Sessions are player-determined (5–20 minutes). World state persists.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Shadow provocation by design:**
- DA surfaces when player treats the social ecology as a resource-extraction field — networking for advantage, accumulating social capital without reciprocity
- DAll surfaces when player avoids NPC interaction, operates solo, or engages only in transactional exchanges (buy/sell) without relational depth
- GA surfaces when player seeks "deep connection" with NPCs without establishing practical coordination — wants intimacy without structure
- GAll surfaces when player forms technically perfect agreements but never allows relationships to evolve beyond initial terms

**Drive probing:** Ecological — all four drives observed in naturalistic behaviour. No forced probes. The open world IS the probe. Agency in self-directed relational choices. Communion in spontaneous collaboration. Eros in relational deepening. Agape in care for NPC wellbeing beyond utility.

## 3. Game Design

**Mechanics:** Social sandbox with 15–20 NPCs, each with: personality, needs, offers, relational history, and trust level. Player can: approach, converse, propose collaboration, set terms, renegotiate, withdraw, deepen, or ignore. NPCs respond dynamically based on player behaviour patterns. No quest markers for relational content — player must initiate.  
**Difficulty staircase:** Early: NPCs are receptive and clear about needs. Mid: NPCs have hidden agendas, conflicting loyalties, or ambiguous signals. Late: social crises require the player to coordinate multiple NPCs, mediate conflicts, or restructure relational networks.  
**Feedback:** World responds organically. Good contracts lead to thriving collaborations. Exploitation leads to reputation damage. Isolation leads to missed opportunities. No scores — only narrative consequences.  
**Progression:** Town/settlement evolves based on player's relational architecture. Thriving networks unlock new content. Collapsed relationships create obstacles. The ecology IS the feedback.

## 4. Item Pool

- 20 NPC personalities with full relational models (needs, boundaries, trust thresholds, growth potential)
- 30 emergent scenarios triggered by relational states (not scripted sequences)
- 12 social crises requiring multi-party coordination
- 8 long-term relationship arcs (acquaintance → partner → deep collaborator)
- Environmental storytelling: NPC behaviour changes reflect player's relational impact
- Adaptive NPC behaviour: NPCs mirror, test, or challenge player's relational patterns

## 5. Technical Requirements

- Social simulation engine: NPC needs, trust, reputation, relational memory
- Emergent event system: scenarios triggered by relational state thresholds, not timers
- Behavioural observation layer: tracks player-initiated interactions, approach patterns, contract formation, boundary maintenance — all without explicit prompting
- Shadow-signal extraction: naturalistic behaviour patterns mapped to drive×shadow over 3+ sessions
- NPC AI: personality-driven responses with relational memory and trust dynamics
- No LLM required for core — pre-authored NPC dialogue with state-driven selection
