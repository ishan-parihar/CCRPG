# Interpersonal / Orange — Embodied-Somatic Game Concept

> **Axis:** Body as site of relating — physical proximity, interpersonal space, embodied boundaries.  **Why this axis for this module:** Contractual coordination has a somatic signature — the body that can hold its ground while remaining open to another. Boundary precision is felt before it is spoken.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Title:** Boundary Field  
**Core loop:** Player manages an avatar's interpersonal space in real-time spatial encounters. Approaching, retreating, holding ground, and adjusting proximity to multiple NPCs simultaneously. The body's spatial behaviour reveals contractual capacity — can the player maintain clear boundaries while remaining relationally available?  
**Infinite checkpoint:** Each encounter is 2–4 minutes. Spatial state saves between encounters.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Shadow provocation by design:**
- DA surfaces as predatory approach patterns — moving into others' space to extract, positioning for advantage, spatial dominance without reciprocity
- DAll surfaces as excessive distance maintenance — never entering relational proximity, retreating from approach, spatial isolation
- GA surfaces as premature intimacy — collapsing interpersonal distance before establishing spatial contracts, rushing into closeness
- GAll surfaces as rigid spatial boundaries — maintaining exact distance regardless of context, refusing to modulate proximity even when appropriate

**Drive probing:** Agency in the capacity to hold spatial ground under pressure. Communion in willingness to share space. Eros in movement toward appropriate closeness. Agape in returning to clear spatial boundaries after connection.

## 3. Game Design

**Mechanics:** Top-down spatial field. Player avatar has a visible "boundary zone" (adjustable radius). NPCs approach with varying intentions (collaborative, exploitative, avoidant, intimate). Player must: (1) adjust boundary radius per-NPC, (2) approach/retreat appropriately, (3) maintain multiple simultaneous spatial contracts, (4) renegotiate distance when context shifts.  
**Difficulty staircase:** Early: single NPC, clear intention. Mid: multiple NPCs with conflicting spatial needs. Late: NPCs who violate boundaries, shift intentions mid-encounter, or pressure for inappropriate proximity/distance.  
**Feedback:** Spatial harmony score — how well the player's boundary field matched the relational context. Dissonance indicators when boundaries are too rigid or too porous.  
**Progression:** Encounter complexity increases. NPC behaviour becomes more ambiguous. Multi-party spatial negotiations require simultaneous boundary management.

## 4. Item Pool

- 36 encounter types across 4 complexity tiers
- 8 NPC archetypes with distinct spatial signatures (encroacher, retreater, oscillator, matcher, tester, respecter, ambiguous, shifting)
- 12 context modifiers (power differential, prior trust, urgency, cultural frame)
- 6 multi-party configurations (3–5 NPCs with competing spatial needs)
- Adaptive difficulty: NPC behaviour complexity scales with demonstrated spatial capacity

## 5. Technical Requirements

- Real-time spatial engine: avatar position, boundary radius, NPC positions, proximity calculations
- Boundary-zone rendering: visual feedback of player's adjustable interpersonal field
- NPC AI: approach/retreat behaviours driven by archetype + context + player response
- Spatial pattern analysis: approach velocity, distance maintenance variance, boundary adjustment frequency
- Shadow-signal extraction: spatial behaviour patterns mapped to drive×shadow coordinates
- Touch/gesture input support for boundary radius adjustment on mobile
