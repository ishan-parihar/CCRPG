# Interpersonal / Amber — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** Planning group coordination — how the player structures shared obligations, assigns roles, and manages the relational demands of group membership across time.
> **Why this axis for this module:** Role-based coordination at Amber requires planning WITHIN the code — knowing who does what, when, and in what order. This modality reveals whether the player can organise group obligations effectively or whether they use planning to bypass genuine belonging (golden-addiction) or cannot plan at all (dark-allergy).

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Guild's Campaign"
- **Core mechanic:** The player plans and executes a multi-step group campaign. Each step requires role-appropriate coordination; the campaign is a sustained group commitment. Planning must serve the group's code, not replace it. The game tests whether the player can structure group obligations effectively while maintaining genuine belonging.
- **Duration:** 5-12 minutes per session
- **Internal progression:** The Group Brief → The Role-Map → The Coordinated Execution → The Unexpected Conflict → The Campaign Complete

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**What this modality uniquely presents:** The PLANNING dimension of group-belonging — how the player structures relational obligations across time. Unlike deterministic (raw coordination accuracy), this modality measures whether the player can ORGANISE group obligations effectively without using planning as a substitute for genuine belonging.

**How this differs from other modalities:** Unlike scenario-choice (single-decision quality), this modality measures multi-step group coordination planning. Unlike embodied-somatic (physical synchrony), this modality measures cognitive structuring of group obligations.

**What it uniquely surfaces:** Golden-addiction (elaborate relational planning without underlying belonging), dark-allergy (cannot plan group coordination; impulsive), and the quality of the player's relationship to group obligation-structure.

**Successful integration:** The player plans group coordination effectively within the code — assigning roles clearly, sequencing obligations, adapting when conflicts arise — without using planning to bypass genuine belonging.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Guild's Campaign: a mission-planning interface where the player receives a group campaign brief and must plan and execute it with their guild. The campaign has multiple steps, each requiring role-appropriate coordination. Aesthetic: Amber-stage guild — mission boards, role-assignments, the systematic structure of collective action.

### Interaction
- **The Group Brief (1-5):** Simple 3-step campaign. "The guild requires: scout the road, secure the pass, escort the merchant." Tests basic role-assignment and sequencing.
- **The Role-Map (5-15):** Player assigns roles and plans the sequence. "Who does what? In what order? What does each role require?" Tests planning-within-the-code.
- **The Coordinated Execution (15-30):** Execute the planned campaign with group members. Tests whether the plan holds under execution pressure.
- **The Unexpected Conflict (30-50):** Mid-campaign interpersonal conflict. "Two members dispute their roles. Your plan must adapt. But the campaign continues." Tests adaptive group-coordination.
- **The Campaign Complete (50+):** Full integration: role-assignment + sequencing + execution + conflict-adaptation simultaneously.

### Feedback
- Effective role-planning → "The roles are clear. Each member knows their place. The guild is served."
- Over-planning → "The plan is elaborate. But the first coordination is simple. Can you coordinate without the full plan?"
- No planning → "The campaign begins without role-assignments. The code has a structure. Without it, how will the group hold?"
- Plan-as-bypass → "You planned the optimal coordination. But the guild gave you a code, not an optimisation problem. What does the code require?"
- Adaptive coordination → "The conflict arose. The plan changed. The group held. This is mastery."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Campaign length: 3 steps → 5 steps → 8 steps
- Role complexity: 2 roles → 4 roles → 6 roles
- Conflict frequency: none → occasional → frequent
- Code-constraint complexity: simple → competing → multi-constraint

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The Group Brief | 1-5 | Simple 3-step role-assignment |
| The Role-Map | 5-15 | Planning within the code |
| The Coordinated Execution | 15-30 | Plan-execution under pressure |
| The Unexpected Conflict | 30-50 | Adaptive group-coordination |
| The Campaign Complete | 50+ | Full planning-belonging integration |

## 4. Item Pool

- **Campaign briefs:** Multi-step group coordination sequences — 25+
- **Role-assignment tasks:** Role-mapping and sequencing challenges — 20+
- **Execution tasks:** Coordinated plan-execution under varying pressure — 20+
- **Conflict scenarios:** Mid-campaign interpersonal conflicts requiring adaptation — 15+
- **Code-constraint tasks:** Planning within complex code-requirements — 10+

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input types:** Tap (role selection); drag (role sequencing); hold (coordination confirmation); swipe (plan navigation)
- **Timing:** Planning time tracked; execution quality measured; adaptation speed recorded; conflict-resolution approach detected
- **NPC/AI:** Adaptive campaign complexity; conflict-event generation; role-assignment quality assessment; shadow-signal accumulator
- **LLM:** Campaign narrative; conflict-event generation; coordination quality assessment for complex cases
- **State persistence:** Full campaign history; role-assignment patterns; execution quality; conflict-adaptation responses; cross-session campaign continuity; drive/shadow signal accumulation; checkpoint position
