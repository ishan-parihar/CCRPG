# Interpersonal / Amber — Language-Reflective Game Concept

> **Axis:** Verbal articulation of group-belonging — how the player talks about loyalty, the code, role-relationships, and group-membership reveals the structure of their interpersonal life at Amber.
> **Why this axis for this module:** The language of group-belonging is diagnostic. "The code requires" (enforcer), "I relate as I choose" (lone wolf), "I understand all perspectives" (premature diplomat), and "I just follow the code" (belonging-refuser) are structurally distinct and reveal which shadow is active.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Guild's Chronicle"
- **Core mechanic:** The player speaks with an NPC guild-chronicler about their relational life within the group. An LLM-powered chronicler analyses the language of group-belonging, surfacing shadow patterns through what the player says and how they say it.
- **Duration:** 3-8 minutes per session
- **Internal progression:** Naming the Role → Explaining the Loyalty → The Conflict Recalled → The Code Examined → The Chronicle Complete

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**What this modality uniquely presents:** The verbal dimension of group-belonging — how the player conceptualises, justifies, and narrates their relational obligations. Language reveals whether group-membership is experienced as sovereign choice, code-compulsion, performed sophistication, or unexaminable tradition.

**How this differs from other modalities:** Unlike deterministic (raw coordination accuracy), this modality measures the METACOGNITIVE relationship to group-belonging. A player who coordinates well (deterministic) but cannot articulate why (language-reflective) has implicit capacity without metacognition. A player who articulates beautifully but cannot coordinate (deterministic) has golden-addiction.

**What it uniquely surfaces:** Golden-addiction (sophisticated relational language without genuine belonging), belonging-refusal (refuses to examine relational demands verbally), and the quality of the player's relationship to group-membership as a concept.

**Successful integration:** The player can name their role clearly, explain their loyalty, acknowledge relational failures, and engage with questions about the code without anxiety or dismissal.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Guild's Chronicle: a dialogue interface where the player speaks with a guild-chronicler about their relational life. The chronicler asks structured questions; the player responds in free text. The LLM analyses responses for shadow patterns.

### Interaction
- **Naming the Role (1-5):** "What is your role in the group? What does it require of you toward others?" Assesses: can they name a concrete role with relational obligations?
- **Explaining the Loyalty (5-15):** "Why do you remain loyal to this group? What would it take for you to leave?" Assesses: is loyalty genuine or performed? Sovereign or compelled?
- **The Conflict Recalled (15-30):** "Tell me about a conflict within the group. How was it resolved?" Assesses: was the code used? Was power used? Was it avoided?
- **The Code Examined (30-50):** "Does the code ever require something of you toward others that feels wrong? How do you respond?" Assesses: can they examine relational demands?
- **The Chronicle Complete (50+):** Full integration: names role, explains loyalty, recalls conflict, examines code — all with healthy drive expression.

### Feedback
- Genuine belonging-language → "You know your role. You feel the loyalty. The chronicle records truth."
- Compelled belonging-language → "You say the code requires. But you chose this group. Was the choosing a choice? The chronicle asks."
- Performed sophistication → "You speak of understanding all perspectives. But do you feel the loyalty? The chronicle asks for the belonging, not the framework."
- Examination-refusal → "The code requires this of you. Good. The chronicle asks only: do you know why? Not to change it — to understand it."
- Proportional conflict-resolution → "You used the code to resolve the conflict. Not power. Not avoidance. The chronicle notes this."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Prompt depth: concrete → abstract
- Loyalty-examination: surface → deep
- Conflict-examination: minor → major → identity-level
- Code-examination: gentle → challenging → fundamental

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Naming the Role | 1-5 | Role identification and relational obligations |
| Explaining the Loyalty | 5-15 | Loyalty quality and sovereignty |
| The Conflict Recalled | 15-30 | Conflict-resolution approach |
| The Code Examined | 30-50 | Meta-cognitive engagement with relational demands |
| The Chronicle Complete | 50+ | Full verbal group-belonging integration |

## 4. Item Pool

- **Role-naming prompts:** Requests to identify role and relational obligations — 20+
- **Loyalty-examination prompts:** Questions about loyalty quality and sovereignty — 20+
- **Conflict-recall prompts:** Questions about past relational conflicts — 15+
- **Code-examination prompts:** Questions about relational demand worth and basis — 15+
- **Integration prompts:** Multi-dimensional group-belonging reflection — 10+

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input types:** Free text (primary); structured selection (secondary)
- **Timing:** Response latency tracked; reflection depth measured; avoidance patterns detected
- **NPC/AI:** LLM-powered chronicler with rubric-based analysis; shadow-pattern detection; drive-health signal extraction; cross-session narrative tracking
- **LLM rubrics:** Genuine vs. performed belonging-language; loyalty sovereignty; code-use vs. power-use in conflict; examination-refusal patterns; enforcer vs. lone-wolf language signatures
- **State persistence:** Full response history; language pattern tracking; shadow-signal accumulation; cross-session narrative coherence; drive-health language scores; checkpoint position
