# Interpersonal / Amber — Embodied-Somatic Game Concept

> **Axis:** The body as the site of group-belonging — physical synchrony with the group, embodied loyalty, whether belonging is genuinely felt in the body or merely conceptual.
> **Why this axis for this module:** Group-belonging that is genuinely integrated lives in the body. Loyalty has a felt warmth. Group synchrony has a felt rhythm. Role-appropriate posture has a felt weight. This modality reveals whether group-belonging is embodied or performed.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "Moving as One"
- **Core mechanic:** The player synchronises physical movement with group members, holds role-appropriate posture, and responds to group-rhythm cues. The body must enact what the code requires. Group synchrony is measured in milliseconds; role-posture is measured in duration and accuracy.
- **Duration:** 4-8 minutes per session
- **Internal progression:** Group Rhythm → Role Posture → Synchronised Response → The Body's Loyalty → Moving as One

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**What this modality uniquely presents:** The SOMATIC dimension of group-belonging — whether membership is genuinely embodied or merely conceptual. A player who coordinates well cognitively (deterministic) but cannot synchronise physically has a mind-body split in their group-belonging.

**How this differs from other modalities:** Unlike deterministic (cognitive coordination accuracy), this modality measures whether group-belonging is GROUNDED IN THE BODY. Unlike scenario-choice (decision quality), this modality measures the physical texture of group-membership.

**What it uniquely surfaces:** The enforcer's body is rigid and cannot synchronise (too locked in own form). The lone wolf's body is disconnected from group-rhythm. The premature diplomat's body performs group synchrony without genuine felt-sense. The belonging-refuser's body holds known group-rhythms but freezes at novel ones.

**Successful integration:** The player demonstrates embodied group-belonging — synchronised, flexible, role-appropriate — neither compulsive nor absent, genuinely grounded in body-awareness.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
Moving as One: a physical-interface game where the player synchronises movement with group members. The interface shows group-rhythm cues; the player matches them. Role-posture tasks require sustained physical enactment. Aesthetic: Amber-stage ceremony — the march, the ritual, the physical texture of collective belonging.

### Interaction
- **Group Rhythm (1-5):** "Match the group's rhythm. Tap with the beat." Basic synchrony. Tests whether the player can physically join the group.
- **Role Posture (5-15):** "Your role requires this posture. Hold it." Sustained role-appropriate physical enactment. Tests embodied role-membership.
- **Synchronised Response (15-30):** "The group responds to the signal together. Match the response." Tests coordinated physical response to shared cues.
- **The Body's Loyalty (30-50):** Loyalty-maintenance through physical adversity. "The rhythm changes. The group adapts. Can you adapt with them?" Tests embodied loyalty-flexibility.
- **Moving as One (50+):** Full integration: group-rhythm + role-posture + synchronised response + loyalty-flexibility simultaneously.

### Feedback
- Group synchrony achieved → "The body moves with the group. The belonging is felt. The order is one."
- Synchrony failure → "The body moved alone. The group's rhythm was there. Can you feel it and join it?"
- Rigid posture → "The role-posture is held. But the body is stone. Role-membership breathes. Can you hold AND breathe?"
- Lone-body → "The body did not join the group's response. The signal was there. Was this a choice or an absence?"
- Novel rhythm refused → "A new group-rhythm is offered. Your body knows the old one. Can it learn the new?"

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Rhythm complexity: simple beat → variable → polyrhythm
- Posture duration: 10 seconds → 1 minute → 3 minutes
- Synchrony precision: ±200ms → ±100ms → ±50ms
- Group size: 2 members → 4 members → 6 members
- Novel rhythm introduction: familiar → slightly novel → significantly novel

| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
