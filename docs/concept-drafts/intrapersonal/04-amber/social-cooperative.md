# Intrapersonal / Amber — Social-Cooperative Game Concept

> **Axis:** Shared identity — how role-identity functions within the group, identity-through-others, and maintaining self-concept when the group challenges or affirms the role.
> **Why this axis for this module:** Role-identity at Amber is fundamentally GROUP-GIVEN. The group assigns the role; the group affirms the identity; the group can challenge it. This modality reveals whether the player's role-identity is genuinely relational or merely individual.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Order's Mirror"
- **Core mechanic:** The player's role-identity is reflected, affirmed, and challenged by NPC group members. The player must maintain their role-identity when the group affirms it, when the group challenges it, and when the group assigns a new role.
- **Duration:** 5-12 minutes per session
- **Internal progression:** The Group's Affirmation → The Role Reflected → The Identity Challenged → The New Role Offered → The Order's Mirror

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**What this modality uniquely presents:** The RELATIONAL dimension of role-identity — how the self-as-role functions within the group that assigns and affirms it. Unlike deterministic (individual identity-consistency), this modality measures whether the player's role-identity is genuinely embedded in group-relationship.

**How this differs from other modalities:** Unlike scenario-choice (individual decisions), this modality measures group-embedded identity. Unlike embodied-somatic (physical identity), this modality measures the social texture of role-identity.

**What it uniquely surfaces:** The role-prisoner collapses when the group challenges the role. The role-refuser ignores group affirmation. The premature individualist claims identity independent of the group. The identity-clinger cannot accept a new role from the group.

**Successful integration:** The player maintains role-identity when affirmed, holds it when challenged, and can accept new roles from the group without either collapsing or refusing.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Order's Mirror: a group-interface where NPC companions reflect, affirm, and challenge the player's role-identity. Each companion has a distinct style (one affirms constantly, one challenges, one offers new roles). Aesthetic: Amber-stage group — the order's ceremony, role-assignment rituals, the social mirror of identity.

### Interaction
- **The Group's Affirmation (1-5):** "The order recognises you as guardian. How does this feel?" Tests response to group-affirmation of role.
- **The Role Reflected (5-15):** "Your companion says: 'You are a good guardian.' How do you respond?" Tests identity-stability under positive reflection.
- **The Identity Challenged (15-30):** "Your companion says: 'Are you really a guardian? You seem more like a healer.' How do you respond?" Tests identity-stability under challenge.
- **The New Role Offered (30-50):** "The order offers you a new role. Do you accept? What happens to the old identity?" Tests role-transition capacity.
- **The Order's Mirror (50+):** Full integration: affirmation + reflection + challenge + new-role simultaneously.

### Feedback
- Stable identity under affirmation → "The group sees you. You see yourself. The mirror is clear."
- Identity-collapse under challenge → "The challenge shook you. The role wavered. Notice: is the role yours? Or is it only the group's?"
- Identity-rigidity under challenge → "The challenge was refused. The role held. But was this strength or was this fear?"
- New role accepted → "The old role is honoured. The new role is taken. The self grows. This is not betrayal — this is growth."
- New role refused → "The new role was offered. You held the old. Notice: was this loyalty or was this fear of change?"

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Challenge intensity: mild → moderate → severe → identity-level
- Affirmation complexity: simple → nuanced → conditional
- New-role complexity: similar → different → contradictory
- Group pressure: gentle → moderate → strong

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The Group's Affirmation | 1-5 | Response to group role-affirmation |
| The Role Reflected | 5-15 | Identity-stability under positive reflection |
| The Identity Challenged | 15-30 | Identity-stability under challenge |
| The New Role Offered | 30-50 | Role-transition capacity |
| The Order's Mirror | 50+ | Full social role-identity integration |

## 4. Item Pool

- **Affirmation scenarios:** Group role-affirmation of varying intensity — 20+
- **Reflection scenarios:** Positive identity-reflection from companions — 20+
- **Challenge scenarios:** Identity-challenges from companions — 20+
- **New-role scenarios:** Role-transition offers from the group — 15+
- **Integration scenarios:** Multi-dimensional identity-in-group tasks — 10+

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input types:** Tap (response selection); hold (deliberation); swipe (companion interaction)
- **Timing:** Response latency to challenges tracked; deliberation time measured; identity-stability patterns detected
- **NPC/AI:** Companion identity-style simulation; challenge-event generation; group-dynamic modelling; shadow-signal accumulator
- **LLM:** Companion dialogue; challenge narration; affirmation delivery; role-transition narration
- **State persistence:** Full group identity history; companion interaction patterns; challenge-response quality; role-transition patterns; cross-session identity continuity; drive/shadow signal accumulation; checkpoint position
