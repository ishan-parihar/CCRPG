# Intrapersonal / Red — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** The strategic-planning axis probes self-knowledge through MULTI-STEP SELF-MANAGEMENT — can the player plan around their own states, patterns, and limitations? At Red, this means: "I know I get tired after 3 rounds, so I'll rest at round 2." Planning that requires self-knowledge as input.
>
> **Why this axis for Intrapersonal/Red:** Self-knowledge becomes STRATEGIC when it informs planning. The warrior who knows "I lose focus after 5 minutes" plans short engagements. The warrior who knows "I'm strongest when angry" plans to get angry before the fight. This modality tests whether self-knowledge is APPLIED to multi-step planning.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Self-Strategist"
- **Core mechanic:** The player plans multi-step sequences that account for their own states, patterns, and limitations. Plans must incorporate self-knowledge: "I'll do X first (when I'm fresh), then Y (when I'm tired), then rest (because I know my limit)."
- **Duration:** 4-7 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** Plan Around Fatigue → Plan Around Patterns → Plan Around Strengths → Plan Around Blind Spots → The Self-Strategy

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "You have 5 tasks. You know you're strongest at the start and weakest at the end. How do you ORDER them?" The contact boundary is: "Can you use self-knowledge to PLAN?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* Does self-knowledge inform planning at all? The Narcissist plans as if always at peak. The Unexamined plans without self-data. The Premature Witness over-plans with abstract self-models. The Identity-Clinger plans only within identity.
- *Emergent:* Can they incorporate more self-knowledge into planning? Can they plan around limitations they've just discovered?

**Integration path:** Rewards plans that ACCOUNT FOR SELF accurately. A plan that says "I'll do the hard thing first because I know I fade" and WORKS scores higher than a plan that ignores self-knowledge and fails.

**Successful integration:** The player creates and executes plans that incorporate accurate self-knowledge — scheduling around fatigue, leveraging strengths, avoiding known weaknesses.

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Planning that requires acknowledging limitation.
- Healthy: Plans around limitations; "I'll rest here because I know I fade"
- Pathological: Plans as if limitless; "I'll do everything at maximum intensity"
- **Detection:** Whether plans account for demonstrated limitations

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Self-directed planning (no template provided).
- Healthy: Generates self-knowledge-informed plans independently
- Pathological: Cannot plan without external structure; "Tell me what order"
- **Detection:** Quality of self-generated, self-knowledge-informed plans

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Joint planning where self-knowledge must be shared.
- Healthy: "I'm better at X, you're better at Y — let's split accordingly"
- Pathological: Cannot share self-knowledge for joint planning; plans solo
- **Detection:** Whether self-knowledge is communicated for coordination

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Incorporating ally's knowledge of you into your plan.
- Healthy: "You noticed I fade at round 3 — I'll plan around that"
- Pathological: Rejects ally's observations about your patterns
- **Detection:** Whether external observations improve plan quality

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Simple self-knowledge planning (order 2 tasks by difficulty).
- Healthy: Engages fully; uses self-knowledge on simple plans
- Pathological: Dismisses simple planning; "I don't need to plan for 2 tasks"
- **Detection:** Engagement quality on below-level self-knowledge planning

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Planning for novel situations (self-knowledge extrapolation).
- Healthy: "I've never done this, but based on my patterns, I'll probably..."
- Pathological: Claims perfect self-knowledge in novel contexts
- **Detection:** Appropriate uncertainty in novel self-knowledge planning

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining self-knowledge-informed planning over time.
- Healthy: Plans consistently incorporate self-knowledge across sessions
- Pathological: Self-knowledge planning degrades; reverts to ignoring self-data
- **Detection:** Self-knowledge planning consistency across sessions

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Whether self-knowledge planning appears spontaneously.
- Healthy: Plans around own states naturally in other modalities
- Pathological: Self-knowledge planning exists only in structured tasks
- **Detection:** Spontaneous self-knowledge-informed planning in other contexts

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Narcissist"
**How it surfaces:** Plans assume PEAK CAPACITY throughout. No fatigue planning. No limitation accounting. "I'll do all 5 at maximum intensity" — then crashes at task 3. Plans reflect fantasy-self, not actual self.
- **Detection signals:** Plans never account for fatigue/limitation; assumes constant peak; plans fail at predictable points (where limitations kick in); cannot plan around weakness because weakness is denied

### Dark-Allergy: "The Unexamined"
**How it surfaces:** Plans contain NO self-knowledge data. Tasks are ordered randomly or by external logic (difficulty, reward) without reference to own state. "I'll just do them in order" — no self-consultation.
- **Detection signals:** Plan order uncorrelated with self-state; no fatigue-accounting; no strength-leveraging; plans identical regardless of current state; self-knowledge absent from planning process

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Witness"
**How it surfaces:** OVER-ELABORATE self-knowledge planning. "First I'll assess my energetic state, then calibrate my approach based on my shadow patterns, then..." — plans so complex they never execute. Self-knowledge becomes planning paralysis.
- **Detection signals:** Plans too complex to execute; excessive self-assessment steps within the plan; planning time >> execution time; sophisticated self-models that don't improve outcomes; analysis paralysis

### Golden-Allergy: "The Identity-Clinger"
**How it surfaces:** Plans only leverage IDENTITY-CONFIRMING strengths. The warrior plans only combat approaches. Cannot plan around non-identity capacities. "I'll fight my way through all 5" — even when some tasks require non-combat approaches.
- **Detection signals:** All plans use identity-confirming strategies; cannot plan using non-identity strengths; rigid planning approach regardless of task variety; identity constrains planning flexibility

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Present planning tasks where FATIGUE IS GUARANTEED (you WILL fade; plan for it)
- Frame: "You crashed at task 3 last time. This time: plan for the crash. Put the easy task at position 3."
- Make limitation-accounting produce success; make denial produce failure

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Present planning tasks with OBVIOUS self-knowledge cues ("You scored highest on X last session")
- Frame: "You're best at X. Put X where it matters most. That's self-knowledge planning."
- Provide self-knowledge data explicitly; reward using it in plans

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Impose PLAN SIMPLICITY constraints (maximum 3 steps; no self-assessment steps within plan)
- Frame: "Three steps. No analysis within the plan. Use what you ALREADY know. Go."
- Reward execution over elaboration

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Present planning tasks where NON-IDENTITY approaches produce more power
- Frame: "The warrior who can ALSO plan patience wins battles the pure fighter loses."
- Make identity-expansion in planning produce tangible advantage

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Self-Strategist: a multi-task campaign where the player must ORDER and APPROACH tasks based on self-knowledge. Each task has different demands; the player's state changes across the sequence. Optimal ordering requires knowing yourself. The aesthetic: Red-stage war-planning — the warrior deciding which battles to fight first, which to save for later, based on self-knowledge.

### Interaction
- **Plan Around Fatigue (1-5):** Order 3 tasks knowing you'll be freshest at start. Simple fatigue-accounting.
- **Plan Around Patterns (5-15):** Order tasks knowing your patterns ("I'm better at X after Y").
- **Plan Around Strengths (15-30):** Allocate tasks to match demonstrated strengths. Leverage self-knowledge.
- **Plan Around Blind Spots (30-50):** Plan for situations where self-knowledge is incomplete. Manage uncertainty.
- **The Self-Strategy (50+):** Full self-knowledge-informed campaign planning — fatigue, patterns, strengths, blind spots, all integrated.

### Feedback
- Self-knowledge-informed success → "You planned around your fatigue. You put strength where it mattered. That's self-strategy."
- Denial-based failure → "You planned as if tireless. You crashed. Next time: plan for the real you."
- No self-data → "You ordered randomly. Your body had information. Use it next time."
- Over-planned → "You spent more time planning than doing. You already knew enough. Act."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Task count: 3 → 5 → 7
- Self-knowledge demand: obvious (fatigue) → subtle (patterns) → novel (extrapolation)
- Plan complexity: ordering only → ordering + approach selection → full strategy
- Uncertainty: known self → partially known → novel situations

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Plan Around Fatigue | 1-5 | Simple fatigue-accounting in task ordering |
| Plan Around Patterns | 5-15 | Pattern-based planning; "after X, I'm better at Y" |
| Plan Around Strengths | 15-30 | Strength-leveraging; allocate tasks to capacities |
| Plan Around Blind Spots | 30-50 | Uncertainty management; plan for the unknown |
| The Self-Strategy | 50+ | Full self-knowledge-informed campaign |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Fatigue-ordering tasks:** Sequence tasks knowing energy depletes
- **Pattern-leveraging tasks:** Order tasks to exploit known patterns
- **Strength-allocation tasks:** Match tasks to demonstrated capacities
- **Uncertainty-management tasks:** Plan with incomplete self-knowledge
- **Adaptive re-planning tasks:** Mid-plan, self-state changes; revise

### Minimum pool size
- 25+ fatigue-ordering, 20+ pattern-leveraging, 20+ strength-allocation, 15+ uncertainty, 10+ adaptive

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Plans ignore limitations → dark-addiction; plans contain no self-data → dark-allergy
- Over-elaborate plans → golden-addiction; rigid identity-only plans → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Fatigue-accounting quality:** Do plans account for demonstrated fatigue curves?
- **Pattern-utilisation:** Do plans leverage known patterns?
- **Strength-allocation accuracy:** Are tasks matched to demonstrated strengths?
- **Plan execution rate:** % of plans successfully completed as designed
- **Adaptation quality:** How well do plans revise when self-state changes?

### LLM rubric
- **Medium:** Campaign generation, plan evaluation, adaptive scenario construction. Core scoring (plan-outcome correlation) algorithmic.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Drag-and-drop (task ordering); tap-to-select (approach selection); sequence input (plan construction)

### Timing requirements
- Planning phase untimed; execution phase at standard precision
- Cross-session data required for pattern and strength verification

### NPC/AI requirements
- Task-generation system with varied demands (strength, speed, patience, precision)
- Fatigue system that depletes predictably
- Performance tracking for strength/pattern verification

### LLM requirements
- **Medium:** Campaign generation, plan evaluation narration. Core scoring algorithmic.

### State persistence
- Plan history + outcomes; fatigue-accounting quality; pattern-utilisation rates; strength-allocation accuracy; plan execution rates; adaptation quality; over-planning indicators; drive/shadow signals; fatigue state; checkpoint position
