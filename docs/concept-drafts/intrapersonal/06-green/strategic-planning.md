# Intrapersonal / Green — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** Planning with the plural self — structuring life across competing self-aspects.  **Why this axis for this module:** Planning reveals whether multiplicity is functional or paralysing; the capacity to allocate resources across genuine self-parts without collapsing or refusing hierarchy is the strategic expression of Green intrapersonal.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Title:** Life Architect  
**Core loop:** Player manages a simulated life where multiple self-aspects each have needs, goals, and time-demands. Player must create schedules, allocate resources, and make trade-offs that honour multiplicity while maintaining coherence. Planning patterns reveal shadow type.  
**Session length:** 5–9 minutes per planning cycle.  
**Infinite checkpoint:** Yes — each planning cycle (one simulated week) is self-contained.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst type:** Resource-scarcity across self-aspects forces genuine prioritisation without elimination — the developmental demand of functional multiplicity.  
**Shadow surfacing:** DA surfaces as incoherent plans (resources scattered randomly, no throughline). DAll surfaces as mono-plans (one self-aspect gets everything, others starved). GA surfaces as meta-planning ("I'll just be present to whatever arises") that produces no actionable structure. GAll surfaces as perfectly equal distribution (every aspect gets exactly the same, regardless of context or need).  
**Drive probes:** Agency = coherent plan with clear centre. Communion = plans that include relational/contextual responsiveness. Eros = plans that stretch toward growth edges. Agape = plans that tend to neglected aspects.

## 3. Game Design

**Mechanic 1 — Aspect Inventory:** Player's simulated life has 4–6 active self-aspects, each with health bars, goals, and weekly needs. Neglected aspects decay; over-served aspects plateau.  
**Mechanic 2 — Weekly Allocation:** Fixed time-resource pool (168 hours abstracted to 20 tokens). Player distributes tokens across aspects. Constraints: some aspects conflict (creative time vs. social time), some synergise (rest + reflection).  
**Mechanic 3 — Disruption Events:** Mid-week events force re-planning (crisis demands one aspect's full attention). Player must adapt without abandoning the others. Recovery planning scored.  
**Progression:** 4 aspects → 6 aspects. Fewer tokens (more scarcity). More disruptions. Aspects develop competing long-term goals that require multi-week strategy.

## 4. Item Pool

- 8 self-aspects available (player activates 4–6): career-builder, relationship-tender, body-keeper, creative-explorer, spiritual-seeker, community-member, learner, rest-guardian
- 16 disruption events (illness, opportunity, conflict, loss, inspiration, obligation, crisis, celebration — each pulling toward specific aspects)
- 12 synergy/conflict pairs between aspects
- Long-term goal tracks: each aspect has a 4-week arc that rewards sustained attention without demanding exclusivity

## 5. Technical Requirements

**Input:** Drag-and-drop token allocation. Tap to respond to disruptions. Swipe to advance week.  
**Metrics captured:** Distribution entropy (even vs. skewed), coherence score (do allocations serve stated goals), adaptability (recovery after disruption), shadow classification from allocation patterns.  
**Adaptive engine:** Aspect-count and token-scarcity adjusted per player. Disruption frequency tuned to probe suspected shadow. Long-term arcs selected to create genuine tension between aspects.  
**Simulation fidelity:** Aspect health responds realistically — neglect causes decay, over-attention causes diminishing returns. Feedback loops visible to player.  
**Accessibility:** All allocation via simple drag/tap. No time pressure on planning phase. Disruption responses have generous windows. Screen-reader compatible token counts.
