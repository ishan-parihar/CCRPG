# Intrapersonal / Orange — Social-Cooperative Game Concept

> **Axis:** Self-in-relation — how self-concept functions in group contexts, maintaining identity while adapting to others.  **Why this axis for this module:** Self-authorship is tested most acutely in social contexts where the pressure to conform, perform, or dissolve is strongest — genuine self-knowledge must survive contact with others.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Name:** The Unchanged Self  
**Core loop:** Player enters cooperative scenarios where group dynamics pressure identity. The game measures whether the player's self-concept remains coherent under social influence — not rigidly unchanged, but genuinely self-authored rather than socially constructed in the moment.  
**Feel:** Social but introspective. The group is the mirror, not the goal. Every interaction is an opportunity to discover whether your self-concept is yours or borrowed.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst type:** Social-identity pressure — situations where group dynamics invite the player to abandon, perform, or rigidify their self-concept.  
**DA surfacing:** The Self-Optimizer uses the group as another metric — "How am I performing relative to others?" Self-concept becomes comparative. Identity in group = rank. They cannot simply *be* in relation without measuring.  
**DAll surfacing:** The Unexamined has no self-concept to maintain in group contexts. They mirror the group, adopt whatever role is available, or withdraw entirely. No stable self appears under social pressure because none was authored.  
**GA surfacing:** The Premature Process-Self performs depth in group contexts — "I'm the deep one," "I see all perspectives" — without genuine self-knowledge underlying the performance. They adapt too fluidly because there's no real self to maintain.  
**GAll surfacing:** The Fixed Self cannot adapt at all. Group feedback is rejected. Others' perspectives on them are dismissed. They maintain identity by refusing all social input — coherent but isolated.  
**Drive probes:** Agency = maintaining self-authored identity under group pressure. Communion = genuinely incorporating others' perspectives without losing self. Eros = curiosity about how others see you (growth data). Agape = accepting social feedback without defensiveness or collapse.

## 3. Game Design

**Cooperative tasks:** Group challenges (with AI agents or async multiplayer) where success requires coordination. The intrapersonal measurement is embedded: before/during/after each task, the player reports their self-concept and the system tracks stability vs. drift.  
**Social pressure mechanics:** (a) Conformity pressure — group consensus differs from player's stated position. (b) Role pressure — group assigns a role that conflicts with self-concept. (c) Feedback pressure — group members offer perspectives on the player that challenge self-image. (d) Performance pressure — group success depends on the player in ways that activate DA.  
**Self-report checkpoints:** At intervals, player answers: "Who are you being right now?" Responses compared to pre-group baseline and to language-reflective data. Drift without awareness = shadow activation.  
**Progression:** Early sessions use low-pressure groups (supportive AI agents). Mid-game introduces disagreement, role-conflict, and honest feedback. Late-game presents scenarios where maintaining authentic self-concept has social cost.  
**Shadow-mode:** Group dynamics specifically designed to activate the dominant shadow — e.g., for DAll: a group that explicitly asks "What do you think? Who are you in this?" forcing self-articulation.

## 4. Item Pool

| Item category | Examples | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Conformity scenarios | Group consensus opposes player's stated view | Agency under pressure |
| Role assignments | "You're the leader / the quiet one / the critic" | Identity vs. role |
| Feedback rounds | AI agents offer honest observations about player | Feedback integration |
| Comparative contexts | Visible performance rankings in group | DA activation |
| Identity articulation | "Introduce yourself to the group" (repeated across contexts) | Self-concept stability |
| Adaptation challenges | Group needs player to flex — can they without losing self? | Communion capacity |

Scenarios scale from supportive → challenging → confrontational.

## 5. Technical Requirements

**AI agent system:** Cooperative partners with distinct personalities and interaction styles. Agents must be sophisticated enough to create genuine social pressure without being manipulative.  
**Self-concept tracking:** Continuous comparison between player's stated self-concept (from language-reflective baseline) and in-group behaviour/self-reports. Drift detection algorithm flags unconscious identity-shifts.  
**Scoring:** Primary metric = self-concept coherence under social pressure (stability without rigidity). Secondary = adaptation quality (can player flex without losing core?). Tertiary = feedback integration (does social input improve self-knowledge?).  
**Session length:** 15–20 minutes. Social scenarios require setup and debrief time.  
**Multiplayer option:** Async cooperative with real players (optional). AI agents as default. Real-player interactions provide higher-fidelity social pressure but require matchmaking and safety protocols.
