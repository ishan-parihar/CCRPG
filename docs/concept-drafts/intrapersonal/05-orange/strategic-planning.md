# Intrapersonal / Orange — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** Planning self-development — structuring growth across time, managing identity-goals.  **Why this axis for this module:** Self-authorship is not just knowing who you are now but projecting who you will become — and whether your plans for yourself are honest, achievable, and genuinely self-authored rather than shadow-driven.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Name:** The Architect's Table  
**Core loop:** Player designs development plans for a character whose traits mirror their own gameplay data. They set goals, allocate resources (time, energy, attention), predict obstacles, and sequence growth steps. The game then simulates outcomes and compares planned trajectory to actual player behaviour patterns.  
**Feel:** Strategic, reflective, future-oriented. The player is both architect and subject — designing a life-plan and being confronted with whether it matches who they actually are.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst type:** Planning-reality discrepancy — the gap between how you plan your growth and how you actually grow (or don't).  
**DA surfacing:** The Self-Optimizer creates hyper-optimised plans with no rest, no plateau tolerance, no "good enough." Every resource is allocated to improvement. Plans are beautiful but unsustainable — and the simulation reveals burnout trajectories.  
**DAll surfacing:** The Unexamined cannot plan self-development because they have no self-model to develop. Plans are generic ("get better at stuff"), role-based ("do what's expected"), or absent. The planning interface itself becomes the catalyst — it demands a self-model.  
**GA surfacing:** The Premature Process-Self creates plans that sound profound but lack concrete steps. "Integrate my shadow" without specifying which shadow or how. Plans are aspirational narratives, not actionable sequences.  
**GAll surfacing:** The Fixed Self creates plans that reinforce existing identity rather than expanding it. All goals are within the known self. No plan includes genuine novelty or identity-revision. Growth is defined as "more of what I already am."  
**Drive probes:** Agency = authoring plans from internal values vs. external expectations. Communion = incorporating relational goals and feedback. Eros = planning toward the unknown (growth beyond current self). Agape = planning rest, integration, and acceptance alongside growth.

## 3. Game Design

**Planning interface:** Player allocates limited "development points" across identity dimensions (drawn from their actual gameplay profile). Must make trade-offs — cannot improve everything simultaneously.  
**Constraint system:** Plans must include: (a) specific goals, (b) predicted obstacles, (c) rest/integration periods, (d) revision triggers ("I'll change this plan if…"). Missing elements are flagged but not forced.  
**Simulation:** The system projects plan outcomes based on the player's actual behavioural patterns from other modules. Reveals where plans conflict with demonstrated tendencies.  
**Retrospective:** After N sessions, player reviews their actual trajectory against their plan. Discrepancy analysis reveals which shadow drove the gap.  
**Progression:** Early sessions plan short-term (1 week). Mid-game extends to months. Late-game introduces identity-level planning ("Who do I want to become?") and confronts the player with whether their plans serve growth or shadow.

## 4. Item Pool

| Item category | Examples | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Goal-setting prompts | "What one thing would you develop next?" | Self-knowledge → planning |
| Resource allocation | "You have 10 points — distribute across 5 dimensions" | Priority honesty |
| Obstacle prediction | "What will get in the way?" | Self-awareness of patterns |
| Rest planning | "When will you stop and integrate?" | DA vs. healthy pacing |
| Revision triggers | "Under what conditions would you change this plan?" | GAll vs. flexibility |
| Identity goals | "Who do you want to become?" | Eros expression |

Items progress from concrete/short-term to abstract/long-term.

## 5. Technical Requirements

**Profile integration:** Requires player's cross-module performance data to generate realistic simulation projections. Plans are tested against actual behavioural patterns, not ideals.  
**Simulation engine:** Simple trajectory model: given player's demonstrated growth rate, consistency, and shadow patterns, project likely outcomes of their plan. Not predictive — revelatory.  
**Scoring:** Primary metric = plan-reality alignment (do plans match actual behaviour?). Secondary = plan completeness (are all elements present?). Tertiary = revision quality (do plan updates reflect genuine learning?).  
**Session length:** 15–25 minutes. Planning is inherently slower — sessions are longer but less frequent (1–2 per week recommended).  
**Temporal persistence:** Plans stored and revisited across sessions. The game has a built-in retrospective cycle that surfaces plan-vs-reality discrepancies at appropriate intervals.
