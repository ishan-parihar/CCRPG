# Cognitive / Magenta — Scenario-Choice Game Concept

> **Axis:** The scenario-choice axis probes cognitive intelligence through DECISIONS — can the player use symbolic thinking to make choices? At Magenta, choices are magical: "If I pick the fire-rune, the door opens. If I pick the water-rune, the river calms." The question is whether the player can use symbols to REASON about outcomes (pre-operational reasoning).
>
> **Why this axis for Cognitive/Magenta:** Symbolic thinking becomes USEFUL when it guides choice. At Magenta, choices are simple (one symbol → one outcome) but the player must hold the symbol-outcome link in mind. This is the bridge between symbol-holding (deterministic) and symbol-application (real-world use).

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Rune-Chooser"
- **Core mechanic:** The player faces simple symbolic dilemmas — choose a rune, and the world responds. Each choice requires holding a symbol-meaning link in mind and selecting based on desired outcome. Pre-operational reasoning through magical choice.
- **Duration:** 3-5 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** The Simple Choice → The Linked Choice → The Costly Choice → The Uncertain Choice → The Wise Choice

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "Two runes. One opens the door. One wakes the beast. Which do you choose?" The contact boundary is: "Can you use symbols to guide your choices?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* How does the player choose? The Magical Thinker chooses by "feeling the power" (not by symbol-meaning). The Concrete-Bound chooses randomly (symbols don't mean anything). The Premature Reasoner over-analyses simple choices. The Wonder-Refuser won't choose (choosing commits to the symbolic world).
- *Emergent:* Can they hold symbol-meaning links and choose accordingly? Can they handle uncertainty?

**Integration path:** Rewards SYMBOL-GUIDED choice — choosing based on what the symbol MEANS, not magical feeling, random selection, or over-analysis. Simple, accurate, symbol-informed decisions.

**Successful integration:** The player holds symbol-meaning links, chooses based on desired outcomes, and accepts the consequences of symbolic choices.

---

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Choices where the "powerful" option conflicts with the "correct" option.
- Healthy: Chooses based on symbol-meaning, not power-fantasy
- Pathological: Always chooses the "powerful" rune regardless of meaning
- **Detection:** Whether choice is guided by symbol-meaning or power-seeking

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Self-directed choice (no prompt about which outcome is desired).
- Healthy: Identifies own desired outcome and chooses accordingly
- Pathological: Cannot choose without being told what to want
- **Detection:** Self-directed choice quality

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Choices that affect others (ally benefits from one choice, player from another).
- Healthy: Can choose for others' benefit using symbolic reasoning
- Pathological: Cannot consider others in symbolic choice; always self-serving
- **Detection:** Whether symbolic reasoning extends to others' outcomes

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Receiving choice-guidance from others (ally suggests which rune).
- Healthy: Integrates ally's suggestion with own symbolic understanding
- Pathological: Cannot accept guidance; must choose alone
- **Detection:** Integration quality of received guidance

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Familiar choices (well-known symbol-outcome links).
- Healthy: Maintains accurate choosing on familiar dilemmas
- Pathological: Bored with familiar choices; seeks novelty over accuracy
- **Detection:** Accuracy maintenance on below-level familiar choices

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Novel choices (new symbols, unknown outcomes).
- Healthy: Approaches with curiosity; uses available information
- Pathological: Claims to know outcomes of novel symbols (premature certainty)
- **Detection:** Appropriate uncertainty vs. false confidence in novel choices

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Returning to simple choices after advancement.
- Healthy: Returns to basics with ease; maintains foundation
- Pathological: Treats simple choices as beneath them
- **Detection:** Engagement quality on maintenance-level choices

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Whether symbolic choice-making appears in other contexts.
- Healthy: Uses symbol-meaning links to guide choices elsewhere
- Pathological: Symbolic choice exists only in structured dilemma tasks
- **Detection:** Transfer of symbolic choice-making to ecological context

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Magical Thinker"
**How it surfaces:** Chooses based on MAGICAL FEELING rather than symbol-meaning. "I feel the power in this rune." The choice is ritual, not reasoning. Correct choices happen by coincidence, not understanding.
- **Detection signals:** Choice accuracy at chance despite high confidence; ritual behaviours before choosing; "I feel it" explanations; no correlation between symbol-meaning knowledge and choice accuracy

### Dark-Allergy: "The Concrete-Bound"
**How it surfaces:** Chooses RANDOMLY or by physical properties (colour, position) rather than symbolic meaning. "I'll pick the left one." Symbols don't inform choice.
- **Detection signals:** Choice accuracy at chance; position/colour biases; no use of symbol-meaning information; "They're just shapes" attitude

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Reasoner"
**How it surfaces:** OVER-ANALYSES simple choices. Seeks hidden patterns, second-order effects, meta-strategies — all beyond Magenta capacity. Paralysis by analysis on choices that should be simple.
- **Detection signals:** Long decision times on simple choices; verbalised complex reasoning about simple dilemmas; seeks patterns that don't exist; treats Magenta choices as Orange-level puzzles

### Golden-Allergy: "The Wonder-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** REFUSES to choose because choosing commits to the symbolic world. "What if I choose wrong?" is really "What if choosing makes the symbols real?" Approach-avoidance at the choice point.
- **Detection signals:** Excessive hesitation; choice-avoidance; "I don't want to choose"; approach-withdrawal at decision points; can choose when forced but avoids voluntary choice

---

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Make symbol-meaning EXPLICIT before choice: "This rune means fire. Fire opens the door. Choose."
- Remove magical ambiguity; make the link CLEAR and LEARNABLE
- Frame: "You don't need to feel the power. You need to know what it means. Look at the symbol."
- Reward choices based on stated meaning, not feeling

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Make symbol-meaning VISIBLE and TANGIBLE: "See the flame shape? It means fire. Fire opens doors."
- Bridge from physical to symbolic: the symbol LOOKS LIKE what it means
- Frame: "It's not random. The shape tells you. Look at it — what does it look like?"
- Reward ANY symbol-guided choice; celebrate first meaning-based selection

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- SIMPLIFY choices; remove complexity: "Two runes. One means open. One means close. Which do you want?"
- Block over-analysis; reward SPEED on simple choices
- Frame: "This is simple. Don't overthink. What does the rune mean? Choose."
- Make simplicity satisfying; depth comes later

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Make choices REVERSIBLE: "Choose. If you don't like it, choose again."
- Remove consequence-anxiety: "Nothing bad happens if you pick wrong. Try."
- Frame: "It's just a choice. You can always choose differently next time."
- Reward choosing itself; accuracy secondary to engagement

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Rune-Chooser: a magical choice-space where symbols lead to outcomes. The player faces rune-dilemmas and must choose based on symbol-meaning. The aesthetic: Magenta-stage crossroads — two paths marked by runes, each leading somewhere different. The world responds to the choice.

### Interaction
- **The Simple Choice (1-5):** One rune = one outcome. "Fire-rune opens the fire-door." Binary, clear.
- **The Linked Choice (5-15):** Symbol-meaning must be REMEMBERED from earlier. "You learned fire-rune means open. Now choose."
- **The Costly Choice (15-30):** Both options have value; one must be sacrificed. Simple trade-offs.
- **The Uncertain Choice (30-50):** Partial information; must infer from symbol-properties.
- **The Wise Choice (50+):** Full symbolic choice-making — hold meaning, weigh options, choose with understanding.

### Feedback
- Correct symbol-guided choice → "You read the rune truly. The door opens."
- Magical-feeling choice (correct by luck) → "It worked, but did you know WHY? What did the rune mean?"
- Random choice → "You guessed. Next time, look at the rune. It tells you."
- Over-analysis → "Simpler. What does the rune mean? That's your answer."
- Refusal → "The runes wait. Whenever you're ready. No rush."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Number of options: 2 → 3 → 4
- Symbol-meaning transparency: obvious (looks like referent) → learned → abstract
- Consequence weight: trivial → moderate → significant
- Information completeness: full → partial → minimal

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The Simple Choice | 1-5 | Binary; symbol-meaning obvious |
| The Linked Choice | 5-15 | Must remember learned meanings |
| The Costly Choice | 15-30 | Trade-offs; both options have value |
| The Uncertain Choice | 30-50 | Partial information; inference needed |
| The Wise Choice | 50+ | Full symbolic choice-making |

---

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Binary choices:** Two runes, clear outcomes
- **Memory-linked choices:** Outcomes depend on previously learned meanings
- **Trade-off choices:** Both options valuable; sacrifice required
- **Inference choices:** Partial information; must reason from symbol-properties
- **Consequence chains:** Choice leads to further choices (simple branching)

### Minimum pool size
- 30+ binary, 25+ memory-linked, 20+ trade-off, 15+ inference, 10+ chains

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Magical-feeling choices → dark-addiction; random/position choices → dark-allergy
- Over-analysis → golden-addiction; choice-avoidance → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Choice accuracy:** Does the choice match the symbol-meaning → desired outcome link?
- **Symbol-meaning retention:** Can they remember learned symbol-meanings across trials?
- **Trade-off quality:** Do they choose the higher-value option in trade-offs?
- **Inference quality:** Can they reason from partial symbol-information?
- **Decision confidence:** Appropriate confidence (not magical certainty or paralysis)

### LLM rubric
- **High:** Scenario generation, outcome narration, choice-quality evaluation (especially for trade-offs and inference). Core binary accuracy is algorithmic.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Tap-to-select (choose rune); drag-to-place (place rune on target); hold-to-confirm (deliberate choice)

### Timing requirements
- Decision time tracked (diagnostic, not punishing); no time pressure at early phases
- Cross-session tracking for symbol-meaning retention

### NPC/AI requirements
- Scenario environments that respond to choices (doors open, paths clear, creatures respond)
- Optional: ally who suggests choices (communion probing)

### LLM requirements
- **High:** Scenario generation, outcome narration, trade-off construction, inference-item generation. Binary accuracy scoring algorithmic.

### State persistence
- Choice history; accuracy per choice-type; decision times; symbol-meaning retention scores; trade-off quality; inference quality; magical-feeling indicators; random-choice indicators; over-analysis indicators; avoidance indicators; drive/shadow signals; fatigue state; checkpoint position
