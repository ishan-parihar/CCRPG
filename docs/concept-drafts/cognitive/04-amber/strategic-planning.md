# Cognitive / Amber — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** The strategic-planning axis probes cognitive intelligence through MULTI-STEP RULE-GOVERNED PLANNING — can the player plan a sequence of actions within a rule-system? At Amber, this is: "The code requires three steps in order. Plan them. Execute them. What comes first? What comes next? What comes last?"
>
> **Why this axis for Cognitive/Amber:** Concrete operational thinking includes SEQUENCING within rules — Tower-of-London-style planning at 3 disks, multi-step procedures, liturgical sequences. This modality tests whether the player can plan and execute ordered actions within a rule-governed system.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Liturgy's Order"
- **Core mechanic:** The player must PLAN and EXECUTE multi-step sequences within a rule-system. Not free planning but RULE-CONSTRAINED planning. "The liturgy requires: first the candle, then the bell, then the word. In that order. Plan your path to complete the rite."
- **Duration:** 3-5 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** The First Rite → The Ordered Steps → The Constrained Path → The Changing Liturgy → The Master Planner

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "The rite has an order. Steps must be taken in sequence. Constraints must be honoured. Can you PLAN the path? Not just do the next thing — SEE the whole sequence and execute it in order." The contact boundary is: "Can you plan multi-step actions within rules?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* How does the player plan? The Rigid Systematiser plans PERFECTLY for the known rite but CANNOT ADAPT when the liturgy changes. The Rule-Breaker cannot plan at all (impulsive execution, wrong order). The Premature Theorist attempts 5-step plans when 3-step capacity is unstable. The System-Refuser plans well for familiar rites but refuses novel sequences.
- *Emergent:* Can they plan within rules flexibly — sequencing correctly while adapting to changes?

**Integration path:** Rewards FLEXIBLE RULE-GOVERNED PLANNING — correct sequencing WITH adaptability. Not rigid, not chaotic, not overreaching, not frozen.

**Successful integration:** The player can plan multi-step actions within a rule-system — the executive foundation of concrete operational thought.

---

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Planning independently (no hints or scaffolding).
- Healthy: Plans sequences independently; sees the path without help
- Pathological: Cannot plan without guidance; OR: plans rigidly without considering alternatives
- **Detection:** Whether planning is self-generated and flexible

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Planning under constraint-ambiguity (unclear which constraints apply).
- Healthy: Makes reasonable planning assumptions under ambiguity
- Pathological: Freezes when constraints are unclear; cannot plan without certainty
- **Detection:** Planning quality under ambiguity

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Planning when companion suggests different sequence.
- Healthy: Considers companion's suggestion while maintaining own plan
- Pathological: Adopts companion's plan immediately; OR: rigidly ignores input
- **Detection:** Whether own plan survives social input

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Collaborative planning (sequencing together).
- Healthy: Contributes to shared plan AND accepts others' contributions
- Pathological: Cannot collaborate; dominates or defers entirely
- **Detection:** Quality of collaborative planning

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Planning consistency over time.
- Healthy: Plans consistently across similar problems
- Pathological: Plans are inconsistent; same problem gets different sequences
- **Detection:** Planning consistency

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Growing planning complexity (longer sequences, more constraints).
- Healthy: Engages increasing complexity with appropriate effort
- Pathological: Cannot grow; OR: jumps to complexity beyond capacity
- **Detection:** Planning growth quality

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining planning capacity across sessions.
- Healthy: Returns to planning tasks with maintained capacity
- Pathological: Planning capacity degrades; must relearn sequencing
- **Detection:** Cross-session planning stability

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Whether planning appears spontaneously in other contexts.
- Healthy: Plans sequences naturally in non-planning tasks
- Pathological: Planning exists only in explicit planning tasks
- **Detection:** Spontaneous planning transfer

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Rigid Systematiser"
**How it surfaces:** Plans PERFECTLY for known sequences but CANNOT ADAPT. When the liturgy changes (new order, new constraints), the plan perseverates on the old sequence. Cannot replan.
- **Detection signals:** Perfect execution of familiar sequences; failure on novel sequences; perseverates on old plan after change; "but the order is…"; cannot generate alternative plans

### Dark-Allergy: "The Rule-Breaker"
**How it surfaces:** CANNOT PLAN. Executes impulsively — does the first thing that comes to mind without sequencing. Wrong order. Skipped steps. No plan, just action.
- **Detection signals:** Wrong sequence order; skipped steps; impulsive execution; no pause before action; "I'll just do it"; cannot state plan before executing

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Theorist"
**How it surfaces:** Attempts COMPLEX plans beyond capacity. Tries 5-step sequences when 3-step is unstable. Plans elaborate strategies that fail in execution.
- **Detection signals:** Attempts longer sequences than capacity; elaborate plans that fail; "I can handle more"; 3-step accuracy <85% but attempts 4-5 step; plans exceed execution

### Golden-Allergy: "The System-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** Plans well for FAMILIAR sequences but REFUSES novel ones. Cannot tolerate new planning challenges. Stays with known rites.
- **Detection signals:** High accuracy on familiar sequences; refusal of novel ones; "I know this rite"; avoids new planning challenges; anxiety at novel sequences; "why change what works?"

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Introduce GRADUAL liturgy-changes (one step different, not whole new sequence)
- Reward replanning: "The rite changed slightly. Can you see the new order? Good. Plan again."
- Frame: "The old rite served well. The new rite is similar. Just one step different. You can plan this."
- Build: adaptive planning (can replan when sequence changes)

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Start with 2-STEP sequences (minimal planning): "First the candle. Then the bell. Two steps. Plan: candle, bell. Go."
- Make planning BRIEF and REWARDING
- Frame: "Just two steps. Think first: what's first? What's second? Good. Now do it. That's planning."
- Build: basic sequencing (plan before act)

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- LOCK complexity until current level is mastered (>90% on 3-step required for 4-step)
- Reward mastery: "Perfect 3-step execution. You OWN this level. Now earn the next."
- Frame: "Master this rite first. The longer liturgy requires this foundation to be solid."
- Build: planning mastery before advancement

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Introduce SLIGHT novelty (familiar rite with one new element)
- Reward engagement with novelty: "A new step in the familiar rite. You planned it. Good."
- Frame: "Almost the same rite. Just one new thing. Your planning still works. Just adapt slightly."
- Build: comfort with novel planning (new sequences aren't threatening)

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Liturgy's Order: a ritual-planning space where the player must sequence actions to complete rites. Steps are shown; constraints are given; the player must plan the correct order and execute. The aesthetic: Amber-stage cathedral — ritual objects arranged on an altar, the player must arrange/execute them in the correct liturgical order.

### Interaction
- **The First Rite (1-5):** 2-step sequence. "Candle, then bell. Plan. Execute." Minimal planning.
- **The Ordered Steps (5-15):** 3-step sequences with constraints. "Bell before word. Candle before bell. What order?"
- **The Constrained Path (15-30):** 3-step with multiple constraints. Tower-of-London-lite. "Cannot move X until Y is placed."
- **The Changing Liturgy (30-50):** Familiar rites with changed order. Must replan.
- **The Master Planner (50+):** Full integration: 3-step + constraints + adaptation + novel sequences.

### Feedback
- Correct sequence → "The rite is complete. The order was honoured. Well planned."
- Perseveration → "The rite changed. Look again. What's the new order? The old plan doesn't serve here."
- Impulsive execution → "Wait. Plan first. What's step one? Step two? Step three? THEN execute."
- Overreaching → "Simpler. Three steps. Master these. The longer rite will come when you're ready."
- Refuses novelty → "Almost the same rite. One thing different. Your planning still works. Just adjust."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Sequence length: 2-step → 3-step → 3-step with constraints
- Constraints: none → one → multiple (Tower-of-London style)
- Novelty: familiar sequences → slight variations → novel sequences
- Time: unlimited → time-awareness → competing demands
- Adaptation: fixed sequences → sequences that change between sessions

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The First Rite | 1-5 | 2-step minimal planning |
| The Ordered Steps | 5-15 | 3-step constrained sequencing |
| The Constrained Path | 15-30 | Multiple constraints (ToL-lite) |
| The Changing Liturgy | 30-50 | Adaptive replanning |
| The Master Planner | 50+ | Full planning integration |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Simple sequences:** 2-3 step rites with clear order
- **Constrained sequences:** 3-step with ordering constraints
- **Tower-of-London items:** Multi-constraint planning problems (3 disks)
- **Adaptive sequences:** Familiar rites with changed elements
- **Novel sequences:** Entirely new rites requiring fresh planning

### Minimum pool size
- 20+ simple, 20+ constrained, 15+ ToL-style, 10+ adaptive, 10+ novel

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Perseverates on old plan → dark-addiction; impulsive/no plan → dark-allergy
- Attempts beyond capacity → golden-addiction; refuses novel sequences → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Sequence accuracy:** % correct step-order execution
- **Planning efficiency:** Minimum moves to solution (ToL-style)
- **Constraint adherence:** % trials where all constraints honoured
- **Adaptation speed:** Trials-to-correct after sequence change
- **Novel planning:** Accuracy on never-before-seen sequences

### LLM rubric
- **None/Minimal:** Fully algorithmic. Sequence correctness, constraint adherence, and planning efficiency are all objective measures.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Drag (arrange sequence); tap (execute steps in order); hold (confirm plan before execution)

### Timing requirements
- Planning time tracked (time between presentation and first action); execution time tracked; no time pressure but efficiency noted

### NPC/AI requirements
- Sequence generation system (rites with varying constraints)
- Constraint system (ordering rules, Tower-of-London mechanics)
- Planning efficiency calculator (minimum moves)
- Perseveration detection (repeated old-plan attempts after change)
- Adaptive difficulty (length, constraints, novelty)

### LLM requirements
- **None:** Fully deterministic. Sequence correctness and constraint adherence are binary.

### State persistence
- Planning history; sequence accuracy; efficiency scores; constraint adherence; adaptation speed; novel-planning accuracy; perseveration counts; impulsivity indicators; overreach indicators; novelty-avoidance indicators; drive/shadow signals; fatigue state; checkpoint position
