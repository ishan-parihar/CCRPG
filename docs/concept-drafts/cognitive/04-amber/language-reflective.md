# Cognitive / Amber — Language-Reflective Game Concept

> **Axis:** The language-reflective axis probes cognitive intelligence through VERBAL ARTICULATION of thinking processes. At Amber, this is: "Can you STATE the rule? Can you EXPLAIN the classification? Can you DESCRIBE what changed?" Not just following rules — ARTICULATING them.
>
> **Why this axis for Cognitive/Amber:** Concrete operational thinking at Amber includes the ability to VERBALISE rules — "I sorted by colour because the rule said colour." This modality tests whether the player can articulate their cognitive process, not just execute it. The gap between doing and saying reveals shadow.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Scribe's Testimony"
- **Core mechanic:** The player must ARTICULATE rules, classifications, and patterns in words. Not just follow the rule — EXPLAIN it. "What is the rule? Why did you sort it that way? What changed?" The scribe records the player's understanding of the code.
- **Duration:** 3-5 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** The First Testimony → The Named Rule → The Explained Classification → The Witnessed Change → The Living Scribe

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "You followed the rule. Good. Now TELL me the rule. Not just do it — SAY it. What is the rule? Why does it work? What would happen if it changed?" The contact boundary is: "Can you articulate your cognitive process?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* How does the player articulate rules? The Rigid Systematiser states rules as ABSOLUTE ("The rule IS: always sort by colour. ALWAYS."). The Rule-Breaker cannot articulate any rule ("I don't know. I just did it. Whatever."). The Premature Theorist uses abstract meta-language ("The underlying principle is categorical differentiation across dimensional axes"). The System-Refuser can state the rule but refuses to discuss its basis ("The rule is the rule. Why do you need to know WHY?").
- *Emergent:* Can they articulate rules clearly, accurately, and with appropriate flexibility?

**Integration path:** Rewards CLEAR, ACCURATE, FLEXIBLE rule-articulation — stating what the rule IS without absolutising it, explaining classifications without rigidifying them, describing changes without distress.

**Successful integration:** The player can articulate their cognitive process — the verbal foundation of metacognition.

---

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Articulating rules independently (no prompts or scaffolding).
- Healthy: States rules clearly and confidently without external support
- Pathological: Cannot articulate without prompting; OR: over-articulates rigidly
- **Detection:** Whether rule-articulation is self-generated and proportionate

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Articulating under ambiguity (rule is unclear or novel).
- Healthy: Offers tentative articulation: "I think the rule might be…"
- Pathological: Cannot articulate when uncertain; freezes; OR: states false certainty
- **Detection:** Articulation quality under ambiguity

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Articulating when the system provides a different explanation.
- Healthy: Maintains own articulation while considering alternative
- Pathological: Adopts system's explanation immediately; OR: rigidly rejects it
- **Detection:** Whether own articulation survives contradiction

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Building shared understanding (articulating for another's benefit).
- Healthy: Adjusts articulation for the listener; explains clearly
- Pathological: Cannot adjust; explains only for self; OR: cannot explain at all
- **Detection:** Quality of communicative articulation

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Articulation consistency over time.
- Healthy: Articulates rules consistently across sessions
- Pathological: Articulations change randomly; no stable verbal model
- **Detection:** Verbal model stability

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Growing articulation complexity (more nuanced explanations).
- Healthy: Articulations become more precise and nuanced over time
- Pathological: Cannot grow; OR: jumps to abstract language without concrete backing
- **Detection:** Articulation growth quality

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining articulation capacity over time.
- Healthy: Can articulate rules after absence (verbal model persists)
- Pathological: Cannot articulate after absence; verbal model degrades
- **Detection:** Articulation persistence

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Whether articulation appears spontaneously.
- Healthy: Spontaneously narrates own cognitive process in other tasks
- Pathological: Articulation exists only when explicitly prompted
- **Detection:** Spontaneous metacognitive narration

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Rigid Systematiser"
**How it surfaces:** Articulates rules as ABSOLUTE. "The rule IS. Always. Never changes. Cannot change." Uses rigid language — "always," "never," "must," "the only way." Cannot articulate exceptions or conditions.
- **Detection signals:** Absolute language; "always/never/must"; cannot state conditions; rigid formulations; distress when asked "what if the rule changed?"; "that's THE rule"

### Dark-Allergy: "The Rule-Breaker"
**How it surfaces:** CANNOT ARTICULATE rules. "I don't know. I just did it. Whatever. Who cares what the rule is?" Either genuinely cannot verbalise their process or dismisses the attempt.
- **Detection signals:** "I don't know"; "whatever"; cannot state any rule; dismissive; "who cares?"; no verbal model; responses are action-only

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Theorist"
**How it surfaces:** Uses ABSTRACT META-LANGUAGE without concrete backing. "The underlying principle is categorical differentiation." Sounds sophisticated but cannot state the SIMPLE rule. Verbal performance exceeds cognitive capacity.
- **Detection signals:** Abstract vocabulary; meta-language; cannot simplify; "the principle is…" but cannot state the concrete rule; verbal sophistication without operational backing

### Golden-Allergy: "The System-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** Can state the rule but REFUSES TO DISCUSS ITS BASIS. "The rule is the rule. Why do you need to know why? Just follow it." Articulation stops at the surface — no willingness to examine.
- **Detection signals:** Surface-only articulation; "the rule is the rule"; refuses "why" questions; "just follow it"; anxiety when asked to examine rule-basis; "that's not important"

---

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Present rules that have CONDITIONS: "The rule is: sort by colour WHEN the border is thick. Sort by shape WHEN the border is thin."
- Reward conditional articulation; block absolute language
- Frame: "Rules have conditions. 'Always' is rare. 'When X, then Y' — that's how rules really work."
- Build: conditional rule-articulation (rules have contexts)

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Start with SIMPLE articulation: "You sorted it left. Why? Because it's red? Say it: 'Red goes left.'"
- Make articulation EASY and REWARDING; one sentence
- Frame: "Just say what you did. 'I put the red one left.' That's articulation. Simple. Done."
- Build: basic rule-articulation (saying what you did)

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Require CONCRETE articulation: "Don't tell me the principle. Tell me the RULE. Simply. 'Red goes left.'"
- Block abstract language; reward simple, accurate statements
- Frame: "Simpler. Not the theory. The rule. What IS it? In plain words. Concrete."
- Build: concrete articulation (simple before complex)

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Introduce GENTLE meta-questions: "You said 'red goes left.' Why red? What makes red special here?"
- Make examination SAFE; no threat to the rule itself
- Frame: "The rule still holds. I'm just curious — why THIS rule? Thinking about it doesn't change it."
- Build: rule-examination safety (asking why isn't threatening)

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Scribe's Testimony: a monastery scriptorium where the player dictates their understanding to a scribe-companion. After performing cognitive tasks (from deterministic axis), the player must ARTICULATE what they did and why. The scribe records, questions, and reflects back.

### Interaction
- **The First Testimony (1-5):** "What did you do? Say it." Simple action-narration after a rule-following task.
- **The Named Rule (5-15):** "What IS the rule? State it." Explicit rule-articulation.
- **The Explained Classification (15-30):** "Why did you sort it that way? Explain." Classification rationale.
- **The Witnessed Change (30-50):** "The rule changed. What was it before? What is it now? How did you know?" Change-articulation.
- **The Living Scribe (50+):** Full metacognitive narration: articulate + explain + examine + adapt.

### Feedback
- Clear articulation → "Well spoken. The scribe records your testimony. The code is known."
- Rigid articulation → "Always? Are you sure? What if the border were thin? Would the rule still hold?"
- Cannot articulate → "Try. Just one sentence. What did you do? 'I put the red one…' where?"
- Abstract bypass → "Simpler. Not the theory. The rule. What IS it? Plain words."
- Refuses examination → "The rule still holds. I'm just curious. Why this rule? Safe to wonder."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Articulation complexity: action-narration → rule-statement → rationale → change-description → meta-examination
- Scaffolding: heavy prompting → light prompting → no prompting → spontaneous
- Ambiguity: clear rules → conditional rules → ambiguous rules
- Social: solo articulation → articulation for another → articulation under disagreement
- Examination depth: "what" → "why" → "what if" → "how do you know"

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The First Testimony | 1-5 | Simple action-narration |
| The Named Rule | 5-15 | Explicit rule-statement |
| The Explained Classification | 15-30 | Classification rationale |
| The Witnessed Change | 30-50 | Change-articulation |
| The Living Scribe | 50+ | Full metacognitive narration |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Action-narration prompts:** "What did you just do?" (after deterministic tasks)
- **Rule-statement prompts:** "What is the rule?" (after rule-following)
- **Rationale prompts:** "Why did you sort it that way?" (after classification)
- **Change-description prompts:** "What changed? How did you know?" (after WCST shifts)
- **Meta-examination prompts:** "Why this rule? Could it be different?" (meta-cognitive)

### Minimum pool size
- 20+ action-narration, 20+ rule-statement, 15+ rationale, 15+ change-description, 10+ meta-examination

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Absolute language → dark-addiction; cannot articulate → dark-allergy
- Abstract bypass → golden-addiction; refuses examination → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Articulation presence:** Can they state anything about their process?
- **Accuracy:** Does their articulation match their actual behaviour?
- **Specificity:** Is the articulation concrete and precise?
- **Flexibility:** Can they articulate conditional rules?
- **Examination:** Can they discuss rule-basis without distress?

### LLM rubric
- **Very High:** Core modality requires natural language evaluation. LLM assesses articulation quality, accuracy, rigidity, abstraction level, and examination willingness. Rubric-guided scoring with specific indicators per shadow quadrant.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Voice/text (primary — natural language articulation); multiple-choice (scaffolded fallback for younger players)

### Timing requirements
- Response latency noted but not pressured; articulation completeness more important than speed

### NPC/AI requirements
- Scribe-companion (asks questions, records, reflects back)
- Articulation quality assessment (accuracy, specificity, flexibility)
- Shadow-signal detection (absolute language, absence, abstraction, refusal)
- Adaptive scaffolding (adjusts prompting based on articulation capacity)

### LLM requirements
- **Very High:** Natural language understanding for articulation assessment. Must evaluate: accuracy (does statement match behaviour?), specificity (concrete vs. vague), flexibility (conditional vs. absolute), depth (surface vs. examined). Rubric-guided with specific shadow indicators.

### State persistence
- Articulation history; accuracy scores; specificity ratings; flexibility metrics; examination depth; absolute-language frequency; absence frequency; abstraction frequency; refusal frequency; drive/shadow signals; scaffolding level; checkpoint position
