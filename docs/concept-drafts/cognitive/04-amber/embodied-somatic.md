# Cognitive / Amber — Embodied-Somatic Game Concept

> **Axis:** The embodied-somatic axis probes cognitive intelligence through THE BODY THAT FOLLOWS RULES — disciplined movement, kata, drill, liturgical form. At Amber, this is: "Can your body follow a rule? Can your body reproduce a sequence? Can your body SHIFT when the form changes?"
>
> **Why this axis for Cognitive/Amber:** Concrete operational thinking lives in the body as DISCIPLINED FORM — the kata, the drill, the liturgical gesture. The body that can follow a 3-step sequence, maintain a pattern, and shift when the form changes demonstrates cognitive capacity somatically. This modality tests whether rule-following is EMBODIED, not just mental.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Form-Keeper"
- **Core mechanic:** The player must execute RULE-GOVERNED BODY SEQUENCES — follow the form, maintain the pattern, reproduce the drill, shift when the form changes. Not free movement but DISCIPLINED movement. "The form is: left-right-hold. Again. Again. Now the form changes: right-left-tap. Can your body follow?"
- **Duration:** 2-4 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; body-fatigue monitored)
- **Internal progression:** The First Form → The Held Pattern → The Living Drill → The Shifting Form → The Master Keeper

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "Here is the form. Your body must follow. Not once — CONSISTENTLY. The same sequence. The same timing. And when the form changes — your body must change too. Can your body serve the code?" The contact boundary is: "Can your body follow rules reliably?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* How does the player's body relate to rules? The Rigid Systematiser executes the form PERFECTLY but CANNOT SHIFT (body locked in old pattern). The Rule-Breaker moves chaotically (refuses disciplined form). The Premature Theorist attempts complex sequences beyond body-capacity. The System-Refuser executes well but freezes when the form changes.
- *Emergent:* Can their body follow rules flexibly — consistently but adaptably?

**Integration path:** Rewards FLEXIBLE EMBODIED RULE-FOLLOWING — consistent form WITH shift-capacity. Not rigid, not chaotic, not bypassing, not frozen.

**Successful integration:** The player's body demonstrates concrete operational mastery — disciplined, flexible, systematic movement.

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Executing body-form independently (no external rhythm/cue).
- Healthy: Maintains form independently; body follows internal rule
- Pathological: Cannot maintain form without external cue; OR: executes rigidly without body-awareness
- **Detection:** Whether embodied rule-following is self-sustained

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Body-form under ambiguity (unclear which form applies).
- Healthy: Selects appropriate body-form under ambiguity
- Pathological: Freezes when form is unclear; body stops
- **Detection:** Body-form selection under ambiguity

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining own body-form when companion moves differently.
- Healthy: Maintains own form while seeing different movement
- Pathological: Body mirrors companion (loses own form); OR: rigidly ignores
- **Detection:** Whether own body-form survives social movement

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Coordinated body-form (moving in synchrony with system/companion).
- Healthy: Synchronises body with external rhythm while maintaining accuracy
- Pathological: Cannot coordinate; either independent or fused
- **Detection:** Quality of coordinated embodied rule-following

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Body-form consistency over extended sequences.
- Healthy: Maintains form accuracy across many repetitions
- Pathological: Form degrades with repetition; OR: perseverates without noticing drift
- **Detection:** Body-form stability over time

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Growing body-form complexity (longer sequences, more elements).
- Healthy: Engages increasing complexity with appropriate effort
- Pathological: Cannot tolerate complexity; OR: attempts beyond body-capacity
- **Detection:** Response to embodied difficulty escalation

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining body-form capacity across sessions.
- Healthy: Returns to forms with maintained capacity
- Pathological: Body-form degrades between sessions; must relearn
- **Detection:** Cross-session body-form stability

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Whether body-form capacity transfers to novel movements.
- Healthy: Applies learned movement-rules to new sequences
- Pathological: Cannot transfer; each new form requires full relearning
- **Detection:** Body-form transfer quality

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Rigid Systematiser"
**How it surfaces:** Body LOCKS into form and CANNOT SHIFT. When the form changes, the body continues the old pattern. Perseverative movement. The body is the rule — it cannot change.
- **Detection signals:** Continues old form after shift; body-perseveration; high accuracy on stable forms, zero on shifted; "my body won't change"; locked movement patterns

### Dark-Allergy: "The Rule-Breaker"
**How it surfaces:** Body moves CHAOTICALLY. Refuses disciplined form. Cannot or will not execute a sequence. Movement is impulsive, random, undisciplined.
- **Detection signals:** Random movement; cannot complete sequences; abandons form mid-execution; "I don't want to do the drill"; chaotic body; no pattern

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Theorist"
**How it surfaces:** Attempts COMPLEX sequences beyond body-capacity. Tries 5-step forms when 3-step forms fail. Body reaches beyond its current mastery.
- **Detection signals:** Attempts longer sequences than capacity allows; fails at current level but requests harder; "this is too simple" (while failing); body overreaches

### Golden-Allergy: "The System-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** Executes forms well but FREEZES when form changes. Body is comfortable in the known pattern but cannot tolerate shift. The body refuses novelty.
- **Detection signals:** High accuracy on familiar forms; freeze at shift; body stops when form changes; "why did it change?"; anxiety in body at novelty; avoids new forms

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Introduce GRADUAL form-transitions (not sudden shifts but slow morphing)
- Reward body-adaptation: "The form is changing. Let your body follow. Slowly. The old form becomes the new."
- Frame: "Your body learned the old form. Good. Now it can learn the new. Same body. New form. Both yours."
- Build: flexible embodied rule-following (body can shift)

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Start with SIMPLE, BRIEF forms (2-step, immediate completion)
- Make disciplined movement feel POWERFUL: "Left-right. Done. You did the form. Your body followed the code."
- Frame: "Not a cage. A dance. Your body knows how. Left-right. Simple. Powerful. Yours."
- Build: embodied rule-following as skill (body-discipline as power)

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- LOCK complexity until current form is mastered (>90% accuracy required)
- Reward mastery at current level: "Perfect form. Your body OWNS this. Now earn the next."
- Frame: "Master this first. Your body needs this foundation. Then the next form will come naturally."
- Build: embodied mastery before advancement

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Introduce form-changes as GENTLE variations (not new forms but slight modifications)
- Reward noticing the change without requiring immediate execution
- Frame: "The form shifted slightly. See? Your body noticed. That's enough. Now try the new version."
- Build: comfort with embodied change (new forms aren't threatening)

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Form-Keeper: a training-hall where body-forms are demonstrated and the player must reproduce them. Sequences are shown; the player executes. The aesthetic: Amber-stage dojo/monastery — disciplined space, clean lines, the form demonstrated by a master-figure, the player's body-outline following.

### Interaction
- **The First Form (1-5):** 2-step body sequence. "Left-right. Again. Again." Simple repetition.
- **The Held Pattern (5-15):** 3-step sequence maintained over time. "Left-right-hold. Ten times. Consistent."
- **The Living Drill (15-30):** Multiple forms; switch between them on cue. "Form A. Form B. Form A. Which one now?"
- **The Shifting Form (30-50):** Form changes WITHOUT cue. Detect and adapt. Body-WCST.
- **The Master Keeper (50+):** Full integration: stable forms + flexible switching + shift-detection + coordination.

### Feedback
- Accurate form → "The form holds. Your body serves the code. Clean. Disciplined. Good."
- Perseveration → "The form changed. Your body is still in the old pattern. Let go. Feel the new form."
- Chaotic movement → "Simpler. Just two moves. Left-right. That's the form. Your body can do this."
- Overreaching → "Not yet. Master THIS form first. Three steps. Perfect. Then four."
- Frozen at shift → "The form changed slightly. That's okay. Your body noticed. Now follow. Gently."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Sequence length: 2-step → 3-step → 3-step under load
- Repetitions: few → many (sustained accuracy)
- Forms available: 1 → 2-3 → multiple (switching)
- Shift frequency: rare → occasional → moderate
- Coordination: solo → with rhythm → with companion

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The First Form | 1-5 | 2-step body sequence |
| The Held Pattern | 5-15 | 3-step sustained accuracy |
| The Living Drill | 15-30 | Multiple forms, cued switching |
| The Shifting Form | 30-50 | Uncued form-change detection |
| The Master Keeper | 50+ | Full embodied cognitive integration |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Simple forms:** 2-3 step body sequences (warm-up)
- **Sustained forms:** 3-step sequences requiring extended repetition
- **Switching forms:** Multiple forms with cued transitions
- **Shifting forms:** Forms that change without cue (body-WCST)
- **Coordinated forms:** Forms requiring synchrony with external rhythm

### Minimum pool size
- 20+ simple, 20+ sustained, 15+ switching, 10+ shifting, 10+ coordinated

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Body perseverates → dark-addiction; chaotic movement → dark-allergy
- Attempts beyond capacity → golden-addiction; freezes at shift → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Form accuracy:** % correct execution of body sequences
- **Sustained accuracy:** Accuracy maintenance over extended repetitions
- **Switching accuracy:** Correct form-selection on cued transitions
- **Shift-detection:** Trials-to-adapt after uncued form-change
- **Coordination:** Synchrony quality with external rhythm

### LLM rubric
- **None/Minimal:** Fully algorithmic. Body-form accuracy is objective (correct sequence/timing). No LLM needed.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Gesture/tap sequences (body-form execution); rhythm-tap (coordination); swipe-direction (movement direction)

### Timing requirements
- Sequence timing tracked (ms precision); rhythm synchrony measured; inter-form intervals standardised

### NPC/AI requirements
- Form-demonstration system (shows sequences to reproduce)
- Accuracy tracking (sequence correctness, timing precision)
- Perseveration detection (continued old-form after shift)
- Fatigue detection (accuracy degradation, body-fatigue signals)
- Adaptive difficulty (sequence length, shift frequency, coordination demand)

### LLM requirements
- **None:** Fully deterministic. Body-form accuracy is binary (correct/incorrect sequence and timing).

### State persistence
- Form accuracy history; sustained accuracy metrics; switching accuracy; shift-detection latency; coordination quality; perseveration counts; chaos indicators; overreach indicators; freeze indicators; drive/shadow signals; body-fatigue state; checkpoint position
