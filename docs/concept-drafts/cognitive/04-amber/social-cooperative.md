# Cognitive / Amber — Social-Cooperative Game Concept

> **Axis:** The social-cooperative axis probes cognitive intelligence through SHARED RULE-FOLLOWING — can the player coordinate cognitive effort with others within a rule-system? At Amber, this is: "The code requires us to work together. Can you follow the rule AND coordinate with your companion? Can you think TOGETHER within the system?"
>
> **Why this axis for Cognitive/Amber:** Amber is the stage of GROUP BELONGING. Cognitive capacity at Amber includes coordinating thought with others — shared classification, collaborative sequencing, group rule-following. This modality tests whether the player can think systematically WITH others, not just alone.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Shared Codex"
- **Core mechanic:** The player and companions must COORDINATE cognitive effort within a shared rule-system. Not solo rule-following but COLLABORATIVE rule-following. "The code requires both of you. You classify the reds. Your companion classifies the blues. Together you complete the ledger."
- **Duration:** 3-5 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** The First Partnership → The Shared Rule → The Divided Labour → The Changing Roles → The Living Order

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "The code is too large for one mind. You need your companion. They need you. Can you follow the rule TOGETHER? Can you coordinate your thinking? Can you trust their classification while maintaining your own?" The contact boundary is: "Can you think systematically with others?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* How does the player coordinate cognitively? The Rigid Systematiser enforces their classification on the companion ("Do it MY way. The rule is clear."). The Rule-Breaker cannot coordinate at all (ignores companion, ignores rules). The Premature Theorist tries to direct the whole system ("I'll manage both parts — I understand the meta-structure"). The System-Refuser coordinates well on familiar tasks but refuses when roles change.
- *Emergent:* Can they coordinate cognitive effort genuinely — contributing AND receiving, leading AND following?

**Integration path:** Rewards GENUINE COGNITIVE COORDINATION — shared rule-following where both parties contribute, neither dominates, and the system works because of collaboration.

**Successful integration:** The player can think systematically with others — the social foundation of concrete operational thought.

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining own cognitive contribution in group.
- Healthy: Contributes own classification/sequencing with confidence
- Pathological: Dominates group cognition; OR: cannot contribute independently
- **Detection:** Whether own cognitive contribution is confident and proportionate

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Contributing under disagreement (companion classifies differently).
- Healthy: Maintains own reasoning while hearing companion's; can explain difference
- Pathological: Cannot maintain under disagreement; OR: rigidly refuses to consider
- **Detection:** Cognitive independence under social pressure

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Receiving companion's cognitive contribution.
- Healthy: Accepts companion's valid classification; integrates into shared work
- Pathological: Cannot accept (rejects all input); OR: accepts everything (no discrimination)
- **Detection:** Quality of receiving others' cognitive work

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Genuine cognitive mutuality (building understanding together).
- Healthy: Shared classification emerges that neither could achieve alone
- Pathological: Cannot achieve genuine mutuality; one-directional only
- **Detection:** Whether collaborative cognition exceeds individual capacity

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Collaborative consistency over time.
- Healthy: Maintains collaborative cognitive quality across sessions
- Pathological: Collaboration quality is random; no stable partnership
- **Detection:** Collaborative consistency

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Growing collaborative complexity (harder shared tasks).
- Healthy: Can coordinate on increasingly complex shared problems
- Pathological: Cannot grow collaboratively; OR: attempts beyond shared capacity
- **Detection:** Collaborative growth quality

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining collaborative capacity over time.
- Healthy: Returns to collaborative tasks with maintained coordination
- Pathological: Collaborative capacity degrades; must rebuild partnership
- **Detection:** Cross-session collaborative stability

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Whether collaborative cognition appears spontaneously.
- Healthy: Coordinates thinking with others naturally in non-collaborative tasks
- Pathological: Collaboration exists only in explicit cooperative tasks
- **Detection:** Spontaneous cognitive coordination

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Rigid Systematiser"
**How it surfaces:** ENFORCES own classification on companion. "Do it MY way. The rule is clear. I know the correct classification." Cannot tolerate companion's different (but valid) approach. Cognitive domination.
- **Detection signals:** Overrides companion; "my way is right"; cannot accept alternative valid approaches; enforces own system; "you're doing it wrong" (when companion is correct)

### Dark-Allergy: "The Rule-Breaker"
**How it surfaces:** CANNOT COORDINATE. Ignores companion's contributions. Ignores shared rules. Acts independently and chaotically within the collaborative space.
- **Detection signals:** Ignores companion; acts solo; breaks shared rules; no coordination; "I'll do my own thing"; collaborative task fails due to non-participation

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Theorist"
**How it surfaces:** Tries to MANAGE the entire system. "I'll direct both parts. I understand the meta-structure." Takes over rather than collaborating. Cognitive imperialism disguised as helpfulness.
- **Detection signals:** Takes over companion's role; "I'll handle it all"; manages rather than collaborates; "I see the bigger picture"; cannot be an equal contributor

### Golden-Allergy: "The System-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** Coordinates well on FAMILIAR tasks but REFUSES when roles change. Cannot adapt to new collaborative arrangements. Stays in known partnership patterns.
- **Detection signals:** Good coordination on familiar tasks; failure when roles swap; "I do the reds, they do the blues" (cannot change); anxiety at role-change; refuses new collaborative structures

---

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Present tasks where companion's approach is DEMONSTRABLY VALID: "They sorted differently. Look — it works. Both ways are correct."
- Reward accepting valid alternatives; block enforcement
- Frame: "Your way works. Their way also works. The code allows both. Can you see that?"
- Build: cognitive flexibility in collaboration (multiple valid approaches)

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Present tasks that REQUIRE coordination (cannot be completed solo): "You need their piece. They need yours. Together or not at all."
- Make coordination NECESSARY and REWARDING
- Frame: "You can't do this alone. Not because you're weak — because it's DESIGNED for two. Your piece + their piece = complete."
- Build: collaborative capacity (coordination as skill)

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Present tasks where taking over FAILS (companion's role requires their specific input): "You can't do their part. Only they can. Trust their contribution."
- Block takeover; reward equal contribution
- Frame: "Not managing. Contributing. Your part. Their part. Equal. The system needs BOTH, not one doing everything."
- Build: equal collaboration (contributor not manager)

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Introduce SLIGHT role-changes (familiar task, slightly different division): "Same task. But today you do the blues. They do the reds. Same rules. Different roles."
- Make role-change GENTLE and SAFE
- Frame: "Same code. Same partnership. Just different seats. You can do this. The rules haven't changed — just who does what."
- Build: collaborative flexibility (roles can change safely)

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Shared Codex: a collaborative classification/sequencing space. Player and companion each have a role within a shared rule-system. Items flow in; each person classifies/sequences their portion; the shared ledger fills. The aesthetic: Amber-stage scriptorium — two scribes at adjacent desks, shared codex between them, items flowing to be classified.

### Interaction
- **The First Partnership (1-5):** Simple shared task. "You sort left. They sort right. Together the ledger fills."
- **The Shared Rule (5-15):** Same rule, different items. Coordinate timing and accuracy.
- **The Divided Labour (15-30):** Different rules for different roles. "You sort by colour. They sort by shape. Both needed."
- **The Changing Roles (30-50):** Roles swap. Adapt to new position within same system.
- **The Living Order (50+):** Full collaborative integration: shared rules + divided labour + role-flexibility + mutual support.

### Feedback
- Good coordination → "The codex fills. Two minds, one system. Well coordinated."
- Enforcement → "They're doing it correctly — just differently. Both valid. Can you see their way works too?"
- Non-coordination → "The codex needs both of you. Your piece is missing. The system is incomplete without you."
- Takeover → "That's their role. Trust them. Do YOUR part. The system needs two contributors, not one manager."
- Role-rigidity → "Same system. New seat. You can do this. The rules are the same — just your position changed."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Task complexity: simple shared sorting → divided labour → role-switching
- Coordination demand: independent parallel → interdependent → sequential handoff
- Disagreement: harmonious → companion uses different (valid) approach → companion makes errors
- Role-change frequency: stable → occasional → frequent
- Group size: dyad → triad → small group

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The First Partnership | 1-5 | Simple parallel shared task |
| The Shared Rule | 5-15 | Coordinated timing and accuracy |
| The Divided Labour | 15-30 | Different roles, same system |
| The Changing Roles | 30-50 | Role-swapping and adaptation |
| The Living Order | 50+ | Full collaborative cognitive integration |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Parallel tasks:** Same rule, different items (basic coordination)
- **Divided tasks:** Different rules for different roles (specialisation)
- **Sequential tasks:** One person's output feeds another's input (handoff)
- **Role-swap tasks:** Familiar tasks with swapped positions
- **Disagreement tasks:** Companion uses different valid approach

### Minimum pool size
- 20+ parallel, 15+ divided, 15+ sequential, 10+ role-swap, 10+ disagreement

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Enforces own way → dark-addiction; cannot coordinate → dark-allergy
- Takes over all roles → golden-addiction; refuses role-change → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Coordination quality:** How well do player and companion work together?
- **Independence:** Does the player maintain own contribution without dominating?
- **Reception:** Can they accept companion's valid contributions?
- **Role-flexibility:** Can they adapt when roles change?
- **Collaborative efficiency:** Does the shared system work better than solo?

### LLM rubric
- **Low:** Core coordination tracking algorithmic (timing, accuracy, role-adherence). LLM for nuanced dominance detection and genuine-vs-performative collaboration assessment.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Tap/drag (classify/sequence own items); signal (coordinate with companion); confirm (accept shared result)

### Timing requirements
- Coordination timing tracked; individual contribution rate; shared completion time

### NPC/AI requirements
- Companion system (NPC with own cognitive style and valid approaches)
- Shared-task system (items requiring collaborative classification/sequencing)
- Coordination tracking (timing, accuracy, role-adherence)
- Dominance detection (one party overriding the other)
- Role-swap system (changing positions within same task)

### LLM requirements
- **Low:** Companion behaviour generation; nuanced collaboration assessment. Core coordination metrics algorithmic.

### State persistence
- Coordination history; independence scores; reception quality; role-flexibility metrics; collaborative efficiency; dominance indicators; non-participation indicators; takeover indicators; rigidity indicators; drive/shadow signals; fatigue state; checkpoint position
