# Cognitive / Red — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** The strategic-planning axis probes cognitive capacity through MULTI-STEP PLANNING — sequencing actions toward a goal, managing resources over time, and maintaining a plan under changing conditions. This modality accesses the EXECUTIVE dimension of cognition: not "can you react" but "can you PLAN and EXECUTE."
>
> **Why this axis for Cognitive/Red:** At Red, planning is concrete, short-horizon, and power-directed. "I will take that hill in 2 moves." The player can sequence 2-3 actions toward a visible goal but cannot yet plan abstractly or maintain long-term strategies. This axis isolates the PLANNING component of executive function — Tower of London, goal-maintenance, and sequential execution.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Siege-Maker"
- **Core mechanic:** The player plans and executes multi-step siege operations — positioning forces, sequencing attacks, managing limited resources — where success requires holding a plan in mind and executing it step-by-step against concrete obstacles.
- **Duration:** 4-10 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints)
- **Internal progression:** 2-move plans → 3-move plans → Resource-constrained plans → Adaptive plans → Contested plans

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** The game presents goals that CANNOT be achieved in one move — they require sequential thinking. The contact boundary is: "Can you hold a 2-step plan in mind and execute it without losing the thread?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* The player's relationship to sequential thinking surfaces. Do they over-plan (dark-addiction)? Refuse to plan and just act (dark-allergy)? Attempt plans beyond their capacity (golden-addiction)? Refuse to engage with multi-step challenges (golden-allergy)?
- *Emergent:* The pull toward longer planning horizons — can they hold one more step? Can they maintain the plan under pressure?

**Integration path:** The game rewards EXECUTED plans (not just conceived ones). A brilliant plan that falls apart in execution scores lower than a simple plan executed cleanly. This teaches: planning is only valuable when it becomes action.

**Successful integration:** The player conceives plans appropriate to their capacity (2-3 steps at Red), holds them in working memory during execution, and adapts when conditions change — all without over-planning or under-planning.

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** The game offers "advisor" suggestions for plans. The player can accept, modify, or reject.
- Healthy: Evaluates suggestions on merit; accepts good ones, rejects bad ones
- Pathological: Rejects ALL suggestions; "My plan or no plan"; cannot integrate others' strategic input
- **Detection:** Suggestion rejection rate independent of suggestion quality; performance after rejection

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Open-ended planning challenges with no single correct solution — the player must CREATE a plan.
- Healthy: Generates plans with confidence; iterates when first attempt fails
- Pathological: Cannot begin without a template; "What am I supposed to do?"; paralysis before open problems
- **Detection:** Time-to-first-action on open problems; quality of self-generated plans; iteration willingness

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Shared planning — player and NPC ally must coordinate a joint plan.
- Healthy: Integrates ally's constraints into own plan; creates plans that work for both
- Pathological: Abandons own plan entirely to follow ally; "Just tell me what to do"
- **Detection:** Whether joint plans reflect player's strategic thinking or pure compliance

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Teaching planning — player must help an NPC recruit create THEIR plan (not just give them one).
- Healthy: Scaffolds the recruit's thinking; asks questions rather than dictating
- Pathological: Dictates the plan; cannot let others think; "Just do what I say"
- **Detection:** Whether teaching involves genuine scaffolding or just instruction-giving

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** The game allows replaying mastered challenges or advancing to harder planning problems.
- Healthy: Enjoys executing clean plans at current level; advances when ready
- Pathological: Cannot rest in 2-step mastery; demands 4-step problems immediately; treats current capacity as failure
- **Detection:** Satisfaction signals at current level; advancement pressure independent of readiness

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Optional "stretch" planning problems that require one more step than current stable capacity.
- Healthy: Attempts stretch problems with curiosity; learns from failure
- Pathological: Attempts stretch problems compulsively; claims success on failed plans; "I almost had it" repeatedly
- **Detection:** Gap between attempted plan-length and successfully executed plan-length

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Foundation planning — simple 1-step problems that maintain basic sequencing.
- Healthy: Executes simple plans with full attention and precision
- Pathological: Dismisses simple plans; executes carelessly; "This is beneath me"
- **Detection:** Execution quality on below-level problems; attention signals on "easy" challenges

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Transfer — using planning skills in unstructured game contexts (not just planning puzzles).
- Healthy: Plans appear in free-play behaviour; the player sequences actions toward goals naturally
- Pathological: Planning exists only in puzzle contexts; free-play is reactive/impulsive
- **Detection:** Sequential behaviour quality in non-puzzle contexts; goal-directed action in free-play

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Compulsive Strategist"
**How it surfaces:** Present a 2-step problem and observe planning time. The compulsive strategist plans for 60 seconds on a problem that requires 5 seconds of thought. They consider every possible failure mode before acting.
- **Detection signals:** Planning time > 4× execution time; plan revision without new information; inability to commit to a plan

### Dark-Allergy: "The Anti-Intellectual Warrior"
**How it surfaces:** Present a problem that clearly requires 2+ steps. The allergy manifests as attempting to solve it in one move, failing, and trying another single move rather than planning.
- **Detection signals:** Single-move attempts on multi-step problems; no pause between attempts; trial-and-error without learning

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Abstractionist"
**How it surfaces:** Present a 2-step problem and observe whether the player attempts a 4-step "elegant" solution instead of the simple correct one.
- **Detection signals:** Over-complex plans for simple problems; "elegant" solutions that fail; inability to choose the simple path

### Golden-Allergy: "The Growth-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** Gradually increase plan-length requirements. The refusal manifests as disengagement when 3-step plans are required.
- **Detection signals:** Performance cliff at specific plan-length; avoidance of longer challenges; preference for repeating mastered 2-step problems

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Game introduces "speed siege" rounds: plan and execute in limited time (forces commitment)
- Rewards EXECUTION over PLANNING (a simple plan executed > a complex plan conceived)
- Frames decisiveness: "The siege-maker who acts takes the wall; the one who plans watches it stand"
- Shows that imperfect plans executed quickly often outperform perfect plans delayed

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Game introduces "scout phase" before action: brief mandatory planning window (3 seconds)
- Shows consequences of unplanned action (visible failure) alongside planned action (visible success)
- Frames planning as "reading the battlefield" — a warrior skill, not a scholar's weakness
- Starts with 1-step plans (barely planning) and extends to 2-step

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Game rewards MINIMUM-MOVE solutions (elegance = simplicity, not complexity)
- Scoring penalises unnecessary steps; the shortest correct plan scores highest
- Pairs with NPC who models efficient planning: "Two moves. Clean. Done."
- Makes simplicity more prestigious than complexity

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Game introduces 3-step problems as OPTIONAL with bonus rewards
- Frames longer plans as "bigger prizes" — more steps = more territory gained
- Provides scaffolding: "What if you did THIS first, then...?"
- Celebrates any engagement with longer planning, however tentative

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The player commands a siege operation from a war-table. A miniature fortress is visible with walls, gates, towers, and defenders. The player has limited siege units (battering rams, ladders, catapults, sappers) that must be deployed in sequence. The aesthetic: Red-stage war-room, brass tokens on a stone map, firelight.

### Interaction
- **2-move sieges:** "Break the gate (ram), then storm the courtyard (infantry)." Two actions in correct sequence.
- **3-move sieges:** "Suppress the archers (catapult), scale the wall (ladders), open the gate from inside (sappers)."
- **Resource-constrained:** "You have 2 rams and 1 catapult. The fortress has 3 gates. Which do you break?"
- **Adaptive plans:** "The enemy moves their archers. Your plan must change. What now?"
- **Contested plans:** "The enemy has a plan too. Anticipate and counter."

### Feedback
- Plan executed → animated siege sequence shows results
- Correct sequence → walls fall, territory gained, power-resonant victory
- Incorrect sequence → visible failure (ram hits reinforced wall, troops exposed)
- Optimal plan → bonus: "The siege-maker's genius!" (minimum moves)
- The game always shows WHY a plan failed (which step was wrong)

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Plan length: 2 → 3 → 4 steps
- Constraints: unlimited resources → limited → scarce
- Adaptivity: static fortress → moving defenders → counter-planning enemy
- Time pressure: unlimited → generous → moderate

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| 2-Move Plans | 1-5 | Simple sequences, clear goals, unlimited resources |
| 3-Move Plans | 5-15 | Longer sequences, some resource constraints |
| Resource-Constrained | 15-30 | Limited units, must choose which goals to pursue |
| Adaptive Plans | 30-50 | Conditions change mid-execution, must re-plan |
| Contested Plans | 50+ | Adversarial planning, anticipation, counter-strategy |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Fortress configurations:** Wall/gate/tower arrangements (procedurally generated from templates)
- **Unit sets:** Available siege units with different capabilities
- **Defender configurations:** Enemy placement and behaviour patterns
- **Constraint sets:** Resource limitations that force prioritisation
- **Adaptation triggers:** Mid-plan changes that require re-planning

### Minimum pool size
- 30+ fortress configurations (2-move difficulty)
- 25+ fortress configurations (3-move difficulty)
- 20+ resource-constrained scenarios
- 15+ adaptive scenarios (with mid-plan changes)
- 10+ contested scenarios (with adversarial planning)

### Generation rules
- Fortresses generated from modular components (walls, gates, towers, moats)
- Difficulty = minimum optimal plan length (computed algorithmically)
- All problems must have at least one solution within the player's resource set
- Adaptive scenarios: defender movements follow predictable (but not obvious) patterns
- Contested scenarios: enemy plans are 1 step shorter than player's capacity (beatable)

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Advisor acceptance/rejection → Agency probing
- Joint planning quality → Communion probing
- Advancement rate and satisfaction at current level → Eros probing
- Foundation drill quality → Agape probing
- Excessive planning time → dark-addiction signal
- Single-move attempts on multi-step problems → dark-allergy signal
- Over-complex plans for simple problems → golden-addiction signal
- Disengagement at longer plan requirements → golden-allergy signal

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Plan length:** Maximum plan length successfully executed (2, 3, 4+ steps)
- **Optimality:** Ratio of player's moves to minimum possible moves
- **Consistency:** Success rate at current plan-length (stability)
- **Adaptation:** Quality of re-planning when conditions change
- **Resource efficiency:** How well limited resources are allocated

### Drive-health scoring
Per `scoring.md` §3: Behavioural signals from planning engagement — advisor usage, joint planning quality, advancement patterns, foundation engagement, transfer to free-play.

### Shadow-state scoring
Per `shadow-diagnostics.md` §3: Planning-time ratios, single-move attempts, over-complexity, disengagement patterns.

### Integration scoring
- Plan length increasing over sessions
- Optimality improving (fewer wasted moves)
- Adaptation quality improving (faster re-planning)
- Transfer: sequential goal-directed behaviour appearing in other game contexts

### LLM rubric
- **Medium:** Generates fortress configurations, narrates siege outcomes, adapts difficulty narrative
- Evaluates plan quality in adaptive/contested scenarios
- Generates contextual framing (war-table dialogue)
- Not required for core plan-length scoring (algorithmic)

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Tap-to-select (choose units and targets)
- Drag-and-drop (position units on map)
- Sequence input (order actions in a queue before execution)
- Swipe (scroll map, adjust view)

### Timing requirements
- Planning time tracked (diagnostic, not punishing at early stages)
- Execution is turn-based (not real-time) — no millisecond precision needed
- Later stages introduce time pressure on re-planning (adaptive scenarios)

### NPC/AI requirements
- Advisor NPC: suggests plans, provides scaffolding
- Ally NPC: coordinates joint plans, has own constraints
- Recruit NPC: receives teaching, asks questions about planning
- Enemy AI: executes counter-plans in contested scenarios (simple rule-based at Red)

### LLM requirements
- **Medium:** Scenario generation, outcome narration, adaptive difficulty
- Generates fortress configurations within difficulty constraints
- Narrates siege outcomes in Red-stage language
- Adapts enemy behaviour descriptions

### State persistence
- Maximum successful plan-length (running estimate)
- Optimality ratio (running average)
- Resource efficiency (running average)
- Planning-time patterns (for shadow detection)
- Drive-health signals from planning behaviour
- Shadow signals from engagement patterns
- Checkpoint position and phase
- Ongoing siege campaign state (territory gained, resources accumulated)
