# Cognitive / Red — Language-Reflective Game Concept

> **Axis:** The language-reflective axis probes cognitive capacity through verbal/written expression — articulating strategy, explaining reasoning, naming patterns. This modality accesses the METACOGNITIVE dimension of cognition: not just "can you do it" but "can you TALK about doing it."
>
> **Why this axis for Cognitive/Red:** At Red, language is concrete, declarative, and power-serving. "I did X because Y." The player can articulate 2-step reasoning but not abstract principles. Language here reveals whether cognitive capacity is genuinely integrated (can explain) or merely performed (can do but not articulate).

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The War-Tongue"
- **Core mechanic:** The player articulates battle strategies, explains tactical decisions, and names cognitive patterns through short verbal/text prompts — evaluated by LLM against a developmental rubric for concrete-operational egocentric reasoning.
- **Duration:** 3-6 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints)
- **Internal progression:** Naming → Explaining → Predicting → Teaching → Commanding

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** The game asks the player to PUT INTO WORDS their cognitive process. This is harder than doing — it requires metacognitive access to one's own thinking. At Red, the contact boundary is: "Can you name what you're doing and why?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* The player's relationship to self-knowledge surfaces. Can they access their own reasoning? Do they over-explain (dark-addiction)? Refuse to explain (dark-allergy)? Perform sophistication (golden-addiction)? Refuse depth (golden-allergy)?
- *Emergent:* The pull toward metacognitive capacity — can they tolerate the gap between doing and knowing-that-they-do?

**Integration path:** The game rewards genuine articulation (concrete, honest, specific) over performance (abstract, vague, impressive-sounding). The LLM rubric distinguishes between authentic Red-stage reasoning ("I hit the blue one because it was next") and performed higher-stage reasoning ("I employed a systematic approach to optimise outcomes").

**Successful integration:** The player can articulate their cognitive strategies in concrete, honest terms — neither over-intellectualising nor refusing reflection.

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** The game asks "How did YOU figure that out?" — probing sovereign ownership of cognitive process.
- Healthy: "I noticed the pattern and used it" — owns their thinking
- Pathological: "I'm the BEST at this, nobody else could see it" — cognitive dominance through language
- **Detection:** Self-aggrandising language; comparative framing; inability to acknowledge difficulty

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** The game presents novel problems and asks "What would you try?" BEFORE attempting.
- Healthy: Articulates a plan with confidence, tolerates uncertainty in the plan
- Pathological: Cannot articulate without guarantee; "I don't know" as refusal rather than honest uncertainty
- **Detection:** Willingness to articulate uncertain plans; quality of "I don't know" (curious vs. defeated)

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** The game shows another player's (NPC) strategy and asks "What do you think of their approach?"
- Healthy: Evaluates genuinely — sees strengths and weaknesses, maintains own perspective
- Pathological: Adopts the other's framing entirely; "Yeah, that's what I would do too" without genuine engagement
- **Detection:** Whether evaluation maintains independent perspective or collapses into agreement

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Collaborative explanation tasks — "Explain this to your training partner so they can do it."
- Healthy: Genuine teaching — adapts explanation to partner's level, checks understanding
- Pathological: Performs teaching without genuine communication; uses jargon to seem smart rather than to help
- **Detection:** Quality of explanation (concrete, adapted) vs. performance (abstract, self-serving)

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** The game asks "What are you good at now?" — probing relationship to current mastery.
- Healthy: Can name current capacities with satisfaction; "I can track two things at once"
- Pathological: Cannot rest in naming current capacity — immediately pivots to what they CAN'T do yet
- **Detection:** Whether self-assessment includes satisfaction or only deficit-focus

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** The game asks "What do you think comes next? What would be harder?" — probing aspiration.
- Healthy: Genuine curiosity about the next level; "Maybe tracking three things?"
- Pathological: Claims to already be there; "Oh I can already do that" without demonstration
- **Detection:** Whether aspiration is grounded (acknowledges gap) or performative (denies gap)

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** The game asks "Explain the basics to a beginner" — probing relationship to foundations.
- Healthy: Explains basics with care and completeness; honours the foundation
- Pathological: Dismisses basics; "That's obvious, why would I explain that?"
- **Detection:** Quality and care of basic explanations; contempt signals

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** The game asks "When did you use this skill outside of training?" — probing transfer.
- Healthy: Can name concrete instances of cognitive capacity in other contexts
- Pathological: Cannot connect training to life; "I just do it here"
- **Detection:** Specificity and authenticity of transfer examples

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Compulsive Strategist"
**How it surfaces:** When asked to explain a simple decision, the player produces EXCESSIVE explanation — 10 sentences where 2 would suffice. Over-analysis through language.
- **Detection signals:** Response length disproportionate to task complexity; hedging/qualifying language; inability to give a simple answer

### Dark-Allergy: "The Anti-Intellectual Warrior"
**How it surfaces:** When asked to explain reasoning, the player refuses or gives minimal non-answers. "I just did it." "I don't know, it worked."
- **Detection signals:** Response length < minimum threshold; refusal language; frustration at being asked to reflect

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Abstractionist"
**How it surfaces:** When asked to explain concrete reasoning, the player uses abstract/formal language inappropriate to their actual capacity. "I employed systematic analysis" when they actually just guessed.
- **Detection signals:** Abstract vocabulary without concrete grounding; mismatch between language sophistication and task performance; inability to give concrete examples

### Golden-Allergy: "The Growth-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** When asked "What would be harder?" or "What comes next?", the player deflects. "I don't want to think about that." "This is fine."
- **Detection signals:** Deflection from growth-oriented questions; comfort-language; refusal to engage with future capacity

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Game presents "one-word answer" challenges — "Name this in ONE word"
- Rewards brevity and precision over exhaustiveness
- Frames conciseness as power: "The warrior who speaks few words is feared most"
- Gradually trains proportional response (match explanation to complexity)

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Game embeds reflection in action: "As you fight, name your moves" (real-time narration)
- Starts with single-word naming ("Block!" "Strike!") and extends to phrases
- Frames articulation as battle-cry — language as weapon, not weakness
- Rewards any genuine reflection, however brief

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Game requires CONCRETE examples for every abstract claim
- "You said 'systematic' — show me exactly what you mean with THIS problem"
- Pairs with NPC who models genuine concrete articulation (not impressive abstraction)
- Makes concrete mastery more rewarding than abstract performance

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Game presents "what if" questions as play, not demand: "Just for fun — what MIGHT be harder?"
- Frames growth-thinking as imagination/adventure, not obligation
- Rewards any engagement with future capacity, however tentative
- NPC models curiosity about growth: "I wonder what's beyond this..."

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The player enters the War-Tongue chamber — a space where warriors articulate their battle knowledge. A "Tongue-Master" NPC (scarred veteran who speaks precisely) presents prompts. The aesthetic is Red-stage: weapon-inscribed walls, battle-maps, trophies of past victories.

### Interaction
- **Naming prompts:** "What did you just do?" → player types/speaks 1-3 words
- **Explanation prompts:** "Why did you choose that?" → player types/speaks 1-3 sentences
- **Prediction prompts:** "What will happen if...?" → player articulates expected outcome
- **Teaching prompts:** "Tell the recruit how to do this" → player explains to NPC
- **Command prompts:** "Order your squad" → player gives tactical instructions

### Feedback
- LLM evaluates against rubric: concrete? honest? proportional? Red-appropriate?
- Genuine concrete reasoning → Tongue-Master nods, progress advances
- Over-abstract performance → Tongue-Master: "Speak plainly, warrior. What did you ACTUALLY do?"
- Refusal/minimal → Tongue-Master: "Even the greatest warrior names their blade."
- The feedback is always in-character, never clinical

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Prompt complexity increases: naming → explaining → predicting → teaching → commanding
- Expected response sophistication increases (but always within concrete-operational bounds)
- LLM rubric adjusts: early checkpoints accept any genuine response; later checkpoints require specificity

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Naming | 1-5 | Single-word/phrase responses to "what did you do?" |
| Explaining | 5-15 | 1-3 sentence explanations of "why?" |
| Predicting | 15-30 | Articulating expected outcomes before acting |
| Teaching | 30-50 | Explaining to others (requires perspective-taking) |
| Commanding | 50+ | Tactical articulation under time pressure |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Naming prompts:** Post-action reflection ("Name what you just did") — tied to deterministic task outcomes
- **Explanation prompts:** Causal reasoning ("Why did that work?") — tied to strategy choices
- **Prediction prompts:** Hypothetical reasoning ("What happens if you do X?") — tied to upcoming challenges
- **Teaching prompts:** Perspective-taking ("Explain to someone who doesn't know") — tied to mastered content
- **Command prompts:** Tactical articulation ("Tell your squad what to do") — tied to planning tasks

### Minimum pool size
- 30+ naming prompts (tied to different task types and outcomes)
- 30+ explanation prompts (tied to different strategy contexts)
- 20+ prediction prompts (tied to different challenge types)
- 20+ teaching prompts (tied to different skill levels)
- 20+ command prompts (tied to different tactical scenarios)

### Generation rules
- LLM generates prompts contextually based on what the player just did
- Prompts are always tied to ACTUAL gameplay (not abstract hypotheticals)
- Difficulty scales with: response length expected, specificity required, time pressure
- Shadow-probing prompts are interspersed naturally (not flagged as special)

### Drive/shadow mapping
- "How did YOU do it?" prompts → Agency probing
- "What do you think of their approach?" prompts → Communion probing
- "What are you good at?" prompts → Eros dark probing
- "What comes next?" prompts → Eros golden probing
- "Explain the basics" prompts → Agape probing
- Over-long responses → dark-addiction signal
- Minimal/refusal responses → dark-allergy signal
- Abstract/performative responses → golden-addiction signal
- Deflection from growth questions → golden-allergy signal

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Concrete reasoning quality:** Can the player articulate cause-effect in concrete terms?
- **Metacognitive access:** Can they name what they're doing while doing it?
- **Proportionality:** Is explanation length proportional to task complexity?
- **Specificity:** Do they use concrete examples or vague abstractions?

### Drive-health scoring
Per `scoring.md` §3: LLM evaluates language patterns against drive-health rubric. Each response is scored for agency/communion/eros/agape signals in both domains.

### Shadow-state scoring
Per `shadow-diagnostics.md` §3: LLM identifies shadow signals in language patterns (over-explanation, refusal, abstraction, deflection).

### Integration scoring
- Progression from naming → explaining → predicting → teaching → commanding
- Quality of articulation improving over sessions (more concrete, more specific, more proportional)
- Transfer: player's language in OTHER game modalities reflects metacognitive growth

### LLM rubric (PRIMARY for this modality)
The LLM evaluates each response against:
1. **Developmental appropriateness:** Is this Red-stage reasoning? (concrete, egocentric, power-framed)
2. **Authenticity:** Does the language match actual performance? (not performing higher)
3. **Proportionality:** Is the response appropriate in length/depth for the prompt?
4. **Drive signals:** Which drives are expressed? Healthy or pathological?
5. **Shadow signals:** Is a shadow quadrant active in this response?

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Text input (typed responses, 1-50 words typical)
- Optional: voice input with speech-to-text (for accessibility and naturalness)
- Choice selection (for scaffolded prompts at early checkpoints)

### Timing requirements
- Response time tracked (but not pressured at early stages)
- Later stages introduce time pressure on command prompts
- No millisecond precision needed (unlike deterministic axis)

### NPC/AI requirements
- Tongue-Master NPC: LLM-driven dialogue, responds to player's actual words
- Training Partner NPC: provides comparison strategies for communion probing
- Recruit NPC: receives player's teaching, asks follow-up questions

### LLM requirements
- **High:** This modality is LLM-primary. Every response is evaluated by LLM against rubric.
- Prompt generation: contextual, tied to recent gameplay
- Response evaluation: developmental rubric scoring (concrete vs. abstract, authentic vs. performative)
- Dialogue generation: NPC responses adapted to player's language level
- Shadow detection: pattern recognition across sessions

### State persistence
- Response history (last 50 responses for pattern analysis)
- Language sophistication trajectory (is articulation improving?)
- Drive-health signals from language (running estimates)
- Shadow signals from language patterns (running estimates)
- Checkpoint position and phase
