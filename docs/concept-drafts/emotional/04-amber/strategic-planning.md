# Emotional / Amber — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** Planning emotional engagement — how the player structures role-emotional obligations across time, sequences emotional responses, and manages the emotional demands of their role.
> **Why this axis for this module:** Role-affect at Amber requires emotional planning — knowing when to feel what, how to sequence emotional responses, and how to manage the emotional demands of sustained role-membership. This modality reveals whether the player can organise their emotional life within the code.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Emotional Steward"
- **Core mechanic:** The player plans and manages the emotional demands of their role across a multi-step scenario. Each step requires a specific role-emotion; the player must sequence, sustain, and transition between role-affects appropriately. The game tests whether the player can steward their emotional life within the code.
- **Duration:** 5-10 minutes per session
- **Internal progression:** The Emotional Brief → The Feeling-Map → The Sequence → The Unexpected Feeling → The Steward's Mastery

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**What this modality uniquely presents:** The PLANNING dimension of role-affect — how the player structures emotional obligations across time. Unlike deterministic (raw affect accuracy), this modality measures whether the player can ORGANISE their role-emotional life effectively without using planning as a substitute for genuine feeling.

**How this differs from other modalities:** Unlike scenario-choice (single-decision quality), this modality measures multi-step emotional planning. Unlike embodied-somatic (physical feeling), this modality measures cognitive structuring of emotional obligations.

**What it uniquely surfaces:** Golden-addiction (elaborate emotional planning without underlying role-affect), dark-allergy (cannot plan emotional engagement; impulsive), and the quality of the player's relationship to emotional obligation-structure.

**Successful integration:** The player plans emotional engagement effectively within the code — sequencing role-emotions clearly, transitioning appropriately, adapting when unexpected feelings arise — without using planning to bypass genuine feeling.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Emotional Steward: a planning interface where the player receives an emotional brief for an upcoming role-event and must plan their emotional engagement. The event has multiple phases, each requiring specific role-emotions. Aesthetic: Amber-stage preparation — the knight preparing for ceremony, the guardian preparing for watch, the emotional texture of role-readiness.

### Interaction
- **The Emotional Brief (1-5):** Simple 3-phase event. "The ceremony requires: solemnity during the oath, warmth during the welcome, vigilance during the watch." Tests basic role-emotion sequencing.
- **The Feeling-Map (5-15):** Player plans the emotional sequence before the event. "Map your feelings. What comes first? What transitions to what?" Tests emotional planning within the code.
- **The Sequence (15-30):** Execute the planned emotional sequence. Tests whether the emotional plan holds under execution pressure.
- **The Unexpected Feeling (30-50):** Mid-event unexpected emotion. "Grief arises during the ceremony. Your plan must adapt. But the role's emotional demands remain." Tests adaptive emotional commitment.
- **The Steward's Mastery (50+):** Full integration: planning + sequencing + adaptation + sustained role-affect simultaneously.

### Feedback
- Effective emotional planning → "The feelings are mapped. Each role-emotion is clear. The steward serves the code."
- Over-planning → "The emotional plan is elaborate. But the first feeling is simple. Can you feel it without the full plan?"
- No planning → "The event begins without emotional preparation. The role-demands are real. But without structure, how will you hold them?"
- Plan-as-bypass → "You planned the optimal emotional sequence. But the role gave you a feeling, not an optimisation problem. What does the role require?"
- Adaptive emotional commitment → "The unexpected feeling arrived. The plan changed. The role-affect held. This is mastery."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Event length: 3 phases → 5 phases → 8 phases
- Emotional complexity: simple sequence → branching → conditional
- Unexpected feeling frequency: none → occasional → frequent
- Role-emotion hierarchy: single role → multiple roles → role-conflict

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The Emotional Brief | 1-5 | Simple 3-phase role-emotion sequence |
| The Feeling-Map | 5-15 | Emotional planning within the code |
| The Sequence | 15-30 | Emotional plan execution under pressure |
| The Unexpected Feeling | 30-50 | Adaptive emotional commitment |
| The Steward's Mastery | 50+ | Full emotional planning integration |

## 4. Item Pool

- **Emotional briefs:** Multi-phase role-emotion sequences of varying complexity — 25+
- **Feeling-map tasks:** Role-emotion sequencing and transition planning — 20+
- **Sequence execution tasks:** Emotional plan execution under varying pressure — 20+
- **Unexpected feeling scenarios:** Mid-event emotional disruptions — 15+
- **Role-conflict tasks:** Managing competing role-emotional demands — 10+

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input types:** Tap (emotion selection); drag (emotion sequencing); hold (feeling confirmation); swipe (plan navigation)
- **Timing:** Planning time tracked; execution latency measured; transition quality recorded; adaptation speed measured
- **NPC/AI:** Adaptive event complexity; unexpected-feeling generation; emotional plan quality assessment; shadow-signal accumulator
- **LLM:** Event narration; unexpected feeling generation; emotional plan quality assessment for complex cases
- **State persistence:** Full event history; emotional planning patterns; execution quality; adaptation responses; cross-session emotional continuity; drive/shadow signal accumulation; checkpoint position
