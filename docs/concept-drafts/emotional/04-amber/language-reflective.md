# Emotional / Amber — Language-Reflective Game Concept

> **Axis:** Verbal articulation of role-emotions — how the player talks about loyalty, guilt, duty-feeling, and group-affect reveals the structure of their role-congruent emotional life.
> **Why this axis for this module:** The language of role-emotion is diagnostic. "I must feel this" (duty-martyr), "I feel what I want" (emotional deserter), "I hold space for your experience" (premature empath), and "I just feel what the code requires" (feeling-refuser) are structurally distinct and reveal which shadow is active.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Confessor's Chamber"
- **Core mechanic:** The player speaks with an NPC confessor about their emotional life within their role. An LLM-powered confessor analyses the language of role-emotion, surfacing shadow patterns through what the player says and how they say it.
- **Duration:** 3-8 minutes per session
- **Internal progression:** Naming the Feeling → Explaining the Guilt → The Loyalty Examined → The Code's Emotional Demands → The Confession Complete

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**What this modality uniquely presents:** The verbal dimension of role-affect — how the player conceptualises, justifies, and narrates their emotional obligations. Language reveals whether emotion is experienced as sovereign feeling, role-compulsion, performed sophistication, or unexaminable code-demand.

**How this differs from other modalities:** Unlike deterministic (which measures raw affect accuracy), this modality measures the METACOGNITIVE relationship to role-emotion. A player who feels role-emotions accurately (deterministic) but cannot articulate them (language-reflective) has implicit capacity without metacognition. A player who articulates beautifully but cannot feel (deterministic) has golden-addiction.

**What it uniquely surfaces:** Golden-addiction (sophisticated emotional language without genuine role-affect), feeling-refusal (refuses to examine emotional demands verbally), and the quality of the player's relationship to role-emotion as a concept.

**Successful integration:** The player can name their role-emotions clearly, explain why they feel them, acknowledge when they've felt inappropriately, and engage with questions about emotional demands without anxiety or dismissal.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Confessor's Chamber: a dialogue interface where the player speaks with a wise NPC confessor about their emotional life. The confessor asks structured questions; the player responds in free text. The LLM analyses responses for shadow patterns and drive-health signals.

### Interaction
- **Naming the Feeling (1-5):** "What do you feel in your role right now?" Assesses: can they name role-congruent emotions? (Dark-allergy: "I feel what I feel." Dark-addiction: overwhelming guilt-list.)
- **Explaining the Guilt (5-15):** "Tell me about a time you felt guilty. What had you done?" Assesses: is the guilt genuine (code-violation) or pathological (constant)? (Duty-martyr: guilt for everything. Emotional deserter: no guilt, only shame.)
- **The Loyalty Examined (15-30):** "What does loyalty feel like to you? How do you know when you feel it?" Assesses: is loyalty genuinely felt or performed? (Premature empath: sophisticated language without felt-sense.)
- **The Code's Emotional Demands (30-50):** "Does your role ever demand you feel something you don't naturally feel? How do you respond?" Assesses: can they examine emotional demands? (Feeling-refuser: refuses. Duty-martyr: "I must feel it regardless.")
- **The Confession Complete (50+):** Full integration: names, explains guilt, examines loyalty, engages emotional demands — all with healthy drive expression.

### Feedback
- Genuine role-affect language → "You feel what the role asks. And you know why. The confessor hears truth."
- Compelled affect language → "You say you must feel this. But feeling cannot be commanded. The confessor asks: do you choose this feeling?"
- Performed sophistication → "You speak of holding space. But do you feel the loyalty? The confessor asks for the feeling, not the words."
- Examination-refusal → "The code demands this feeling. Good. The confessor asks only: do you know why? Not to change it — to understand it."
- Proportional guilt → "You felt guilty when you broke your word. That is right. The confessor notes: the guilt was proportional."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Prompt depth: concrete ("what do you feel?") → abstract ("what does loyalty mean to you?")
- Guilt-examination: minor violation → major violation → identity-level breach
- Loyalty-examination: surface ("do you feel loyal?") → deep ("what would you sacrifice for the group?")
- Emotional demand examination: gentle → challenging → fundamental

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Naming the Feeling | 1-5 | Role-emotion identification |
| Explaining the Guilt | 5-15 | Guilt-quality and proportionality |
| The Loyalty Examined | 15-30 | Loyalty-feeling vs. performance |
| The Code's Emotional Demands | 30-50 | Meta-cognitive engagement with role-emotions |
| The Confession Complete | 50+ | Full verbal role-affect integration |

## 4. Item Pool

- **Feeling-naming prompts:** Requests to identify current role-emotions — 20+
- **Guilt-examination prompts:** Questions about guilt quality and proportionality — 20+
- **Loyalty-examination prompts:** Questions about loyalty-feeling vs. performance — 15+
- **Emotional demand prompts:** Questions about role's emotional requirements — 15+
- **Integration prompts:** Multi-dimensional role-affect reflection — 10+

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input types:** Free text (primary); structured selection (secondary)
- **Timing:** Response latency tracked; reflection depth measured; avoidance patterns detected
- **NPC/AI:** LLM-powered confessor with rubric-based analysis; shadow-pattern detection; drive-health signal extraction; cross-session narrative tracking
- **LLM rubrics:** Genuine vs. performed affect language; guilt proportionality; loyalty-feeling vs. calculation; examination-refusal patterns; duty-martyr vs. emotional deserter signatures
- **State persistence:** Full response history; language pattern tracking; shadow-signal accumulation; cross-session narrative coherence; drive-health language scores; checkpoint position
