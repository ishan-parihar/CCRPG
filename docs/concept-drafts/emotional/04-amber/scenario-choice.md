# Emotional / Amber — Scenario-Choice Game Concept

> **Axis:** Decision-making when role-emotion conflicts with personal emotion — reveals all 4 shadows through choice patterns under emotional pressure.
> **Why this axis for this module:** Role-affect is revealed most clearly when the role's emotional demands conflict with what the player personally feels. The scenario-choice modality presents exactly these conflicts: duty-martyr always chooses role-emotion, emotional deserter always chooses personal emotion, premature empath chooses performed sophistication, feeling-refuser chooses role-emotion but refuses to examine why.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Heart's Dilemma"
- **Core mechanic:** The player faces branching scenarios where their role's emotional demands conflict with personal feelings, competing loyalties, or the call to examine their emotional life. Choices reveal the structure of their role-affect. No choice is "wrong" — but each is diagnostic.
- **Duration:** 4-10 minutes per session
- **Internal progression:** The Simple Role-Feeling → The Conflicting Emotion → The Costly Loyalty → The Feeling Questioned → The Heart's Integration

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**What this modality uniquely presents:** The DECISION dimension of role-affect — how the player chooses when role-emotion and personal emotion conflict. Unlike deterministic (raw affect accuracy), this modality measures the QUALITY of emotional choice under moral-emotional pressure.

**How this differs from other modalities:** Unlike language-reflective (verbal articulation), this modality measures actual choice behaviour under emotional conflict. A player who articulates role-emotions beautifully but chooses personal emotion here has a language-behaviour gap. A player who cannot articulate but chooses role-consistently has implicit role-affect.

**What it uniquely surfaces:** All 4 shadow quadrants through choice patterns. The duty-martyr always chooses the role-emotion regardless of context. The emotional deserter always chooses personal emotion. The premature empath chooses the "emotionally sophisticated" option. The feeling-refuser chooses role-emotion but refuses to examine the choice.

**Successful integration:** The player makes contextually appropriate emotional choices — feeling role-emotions when appropriate, acknowledging personal emotions when appropriate, examining emotional demands when appropriate.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Heart's Dilemma: a narrative interface presenting emotional conflicts within an Amber-stage world. Each scenario has 3-4 choices representing different emotional responses. Aesthetic: Amber-stage world — guild halls, sacred duties, the felt weight of belonging.

### Interaction
- **The Simple Role-Feeling (1-5):** Clear role-emotion, clear personal emotion. "Your order lost a battle. You feel grief. But your role requires you to show strength. What do you do?" Tests basic role-affect vs. personal affect.
- **The Conflicting Emotion (5-15):** Two role-emotions conflict. "Your loyalty to your guild and your loyalty to your family pull in opposite directions. What do you feel? What do you show?" Tests emotional hierarchy.
- **The Costly Loyalty (15-30):** Feeling the role-emotion costs significantly. "Feeling loyalty to this group means feeling grief for their loss — a loss that doesn't personally affect you. Can you feel it?" Tests genuine vs. performed loyalty.
- **The Feeling Questioned (30-50):** The role's emotional demand is challenged. "Your elder says the guilt you feel is disproportionate. Do you examine it? Release it? Hold it anyway?" Tests emotional examination capacity.
- **The Heart's Integration (50+):** Full integration: conflicting emotions + cost + examination + sovereign choice simultaneously.

### Feedback
- Role-emotion chosen with awareness → "You felt what the role required. And you knew it was a choice. The heart is both free and bound."
- Role-emotion chosen compulsively → "You felt what you must feel. But was this a choice? The heart asks: can you feel AND choose?"
- Personal emotion chosen → "You felt what you felt. The role's demand was set aside. Notice: was this sovereign or was this avoidance?"
- Emotional sophistication chosen → "You held space for the complexity. But did you feel the loyalty? The heart asks for the feeling, not the framework."
- Emotional examination → "You questioned the feeling. This is not betrayal — this is wisdom. The heart grows stronger when examined."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Conflict intensity: mild → moderate → severe → identity-level
- Emotional hierarchy complexity: simple → competing → multi-layered
- Examination depth: gentle → direct → fundamental
- Choice consequences: immediate → delayed → cross-session

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The Simple Role-Feeling | 1-5 | Single role-emotion vs. personal emotion |
| The Conflicting Emotion | 5-15 | Two role-emotions in conflict |
| The Costly Loyalty | 15-30 | Genuine vs. performed loyalty |
| The Feeling Questioned | 30-50 | Emotional demand examined |
| The Heart's Integration | 50+ | Full role-affect integration under maximum pressure |

## 4. Item Pool

- **Simple role-feeling scenarios:** Single role-emotion vs. personal emotion — 25+
- **Conflicting emotion scenarios:** Two role-emotions in conflict — 20+
- **Costly loyalty scenarios:** Genuine vs. performed loyalty tests — 20+
- **Feeling-examination scenarios:** Emotional demand challenged — 15+
- **Integration scenarios:** Multi-dimensional emotional conflicts — 10+

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input types:** Tap (choice selection); hold (deliberation); swipe (scenario navigation)
- **Timing:** Choice latency tracked; deliberation time measured; avoidance patterns detected
- **NPC/AI:** Adaptive scenario selection based on shadow-signal accumulation; consequence-delivery; cross-session narrative continuity; drive-health signal extraction from choice patterns
- **LLM:** Consequence narration; scenario adaptation; choice-quality assessment for ambiguous cases
- **State persistence:** Full choice history; shadow-pattern tracking; drive-health signal accumulation; cross-session narrative coherence; emotional-conflict history; checkpoint position
