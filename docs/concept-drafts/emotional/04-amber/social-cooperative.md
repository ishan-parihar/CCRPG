# Emotional / Amber — Social-Cooperative Game Concept

> **Axis:** Shared role-affect — how the player holds group-emotions, responds to others' emotional code-violations, and maintains their role-affect within a group context.
> **Why this axis for this module:** Role-affect at Amber is fundamentally GROUP-EMBEDDED. Loyalty is felt toward the group. Guilt is felt before the group. Duty-feeling is sustained by the group's shared code. This modality reveals whether the player's role-affect is genuinely relational or merely individual.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Order's Feeling"
- **Core mechanic:** The player and NPC companions share role-emotional obligations. Each member's emotional engagement affects the group's cohesion. The player must maintain their own role-affect while supporting others in maintaining theirs — and must decide what to do when a companion's emotional engagement fails.
- **Duration:** 5-12 minutes per session
- **Internal progression:** The Shared Feeling → The Group's Grief → The Companion's Coldness → The Emotional Code Enforced → The Order Feels Together

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**What this modality uniquely presents:** The RELATIONAL dimension of role-affect — how emotion functions within a group. Unlike deterministic (individual affect accuracy), this modality measures whether the player's role-affect is genuinely embedded in shared emotional obligation.

**How this differs from other modalities:** Unlike scenario-choice (individual decision quality), this modality measures group-embedded emotional engagement. Unlike embodied-somatic (physical feeling), this modality measures the social texture of role-affect.

**What it uniquely surfaces:** The duty-martyr imposes emotional standards on others. The emotional deserter cannot feel group-emotions. The premature empath performs group emotional attunement without genuine feeling. The feeling-refuser cannot form new group emotional bonds outside the existing code.

**Successful integration:** The player holds their own role-affect, supports companions in holding theirs, responds appropriately when companions' emotional engagement fails, and maintains the group's shared emotional life without either enforcing rigidly or abandoning collectively.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Order's Feeling: a group-interface where the player and 2-3 NPC companions share role-emotional obligations through a series of events. Each companion has their own emotional style (one tends toward duty-martyrdom, one toward emotional desertion, one toward performed sophistication). Aesthetic: Amber-stage brotherhood — shared grief, shared pride, the felt weight of collective emotional life.

### Interaction
- **The Shared Feeling (1-5):** Group shares a simple role-emotion. "The order has won a victory. Feel the shared pride together." Tests basic group-affect formation.
- **The Group's Grief (5-15):** Group must feel a difficult role-emotion together. "A companion has fallen. The order grieves." Tests genuine vs. performed group-affect.
- **The Companion's Coldness (15-30):** One companion fails to feel the group-emotion. "Mira shows no grief. What do you do?" Tests response to others' emotional code-failure.
- **The Emotional Code Enforced (30-50):** The group must decide whether to address the companion's emotional coldness. Tests the player's relationship to collective emotional standards.
- **The Order Feels Together (50+):** Full integration: individual role-affect + group coordination + emotional-failure response + code-enforcement simultaneously.

### Feedback
- Group affect held → "The order feels together. Each member's feeling is the group's strength. The code lives in the heart."
- Companion coldness ignored → "Mira felt nothing. You felt. But the group's emotional code was broken. What does the order mean if one member's coldness is ignored?"
- Companion coldness enforced rigidly → "Mira was corrected. The code was enforced. But was this compassion or rigidity? The order asks."
- Group affect abandoned → "The feeling was not shared. The order's emotional life was left. Notice: was this a sovereign group decision or collective avoidance?"
- New group emotion formed → "A new shared feeling is offered. The order can grow emotionally. Can you feel this new thing together?"

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Group size: 2 companions → 3 companions → 4 companions
- Companion emotional styles: simple → complex → conflicting
- Emotional-failure frequency: rare → occasional → frequent
- Code-enforcement complexity: clear → ambiguous → contested

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The Shared Feeling | 1-5 | Simple group role-emotion formation |
| The Group's Grief | 5-15 | Difficult group-affect engagement |
| The Companion's Coldness | 15-30 | Response to others' emotional failure |
| The Emotional Code Enforced | 30-50 | Collective emotional standard enforcement |
| The Order Feels Together | 50+ | Full group role-affect integration |

## 4. Item Pool

- **Shared feeling scenarios:** Group role-emotion formation of varying complexity — 20+
- **Group grief/pride tasks:** Difficult collective affect engagement — 20+
- **Companion emotional-failure scenarios:** Responses to others' role-affect failure — 20+
- **Emotional code-enforcement scenarios:** Collective emotional standard decisions — 15+
- **New group emotion scenarios:** Group emotional expansion — 10+

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input types:** Tap (choice selection); hold (feeling confirmation); swipe (companion interaction)
- **Timing:** Response latency to companion emotional-failure tracked; enforcement decision time measured; group affect quality assessed
- **NPC/AI:** Companion emotional-style simulation; emotional-failure event generation; group-dynamic modelling; shadow-signal accumulator; collective affect tracking
- **LLM:** Companion dialogue; emotional-failure narration; enforcement-consequence delivery; group-dynamic adaptation
- **State persistence:** Full group affect history; companion interaction patterns; emotional-failure response quality; enforcement decisions; cross-session group emotional continuity; drive/shadow signal accumulation; checkpoint position
