# Emotional / Amber — Immersive-RPG Game Concept

> **Axis:** Ecological role-affect — whether role-emotions appear spontaneously in free-play, without structured prompts or explicit emotional tasks.
> **Why this axis for this module:** The deepest test of role-affect is whether it appears SPONTANEOUSLY in unstructured contexts. A player who feels role-emotions in structured tasks but not in free-play has compartmentalised capacity. The immersive-RPG reveals whether role-affect has genuinely transferred to lived emotional behaviour.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Living Code"
- **Core mechanic:** The player inhabits a role within an Amber-stage world and navigates a free-play environment where role-emotional situations arise naturally. No explicit affect tasks — the player's role-affect is revealed through spontaneous emotional responses, role-consistent behaviour, and how they handle the inevitable conflicts between role-emotion and personal feeling.
- **Duration:** 8-20 minutes per session (open-ended; player-determined)
- **Internal progression:** Taking the Role → The World's Emotional Demands → The Personal Feeling Arises → The Code's Feeling Is Tested → The Living Code

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**What this modality uniquely presents:** The ECOLOGICAL dimension of role-affect — whether role-emotions appear spontaneously in unstructured contexts. Unlike all other modalities (which present explicit emotional challenges), this modality observes the player's natural emotional behaviour in a world that has role-emotional demands embedded in it.

**How this differs from other modalities:** Unlike deterministic, language-reflective, scenario-choice, embodied-somatic, strategic-planning, and social-cooperative — this modality presents NO explicit emotional challenges. The player simply inhabits the world. Their role-affect (or lack of it) emerges naturally.

**What it uniquely surfaces:** Whether role-affect has genuinely transferred to lived emotional behaviour. The duty-martyr will find emotional obligations everywhere and cannot rest. The emotional deserter will ignore role-emotional cues spontaneously. The premature empath will perform emotional sophistication without genuine feeling. The feeling-refuser will stay in known emotional territory and refuse novel role-affects.

**Successful integration:** The player inhabits their role emotionally — feeling role-emotions when they arise, resting when appropriate, adapting when the world demands it — without either compulsive duty-feeling or spontaneous emotional desertion.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Living Code: an open-world RPG environment set in an Amber-stage world. The player has a role (guardian, guild member, temple keeper) with associated emotional obligations. The world presents situations that naturally invoke role-affect — without labelling them as "emotional challenges." Aesthetic: Amber-stage world — feudal order, guild structures, the lived emotional texture of belonging.

### Interaction
- **Taking the Role (1-5):** Player selects a role and receives its associated emotional obligations. "As a guardian, you feel: vigilance for those in your care, loyalty to your order, guilt when you fail your duty." No explicit tasks — just the role.
- **The World's Emotional Demands (5-15):** The world presents situations that invoke role-emotions. A companion is in danger (vigilance). The order achieves a victory (shared pride). A vow is broken (guilt). The player responds (or doesn't) spontaneously.
- **The Personal Feeling Arises (15-30):** The world presents situations where personal emotion conflicts with role-emotion. The player's spontaneous response is observed.
- **The Code's Feeling Is Tested (30-50):** The world presents situations where the role's emotional demands are questioned. An unjust emotional demand. A role-emotion that has become destructive. The player's spontaneous response to emotional examination is observed.
- **The Living Code (50+):** Full integration: spontaneous role-affect + personal emotion management + emotional examination — all in free-play.

### Feedback
- Role-consistent emotional behaviour → "The guardian feels as a guardian feels. The world notices. The order's emotional life is alive."
- Role-inconsistent emotional behaviour → "The guardian felt outside the code. The world noticed. What does this mean for the role?"
- Compulsive emotional obligation-seeking → "The guardian finds emotional duty everywhere. Even in rest. The order asks: can you feel AND rest?"
- Emotional obligation-avoidance → "The guardian passed the moment without feeling. The code was not invoked. The world asks: was this a choice or an absence?"
- Emotional examination → "The guardian questioned the feeling. This is not betrayal — this is wisdom. The code grows stronger when examined."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- World emotional complexity: simple role-emotions → competing → multi-layered
- Personal-role conflict frequency: rare → occasional → frequent
- Emotional examination depth: gentle → direct → fundamental
- Role emotional complexity: single obligation → multiple → hierarchy

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Taking the Role | 1-5 | Role selection and emotional obligation awareness |
| The World's Emotional Demands | 5-15 | Spontaneous role-affect response |
| The Personal Feeling Arises | 15-30 | Spontaneous personal-role emotional conflict |
| The Code's Feeling Is Tested | 30-50 | Spontaneous emotional examination response |
| The Living Code | 50+ | Full ecological role-affect integration |

## 4. Item Pool

- **World emotional demand scenarios:** Situations invoking role-emotions spontaneously — 30+
- **Personal-role conflict scenarios:** Situations conflicting personal and role-emotion — 25+
- **Emotional examination scenarios:** Situations questioning role-emotional demands — 20+
- **Rest scenarios:** Situations where emotional rest is appropriate — 15+
- **Novel role-emotion scenarios:** Situations presenting new role-emotional obligations — 15+

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input types:** Tap (action selection); hold (deliberation); swipe (world navigation); free movement
- **Timing:** Response latency to role-emotional situations tracked; personal-role conflict response time measured; emotional examination engagement duration recorded
- **NPC/AI:** World-event generation based on role and shadow-signal accumulation; role-emotional invocation calibration; personal-role conflict generation; ecological emotional behaviour analysis
- **LLM:** World narration; NPC dialogue; consequence delivery; role-emotional invocation naturalness; examination depth
- **State persistence:** Full world-interaction history; spontaneous role-affect patterns; personal-role conflict responses; emotional examination engagement; role-consistency tracking; cross-session world-state continuity; drive/shadow signal accumulation; checkpoint position
