# Emotional / Amber — Deterministic Game Concept

> **Axis:** Objective measurement of role-congruent affect — recognition accuracy, guilt-vs-shame discrimination, loyalty-feeling intensity, and duty-emotion persistence.
> **Why this axis for this module:** Role-affect at Amber is measurable. Whether a player recognises guilt vs. shame, feels loyalty-warmth vs. transactional calculation, and sustains duty-feeling vs. raw motivation are all distinguishable through objective psychophysical tasks.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Feeling of the Code"
- **Core mechanic:** The player encounters role-emotional stimuli and must identify, discriminate, and sustain the correct role-congruent affect. Tasks are binary and objective: is this guilt or shame? Is this loyalty or calculation? Does the duty-feeling hold?
- **Duration:** 3-6 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-monitored)
- **Internal progression:** Affect Recognition → Guilt-Shame Discrimination → Loyalty Detection → Duty-Feeling Persistence → Full Role-Affect Integration

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**What this modality uniquely presents:** Pure role-affect challenge with no social, verbal, or embodied mediation. The emotional stimulus is presented; the player identifies or sustains the correct role-congruent affect. Binary. Objective. Ground truth for all other modalities.

**How this differs from other modalities:** No language required (unlike language-reflective). No decision-making under ambiguity (unlike scenario-choice). No body involvement (unlike embodied-somatic). No planning (unlike strategic-planning). No coordination (unlike social-cooperative). No free-play (unlike immersive-rpg). JUST: here is the emotional stimulus. What is the role-congruent response?

**What it uniquely surfaces:** The RAW capacity — stripped of compensatory strategies. A player who talks well about loyalty (language-reflective) but fails guilt-shame discrimination here has golden-addiction. A player who performs emotional sophistication but cannot sustain duty-feeling has bypassed role-affect.

**Successful integration:** The player demonstrates role-congruent affect — accurate, sustained, proportional — in pure psychophysical terms.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Feeling of the Code: a codex-style interface presenting emotional scenarios and requiring the player to identify the correct role-congruent affect. Aesthetic: Amber-stage monastery — illuminated manuscripts, emotional vignettes, the weight of felt obligation.

### Interaction
- **Affect Recognition (1-5):** "A guardian sees a child in danger. What does the guardian feel?" Multiple choice: vigilance / rage / indifference / pride. Tests role-affect recognition.
- **Guilt-Shame Discrimination (5-15):** "You broke your vow. You feel ___." Guilt (I violated my code) vs. shame (others saw me fail) vs. fear (I'll be punished). Tests genuine guilt-capacity.
- **Loyalty Detection (15-30):** Sustained loyalty-feeling tasks. "Hold the feeling of belonging to your order. Does it hold?" Intensity tracking over time.
- **Duty-Feeling Persistence (30-50):** Low-reward duty-feeling tasks. "The duty is boring. The feeling must hold." Tests duty-emotion without external reward.
- **Full Role-Affect Integration (50+):** All dimensions simultaneously: recognition + discrimination + loyalty + duty-feeling.

### Feedback
- Correct role-affect → "The code is felt. Your role lives in you. The feeling is true."
- Guilt-shame confusion → "That is shame — the fear of others' eyes. Guilt is different: the code speaks from within. Can you feel the difference?"
- Loyalty-flatness → "The order is there. Can you feel the warmth of belonging? Not calculation — warmth. Try again."
- Duty-feeling collapse → "The feeling faded. The duty remains. Can the feeling return without the reward?"
- Constant guilt → "The code has not been violated. The guilt is not proportional. Notice: is this guilt or is this the role consuming you?"

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Affect complexity: basic role-emotions → nuanced role-emotions → mixed role-emotions
- Guilt-shame discrimination: obvious → subtle → ambiguous
- Loyalty intensity: strong → moderate → faint
- Duty-feeling duration: 30 seconds → 2 minutes → 5 minutes
- Role complexity: single role → dual role → role-conflict

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Affect Recognition | 1-5 | Basic role-emotion identification |
| Guilt-Shame Discrimination | 5-15 | Genuine guilt vs. shame vs. fear |
| Loyalty Detection | 15-30 | Loyalty-feeling intensity and duration |
| Duty-Feeling Persistence | 30-50 | Duty-emotion without external reward |
| Full Role-Affect Integration | 50+ | All dimensions simultaneously |

## 4. Item Pool

- **Role-affect recognition items:** Scenarios requiring identification of role-congruent emotion — 30+
- **Guilt-shame discrimination items:** Scenarios distinguishing guilt, shame, and fear — 25+
- **Loyalty-feeling items:** Sustained loyalty-affect tasks — 20+
- **Duty-emotion items:** Low-reward duty-feeling persistence tasks — 20+
- **Role-conflict items:** Scenarios with competing role-emotional demands — 15+

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input types:** Tap (affect selection); hold (sustained feeling); slider (intensity rating)
- **Timing:** Response latency tracked; sustained-feeling duration measured; intensity decay monitored
- **NPC/AI:** Adaptive difficulty; guilt-shame discrimination calibration; loyalty-intensity tracking; duty-feeling persistence measurement; shadow-signal accumulator
- **LLM:** None — fully deterministic scoring
- **State persistence:** Full trial history; recognition accuracy; guilt-shame discrimination scores; loyalty-feeling duration; duty-emotion persistence; cross-session stability; drive/shadow signal accumulation; checkpoint position
