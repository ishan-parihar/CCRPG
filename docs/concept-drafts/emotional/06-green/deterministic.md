# Emotional / Green — Deterministic Game Concept

> **Axis:** Objective empathic measurement — multi-perspective affect recognition accuracy, co-regulation precision, boundary detection.  **Why this axis for this module:** Pluralistic empathy must be measurable; deterministic tasks isolate whether the player can actually perceive and hold multiple emotional signatures or merely performs the capacity.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Title:** Affective Field  
**Core loop:** Player observes a scene containing 3–10 characters each expressing distinct emotional signatures. Player must identify, differentiate, and hold awareness of all simultaneously — then respond to co-regulation prompts without losing track of any signature.  
**Session length:** 4–8 minutes (infinite checkpoint).  
**Progression:** Signature count scales from 3 → 10; contradiction intensity increases; out-group characters introduced at higher tiers.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst type:** Perceptual-affective load under increasing complexity.  
**Shadow surfacing:**
- DA surfaces as over-attribution — player "sees" emotions that aren't present, merges distinct signatures into one undifferentiated mass.
- DAll surfaces as under-attribution — player identifies fewer signatures than present, particularly missing relational/vulnerable affects.
- GA surfaces as pattern-matching without genuine discrimination — correct labels but wrong relational weighting.
- GAll surfaces as accurate recognition that plateaus — refuses to engage with transpersonal prompts at ceiling.

**Drive probing:** Agency via boundary-maintenance trials (can player identify "not mine" affects); Communion via co-regulation accuracy; Eros via willingness to engage higher-complexity trials; Agape via return-to-accuracy after overload.

## 3. Game Design

**Mechanics:**
- **Recognition phase:** Scene displays characters with micro-expression, posture, vocal-tone, and contextual cues. Player tags each character's primary and secondary affect from a 24-emotion palette.
- **Contradiction phase:** Two characters express mutually opposing emotions about the same event. Player must hold both as valid without collapsing into one.
- **Co-regulation phase:** One character's affect begins shifting. Player must track the shift and respond with appropriate co-regulatory input (timing-based selection from 4 options).
- **Boundary phase:** Empathic load increases — player must identify which affects are "theirs" vs. "received" while maintaining accuracy.

**Scoring:** Accuracy (correct identification), discrimination (distinguishing similar affects), contradiction tolerance (holding both without collapse), co-regulation precision (timing + selection), boundary integrity (self/other distinction under load).

**Difficulty staircase:** Adaptive — signature count, contradiction intensity, co-regulation speed, and boundary-load all scale independently based on player performance.

## 4. Item Pool

- 24-emotion palette (Plutchik-extended with relational affects: attunement, rupture, co-joy, shared-grief)
- 200+ character-scene combinations across cultural contexts
- 40 contradiction scenarios (paired opposing affects)
- 30 co-regulation sequences (shift patterns with correct response windows)
- 20 boundary-load escalation patterns
- Out-group character sets (cultural, age, neurotype diversity)

## 5. Technical Requirements

- Micro-expression rendering system (procedural face generation or curated stimulus library)
- Adaptive difficulty engine with 5 independent parameters
- Response-time capture at 16ms precision for co-regulation timing
- Session state persistence at every trial boundary
- Psychometric model: 2PL IRT per sub-skill, drive-health scoring via dual-domain formula
- Shadow-detection thresholds: over-attribution ratio > 1.4 flags DA; under-attribution < 0.6 flags DAll; high-accuracy + low-ceiling-engagement flags GAll; correct-labels + wrong-weighting flags GA
