# Emotional / Green — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** Planning empathic life — structuring emotional availability, managing compassion fatigue, sustaining pluralistic empathy over time.  **Why this axis for this module:** Green empathy without strategic structure collapses into overwhelm or burns out. The capacity to PLAN one's empathic engagement — when to open, when to restore, how to sustain — is the executive dimension of pluralistic affect.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Title:** The Empathic Economy  
**Core loop:** Player manages an "empathic budget" across multiple relational demands over simulated time — allocating emotional availability, scheduling restoration, anticipating empathic load, and maintaining sustainable pluralistic engagement without collapse or withdrawal.  
**Session length:** 5–9 minutes (infinite checkpoint).  
**Progression:** Relational demands increase; time horizons extend; unexpected empathic crises introduced; out-group demands added.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst type:** Executive-empathic planning under resource constraint.  
**Shadow surfacing:**
- DA surfaces as over-allocation — player gives empathic availability to everyone, schedules no restoration, budget depletes to zero (compassion fatigue pattern).
- DAll surfaces as over-structured withdrawal — player allocates minimal availability, maximises restoration, treats empathy as costly resource to hoard.
- GA surfaces as planning for transpersonal engagement without concrete relational allocation — "I'll hold space for the collective" while specific persons go unattended.
- GAll surfaces as excellent pluralistic planning that refuses to include growth-edges — plans only for comfortable empathic engagements.

**Drive probing:** Agency via boundary-setting in allocation (saying "not now"); Communion via quality of relational prioritisation; Eros via inclusion of growth-edge relationships; Agape via concrete care for specific persons over abstract "availability."

## 3. Game Design

**Mechanics:**
- **Assessment phase:** Player sees empathic budget (finite resource) and the week's relational demands — 5–12 characters/groups each needing different types of empathic engagement.
- **Allocation phase:** Player distributes availability across demands. Each allocation has a cost and relational outcome. Over-allocation depletes; under-allocation damages relationships.
- **Crisis phase:** Unexpected empathic demands arise mid-plan. Player must re-allocate without collapsing the whole structure.
- **Restoration phase:** Player schedules self-restoration. Skipping restoration degrades future capacity. Over-restoring at others' expense reveals DAll.
- **Review phase:** Outcomes unfold — which relationships thrived, which suffered, how budget held up.

**Scoring:** Budget sustainability (didn't deplete to zero), relational equity (no single relationship starved), crisis resilience (maintained structure under surprise), growth-inclusion (allocated toward challenging relationships), restoration adequacy (maintained capacity over time).

**Difficulty staircase:** More demands, tighter budget, more frequent crises, out-group demands, longer time horizons requiring multi-week planning.

## 4. Item Pool

- 60+ relational demand profiles (intimate partner, friend-in-crisis, colleague, out-group member, community obligation, stranger)
- 20 crisis scenarios (unexpected empathic demands with time pressure)
- 15 restoration activities (varying effectiveness and time cost)
- Budget calibration variants (different starting capacities)
- Growth-edge relationship profiles (unfamiliar cultural contexts, challenging personalities)
- Long-term consequence chains (allocation patterns compound over simulated weeks)

## 5. Technical Requirements

- Resource-management engine: empathic budget as depletable/renewable resource with decay and restoration curves
- Relational outcome model: allocation quality → relationship health over time
- Crisis injection system: adaptive timing based on player's current budget state
- Multi-week simulation: plans compound; neglected relationships degrade; over-extension creates future vulnerability
- Scoring model: multi-dimensional sustainability metric
- Shadow detection: zero-budget-at-end flags DA; maximum-hoarding flags DAll; abstract-allocation-without-persons flags GA; growth-edge-avoidance flags GAll
- Session persistence at phase boundaries
- Visualisation: empathic budget as intuitive resource meter; relational health as visible indicators
