# Spiritual / Green — Embodied-Somatic Game Concept

> **Axis:** Body as site of pluralistic faith — multi-tradition embodied practice, sacred movement across traditions.  **Why this axis for this module:** Spiritual tourism lives in the head; genuine pluralistic faith lives in the body. The player who has PRACTICED multiple traditions carries them somatically — the body knows what the intellect merely collects.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Name:** Temple of Many Bodies  
**Core loop:** Player engages in embodied practices drawn from multiple spiritual traditions — breath patterns, movement sequences, postural holds, rhythm cycles. The game measures whether the body has genuinely internalized multi-tradition practice or merely mimics surface forms. Somatic fluency across traditions reveals depth vs. tourism.  
**Session length:** 5–10 minutes per checkpoint.  
**Infinite checkpoint:** Each session focuses on 1–2 traditions' embodied practices; cross-session progression builds a somatic vocabulary spanning traditions.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst type:** Multi-tradition embodied practice with somatic accuracy measurement.  
**Shadow surfacing:** DA surfaces as body-sampling — attempts many tradition-specific movements but none with embodied fluency; the body hasn't committed to any practice long enough to internalize it. DAll surfaces as somatic refusal — mechanical execution without sacred engagement; the body moves but doesn't pray. GA surfaces as premature somatic synthesis — blends traditions' movements before mastering any single tradition's form. GAll surfaces as refusal to acknowledge that some traditions' somatic practices are more developed.  
**Drive probes:** Eros measured by willingness to attempt unfamiliar tradition's embodied practice. Agape measured by quality of return to familiar practice after exploration. Agency measured by somatic confidence in one's own practice-body. Communion measured by capacity to synchronize with another tradition's rhythm.

## 3. Game Design

**Practice modalities:** (a) Breath: pranayama patterns, Sufi breath, hesychast breathing, Buddhist anapanasati, Taoist embryonic breathing. (b) Movement: yoga asana, tai chi forms, Sufi whirling, sacred dance, walking meditation, prostrations. (c) Stillness: zazen posture, Christian contemplative sitting, Hindu meditation mudras, Quaker silence-holding. (d) Rhythm: drumming traditions, chanting cadences, prayer-bead timing, liturgical rhythm.  
**Measurement:** Accelerometer/gyroscope data (mobile) or timing-based input (desktop) measures: rhythm accuracy, postural stability, breath-pattern consistency, transition fluency between practices.  
**Difficulty staircase:** Level 1: follow a single tradition's practice with guidance. Level 2: execute from memory. Level 3: transition between two traditions' practices fluidly. Level 4: identify which tradition a presented practice belongs to through body-feel. Level 5: create genuine somatic dialogue between traditions (not blending — dialoguing).  
**Shadow-mode:** When DA detected, sessions demand sustained single-tradition practice (10+ minutes in one form). When DAll detected, sessions introduce sacred framing that invites the body to pray rather than exercise. When GA detected, sessions require mastery-demonstration in single traditions before allowing cross-tradition work. When GAll detected, sessions present clearly more-developed somatic traditions and invite acknowledgment.

## 4. Item Pool

**Practice library:** 8+ traditions × 4 modalities × 3 difficulty levels = 96+ distinct embodied practices.  
**Tradition-specific markers:** Each practice tagged with tradition-specific quality indicators (e.g., pranayama requires specific nostril-timing ratios; tai chi requires specific weight-transfer patterns).  
**Cross-tradition pairs:** Practices paired by structural similarity (e.g., hesychast breathing ↔ anapanasati) for comparative work.  
**Adaptive selection:** Practice selection based on player's somatic profile — traditions with low embodied fluency are prioritized; traditions with high fluency become the ground from which new traditions are explored.

## 5. Technical Requirements

**Input modalities:** Primary: device accelerometer + gyroscope for movement/posture. Secondary: microphone for breath-pattern detection. Tertiary: touch-timing for rhythm-based practices. Fallback: button-press timing for desktop without sensors.  
**Scoring:** Per-practice accuracy (tradition-specific quality metrics) × sustained-duration bonus × cross-tradition fluency composite. Depth-score: accuracy within single tradition over time. Breadth-score: number of traditions with above-threshold fluency.  
**Calibration:** Initial sessions calibrate to player's physical capacity — scoring adjusts for age, mobility, and experience. Sacred engagement measured through practice-duration willingness and return-frequency, not physical performance.  
**Telemetry:** Per-tradition somatic fluency scores, practice-duration patterns (DA indicator: short sessions across many traditions), sacred-engagement proxy (voluntary practice extension), cross-tradition transition quality, shadow-indicator composites.  
**Accessibility:** All practices have seated/standing/lying alternatives. No practice requires specific physical capacity. Measurement adapts to available input modalities.
