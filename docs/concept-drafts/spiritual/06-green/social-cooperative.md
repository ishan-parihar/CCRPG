# Spiritual / Green — Social-Cooperative Game Concept

> **Axis:** Shared pluralistic faith — interfaith dialogue, group sacred practice across traditions.  **Why this axis for this module:** Pluralistic faith is ultimately relational — it exists in the BETWEEN of traditions meeting. The social dimension reveals whether the player can genuinely encounter another's sacred without colonizing, dismissing, or prematurely synthesizing it.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Name:** The Interfaith Circle  
**Core loop:** Player engages in cooperative interfaith encounters — dialogue exchanges, shared practice sessions, collaborative sacred projects — with AI-driven partners representing different spiritual traditions. The game measures quality of interfaith engagement: genuine curiosity vs. tourism, deep listening vs. projection, collaborative creation vs. homogenization.  
**Session length:** 5–8 minutes per checkpoint.  
**Infinite checkpoint:** Each session is a distinct interfaith encounter; relationship-depth with tradition-partners accumulates across sessions.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst type:** Cooperative interfaith engagement requiring genuine relational encounter with otherness.  
**Shadow surfacing:** DA surfaces as shallow engagement with many tradition-partners without deepening any relationship — "spiritual networking" without intimacy. DAll surfaces as inability to engage the sacred dimension of dialogue — keeps conversation analytical, refuses to participate in shared practice. GA surfaces as performing unity — "we're all saying the same thing!" — without genuinely hearing what the other tradition is actually saying. GAll surfaces as enforcing equality in dialogue — shutting down any suggestion that traditions might have different depths or that one partner's tradition might offer something the player's doesn't.  
**Drive probes:** Eros probed by encounters that invite the player toward unfamiliar sacred territory through relationship. Agape probed by encounters requiring the player to share their own tradition's depth vulnerably. Agency probed by encounters requiring the player to maintain their own stance while genuinely hearing another's. Communion probed by encounters requiring genuine co-creation across traditions.

## 3. Game Design

**Encounter types:** (a) Dialogue: structured interfaith conversation where player responds to tradition-partner's sharing about their path. (b) Shared-practice: player and tradition-partner engage in each other's practices together. (c) Sacred-project: collaborative creation (ritual, prayer, art) that must honor both traditions without homogenizing. (d) Conflict-resolution: traditions disagree on a practical matter; player must navigate without dismissing either. (e) Depth-exchange: tradition-partner shares something esoteric; player must receive it without colonizing or dismissing.  
**AI tradition-partners:** 8+ distinct AI characters, each embodying a specific tradition with depth, personality, and boundaries. Partners have their own "comfort zones" and will push back against tourism, premature synthesis, or dismissal.  
**Relationship mechanics:** Each tradition-partner has a trust-meter that increases with genuine engagement and decreases with tourism, projection, or dismissal. Higher trust unlocks deeper sharing and more challenging encounters.  
**Shadow-mode:** When DA detected, tradition-partners demand depth before breadth — "you've visited many of us but known none." When DAll detected, partners invite felt participation — "don't analyze my prayer; pray WITH me." When GA detected, partners insist on difference — "please don't tell me my tradition is the same as yours." When GAll detected, partners gently assert depth — "my tradition has spent 2000 years developing this; that matters."

## 4. Item Pool

**Tradition-partner profiles:** 12+ fully-developed AI characters with: tradition-specific knowledge, personality, boundaries, sharing-progression (what they reveal at each trust-level), and challenge-behaviors (how they push back against shadows).  
**Dialogue trees:** 50+ dialogue scenarios per tradition-partner, branching based on player response quality.  
**Shared-practice scripts:** 30+ cooperative practice sequences spanning tradition-pairs.  
**Sacred-project templates:** 20+ collaborative creation frameworks that require genuine interfaith synthesis without homogenization.  
**Adaptive pairing:** Tradition-partners selected based on player's shadow-profile and growth-edge.

## 5. Technical Requirements

**AI dialogue:** LLM-driven tradition-partners operating within rubric-defined personality and knowledge boundaries. Partners maintain consistency across sessions (memory of prior encounters).  
**Response evaluation:** Player responses scored on: genuine curiosity (questions that demonstrate real interest), deep listening (responses that accurately reflect what partner shared), appropriate vulnerability (sharing own tradition without defensiveness), and collaborative quality (co-creation that honors both).  
**Scoring:** Relationship-depth composite across tradition-partners × engagement-quality per encounter × breadth of genuine relationships (not shallow contacts). Depth-weighted: one deep interfaith relationship scores higher than five shallow ones.  
**Telemetry:** Per-partner trust-levels, engagement-quality trends, dialogue-pattern analysis (tourism indicators, dismissal indicators, synthesis indicators, fortress indicators), cooperative-creation quality scores, shadow-indicator composites.  
**Social features:** Optional real-player interfaith encounters (future phase) with AI-mediated structure and safety.
