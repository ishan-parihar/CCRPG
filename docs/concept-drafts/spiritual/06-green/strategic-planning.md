# Spiritual / Green — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** Planning spiritual life — structuring multi-tradition practice, managing depth vs. breadth.  **Why this axis for this module:** The dilettante has no plan; the genuine pluralist has a STRUCTURE for engaging multiple traditions with both breadth and depth. Strategic planning reveals whether pluralistic faith is intentional or merely scattered.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Name:** The Pilgrim's Calendar  
**Core loop:** Player designs and manages a spiritual practice schedule across multiple traditions, allocating time, energy, and commitment. The game presents resource constraints (finite hours, energy, social obligations) that force genuine prioritization. Planning patterns reveal whether the player can structure depth-within-breadth or defaults to shallow sampling.  
**Session length:** 5–8 minutes per checkpoint.  
**Infinite checkpoint:** Each session presents a new planning horizon (week/month/season); cumulative planning patterns reveal developmental trajectory.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst type:** Resource-constrained spiritual life planning that forces depth-vs-breadth tradeoffs.  
**Shadow surfacing:** DA surfaces as plans that spread thin across many traditions with no depth-commitment — every tradition gets a taste, none gets sustained practice. DAll surfaces as plans that exclude spiritual practice entirely or reduce it to "intellectual study only." GA surfaces as plans that jump to "integral practice" without scheduling genuine immersion in particular traditions. GAll surfaces as plans that refuse to prioritize any tradition over another — perfectly equal allocation that prevents depth anywhere.  
**Drive probes:** Eros probed by planning horizons that invite long-term spiritual aspiration. Agape probed by planning that requires returning to sustain existing practice. Agency probed by requiring personal prioritization under constraint. Communion probed by planning that includes shared practice with others from different traditions.

## 3. Game Design

**Planning mechanics:** (a) Time-allocation: 168 hours/week; player allocates spiritual practice time across traditions, with minimum-depth thresholds that must be met for practice to "count." (b) Depth-gates: each tradition has depth-milestones that require sustained commitment (e.g., "Zen requires 3 months of daily zazen before koan work unlocks"). (c) Synergy-bonuses: certain tradition-pairs enhance each other when practiced in sequence (e.g., contemplative prayer + vipassana). (d) Conflict-costs: some traditions have exclusivity requirements that create genuine tradeoffs.  
**Constraint escalation:** Early sessions have generous time. Mid-sessions introduce life-constraints (work, family, health) that compress available time. Late sessions add social obligations (community expects single-tradition loyalty) and opportunity costs (a retreat in tradition X means missing a festival in tradition Y).  
**Shadow-mode:** When DA detected, game introduces minimum-depth requirements — "you cannot add a new tradition until you've reached depth-milestone 2 in at least one." When DAll detected, game presents planning scenarios where spiritual practice has measurable life-benefits that appeal to rational self-interest. When GA detected, game blocks "integral practice" options until particular-tradition depth is demonstrated. When GAll detected, game presents scenarios where equal allocation demonstrably fails and prioritization succeeds.

## 4. Item Pool

**Tradition-practice database:** 12+ traditions × 5 practice-types × 4 depth-levels = 240+ schedulable practices.  
**Depth-milestone system:** Each tradition has 5 depth-milestones with specific time-investment requirements (e.g., "Buddhist Level 3: 90 days of daily meditation + one 7-day retreat").  
**Life-constraint scenarios:** 50+ constraint-sets representing different life situations (student, parent, professional, retiree, crisis, abundance).  
**Planning templates:** Pre-built plans representing each shadow archetype (the dilettante's scattered plan, the atheist's empty plan, the non-dualist's premature integral plan, the fortress's perfectly-equal plan) used as diagnostic comparisons.

## 5. Technical Requirements

**Interface:** Calendar/schedule builder with drag-and-drop practice blocks. Visual feedback showing depth-progress per tradition (progress bars), breadth-coverage (tradition-wheel), and balance indicators.  
**Scoring:** Composite of: depth-achievement (traditions reaching milestones), breadth-coverage (traditions engaged above threshold), sustainability (plans that could actually be maintained), and integration (synergy-bonuses activated). Penalized for: over-spread (too many traditions below threshold), abandonment (traditions started then dropped), and rigidity (inability to adapt plan when constraints change).  
**Adaptive difficulty:** Constraint severity increases as player demonstrates competence. Planning horizons extend from weekly to seasonal to yearly as capacity grows.  
**Telemetry:** Tradition-count per plan, depth-per-tradition distribution, time-to-decision (hesitation on prioritization = GAll indicator), plan-revision frequency (constant revision = DA indicator), constraint-response patterns, shadow-indicator composites from planning behavior.
