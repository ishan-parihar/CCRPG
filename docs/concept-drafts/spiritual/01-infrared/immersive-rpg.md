# Spiritual × Infrared — Immersive-RPG Game

## 1. Core Concept

**Title:** The World That Strikes

An open firelit world — cave, clearing, shoreline — where the player moves freely. No objectives. No prompts. The game embeds vast-phenomena into the ecology and measures whether the player NOTICES them, APPROACHES them, and is STRUCK by them without being told to look.

This is the ecological validity test: does awe-response appear spontaneously in free-play? Not when prompted, not when measured, not when the game says "look here" — but when the organism encounters the numinous on its own terms, in its own time.

## 2. Mechanical Specification

**Input:** Free movement (joystick/swipe) through a small explorable environment. No HUD, no objectives, no markers.

**Environment:** A procedurally varied nightscape — fire at centre, explorable periphery containing embedded vast-phenomena:
- A cliff edge revealing depth
- A gap in canopy revealing stars
- A still pool reflecting firelight into apparent infinity
- A distant sound (ocean, wind through canyon) implying enormity beyond sight

**Metrics captured (all implicit — player never knows what's measured):**
- Discovery rate: how many vast-phenomena does the player find per session?
- Approach behaviour: does the player move toward vast-phenomena or away?
- Dwell time: how long does the player remain near each vast-phenomenon?
- Stillness onset: does movement cease near vast-phenomena? (spontaneous awe-freeze)
- Return pattern: does the player return to previously discovered vast-phenomena?
- Exploration vs. fixation: does the player explore broadly or fixate on one phenomenon?
- Fire-return frequency: how often does the player return to safety between explorations?

**Progression:** The environment subtly evolves across sessions — new vast-phenomena appear, familiar ones shift. The game tracks whether the player's wonder-capacity grows (discovering subtler phenomena) or decays (only responding to dramatic ones).

## 3. Shadow Diagnostics

| Shadow | Behavioural Signal |
|---|---|
| DA (Awe-Fixation) | Finds one vast-phenomenon and never leaves; infinite dwell; no exploration after first discovery |
| DAll (Awe-Numbness) | Moves through environment without differential behaviour near vast-phenomena; no dwell, no stillness |
| GA (Premature Animism) | Treats everything as vast; dwell-time undifferentiated between vast and ordinary; no discrimination |
| GAll (Awe Fortress) | Discovers vast-phenomena (approach behaviour present) but minimal dwell; finds then immediately leaves |

## 4. Catalyst → Experience → Integration

**Catalyst:** The world itself — vast-phenomena embedded in ecology, waiting to be discovered. The game trusts the player to find the numinous without guidance.

**Experience:** Spontaneous wonder. The most ecologically valid form of awe-response — not evoked by a test, not prompted by a game mechanic, but arising naturally from encounter with an environment that contains enormity.

**Integration:** The fire-return. The player's home-base (the campfire) is always available. Integration = the player can explore, be struck, and return. The rhythm of venture-and-return IS the proto-spiritual practice: going out to meet the vast, coming home to the ordinary, carrying something back.

**Healing vector (bottom-up):** For awe-numbness — vast-phenomena gradually become more salient across sessions; for awe-fixation — the fire's warmth increases, making return more attractive.

**Evolution vector (top-down):** For premature animism — the environment includes clearly non-vast elements, testing discrimination; for awe-fortress — vast-phenomena become warmer and less threatening, inviting longer dwell.

## 5. Infinite Checkpoint Structure

Each session = one night in the world (5–15 minutes, player-determined). The player leaves by returning to fire and resting. The world persists — discoveries carry over, new phenomena emerge. Theta-decay: if the player doesn't visit, their discovery-map fades; previously found vast-phenomena must be re-discovered, testing whether the awe-response is still alive.
