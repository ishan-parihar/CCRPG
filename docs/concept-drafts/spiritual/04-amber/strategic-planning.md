# Spiritual / Amber — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** Planning sacred practice — structuring ritual obligations, maintaining tradition across time.  **Why this axis for this module:** Mythic-membership faith requires *maintenance* — the tradition must be kept alive through structured practice across days, seasons, and life-stages. How the player plans and sustains sacred structure reveals whether faith is compulsive, absent, performative, or genuinely integrated.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Title:** The Sacred Calendar

**Core Mechanic:** Resource-allocation and scheduling puzzles where the player must maintain a tradition's ritual obligations across competing time-demands. The player structures daily devotions, seasonal observances, and life-passage preparations while managing finite time/energy resources.

**Duration:** 5–8 minutes per session.

**Internal Progression:** (1) Single daily practice → (2) Weekly rhythm → (3) Seasonal calendar → (4) Competing obligations → (5) Sustaining tradition through crisis/change.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Unique Presentation:** A calendar/planner interface with sacred and secular demands competing for time-slots. The aesthetic is warm and tradition-coloured — not clinical.

**Differs From Others:** No knowledge-testing (deterministic), no language (language-reflective), no dilemmas (scenario-choice), no body (embodied-somatic), no others (social), no free-play (immersive). Pure planning-as-faith-maintenance.

**Uniquely Surfaces:** DA through over-structuring — every moment scheduled for sacred practice, no flexibility, secular life sacrificed. DAll through inability to maintain any sacred structure — everything secular, no rhythm. GA through planning "advanced" practices (meditation retreats, interfaith study) without maintaining basic daily devotion. GAll through rigid maintenance of existing structure + panic when the calendar must adapt.

**Successful Integration:** Player maintains meaningful sacred rhythm AND adapts flexibly when life demands change — tradition is alive, not mechanical.

## 3. Game Design

**Setup:** Player receives a week/season with time-slots and a set of tradition-obligations (daily prayer, weekly gathering, seasonal feast, life-passage preparation). Secular demands compete for the same slots.

**Interaction Phases:**
1. **Foundation** — place a single daily practice into the week
2. **Rhythm** — build weekly sacred rhythm (gathering, study, rest-day)
3. **Season** — layer seasonal observances onto the weekly rhythm
4. **Conflict** — secular demands encroach; something must give
5. **Adaptation** — a life-change (move, illness, loss) disrupts everything; rebuild sacred structure

**Feedback Examples:**
1. Balanced sacred/secular schedule → calendar glows warmly, tradition-health rises
2. Over-scheduled sacred (no secular life) → calendar cracks, burnout indicator
3. No sacred structure at all → calendar greys out, emptiness indicator
4. Advanced practices without daily foundation → structure wobbles, top-heavy
5. Rigid refusal to adapt when life changes → calendar shatters, tradition becomes burden

**Difficulty Adaptation:** More obligations compete, time becomes scarcer, disruptions become more severe, the "right" balance becomes less obvious. Seasonal complexity increases.

**Internal Progression Table:**

| Level | Planning Task | Shadow Probed | Drive Engaged |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single daily practice | Baseline | Communion (joining rhythm) |
| 2 | Weekly sacred rhythm | DAll (no structure) | Agape (returning daily) |
| 3 | Seasonal calendar layering | DA (over-structuring) | Agency (personal prioritisation) |
| 4 | Competing obligations | DA/GAll (rigidity/panic) | Eros (reaching toward flexibility) |
| 5 | Rebuilding after disruption | All four | All four (adaptive integration) |

## 4. Item Pool

**Category 1 — Daily Practices (20+):** Morning prayer, evening prayer, meal blessings, scripture reading, meditation sit, gratitude practice, devotional chanting, candle-lighting, journaling-as-prayer, walking meditation, midday pause, bedtime blessing, morning offering, breath-prayer, sacred reading, contemplation period, body-prayer, nature-acknowledgment, ancestor-remembrance, silence practice.

**Category 2 — Weekly Observances (15+):** Sabbath/rest-day, communal gathering, study group, service/charity, fasting day, feast day, confession/examination, teaching/mentoring, pilgrimage-walk, choir/chanting group, sacred-meal preparation, community service, prayer-circle, tradition-study, youth-teaching.

**Category 3 — Seasonal Obligations (15+):** Major holy days, fasting seasons, feast seasons, pilgrimage periods, initiation seasons, harvest celebrations, solstice/equinox observances, new-year rituals, remembrance periods, preparation seasons, purification periods, celebration cycles, retreat times, renewal ceremonies, ancestor-honouring seasons.

**Category 4 — Secular Competing Demands (20+):** Work deadlines, family obligations, health appointments, social events, travel, education, rest/recovery, household maintenance, financial pressures, relationship needs, creative projects, exercise, entertainment, community (non-sacred) obligations, career advancement, childcare, eldercare, emergencies, moving house, illness recovery.

**Category 5 — Life-Disruptions (15+):** Job loss, new baby, death in family, serious illness, relocation, divorce/separation, faith-crisis, community-split, natural disaster, pandemic, aging-parent care, child leaving home, retirement, conversion of family member, tradition-community dissolving.

## 5. Technical Requirements

**Input Types:** Drag-and-drop scheduling, tap to place/remove, swipe to reschedule, pinch to zoom time-scale (day→week→season).

**Timing:** No time pressure within session. Planning is contemplative. Session ends when player confirms schedule or time-cap reached.

**NPC/AI:** Calendar responds dynamically — shows consequences of choices over simulated time. No character NPCs.

**LLM:** Generates tradition-specific obligation sets. Adapts disruption scenarios to player's tradition-stream and life-context.

**State Persistence:** Planning-pattern history (over-structured vs. under-structured vs. balanced), adaptation-flexibility scores, tradition-maintenance consistency across sessions, disruption-recovery patterns, sacred/secular ratio trajectory.
