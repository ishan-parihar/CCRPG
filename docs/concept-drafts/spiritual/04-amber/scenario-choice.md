# Spiritual / Amber — Scenario-Choice Game Concept

> **Axis:** Faith dilemmas where tradition conflicts with personal experience or other traditions.  **Why this axis for this module:** Mythic-membership faith is tested at its boundaries — when the tradition says one thing and experience says another, the player's choice reveals whether faith is rigid, absent, bypassed, or genuinely integrated.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Title:** The Crossroads of Faith

**Core Mechanic:** Branching narrative dilemmas where the player's tradition-commitments are placed in tension with competing goods — personal experience, compassion for outsiders, rational evidence, or the call to deepen. Each choice maps to a shadow-quadrant without the player knowing.

**Duration:** 5–8 minutes per session (2–3 dilemmas).

**Internal Progression:** (1) Simple tradition-loyalty choices → (2) Tradition vs. compassion → (3) Tradition vs. evidence → (4) Tradition vs. other-tradition → (5) Tradition vs. the call to deepen.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Unique Presentation:** Narrative vignettes set within the player's tradition-world. Characters face genuine faith-crises. The player advises or decides.

**Differs From Others:** No objective measurement (deterministic), no language production (language-reflective), no body (embodied-somatic), no planning (strategic), no others present (social), no free-play (immersive). Pure dilemma-as-catalyst.

**Uniquely Surfaces:** DA through always choosing tradition over compassion/evidence/others. DAll through always choosing against tradition. GA through choosing "transcend both" options that avoid genuine commitment. GAll through choosing tradition but with visible distress/avoidance when doubt-options appear.

**Successful Integration:** Player makes choices that honour tradition AND acknowledge complexity — neither rigidly loyal nor prematurely transcendent.

## 3. Game Design

**Setup:** A narrative frame: the player is a tradition-keeper (priest, elder, teacher, parent) whose community faces faith-challenges. Dilemmas arrive as community members seeking guidance.

**Interaction Phases:**
1. **Context** — the dilemma is presented with full emotional weight
2. **Stakes** — what is at risk for each possible choice is made clear
3. **Choice** — 4 options (mapping to DA, DAll, GA, GAll — never labelled)
4. **Consequence** — the narrative shows what unfolds from the choice
5. **Reflection** — a brief moment to sit with the outcome (no second chance)

**Feedback Examples:**
1. Tradition-honouring choice with compassion → community strengthens, questioner feels held
2. Rigid-exclusion choice → community tightens but someone is lost
3. Dismissal-of-tradition choice → freedom gained but belonging fractured
4. Premature-transcendence choice → sounds wise but no one feels helped
5. Avoidance choice → tension deferred but grows underground

**Difficulty Adaptation:** Dilemmas become more genuinely difficult — both sides become more compelling, stakes increase, easy answers disappear. The "right" answer becomes less obvious.

**Internal Progression Table:**

| Level | Dilemma Type | Shadow Probed | Drive Engaged |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Simple loyalty test | Baseline | Communion (belonging) |
| 2 | Tradition vs. compassion for outsider | DA (exclusion) | Agape (return to care) |
| 3 | Tradition vs. rational evidence | GAll (fear of doubt) | Eros (reaching toward truth) |
| 4 | Tradition vs. compelling other-tradition | DA/GA (rigidity/bypass) | Agency (sovereign discernment) |
| 5 | Tradition vs. call to deepen/transform | All four | All four (integration) |

## 4. Item Pool

**Category 1 — Loyalty Dilemmas (15+):** A member wants to leave; a child questions; a festival conflicts with work; a newcomer asks "why?"; a lapsed member returns; tradition requires sacrifice of comfort; an elder's teaching feels outdated; a rule seems arbitrary; a tradition-practice is inconvenient; the community asks more than feels fair; a sacred text contradicts itself; a ritual feels empty; duty conflicts with desire; tradition demands forgiveness of someone who hurt you; the community judges someone you love.

**Category 2 — Compassion Dilemmas (15+):** An outsider needs help but tradition says "not our concern"; a tradition-rule causes suffering; a marginalised member seeks belonging; a tradition-punishment seems cruel; mercy conflicts with justice-code; an enemy's child is hungry; tradition says "shun" but love says "embrace"; a heretic is also kind; the tradition's history includes violence; a convert wants in but hasn't completed requirements; tradition says "endure" but someone is breaking; compassion for animals conflicts with ritual; a minority is excluded by tradition; the poor are told to tithe; a widow breaks mourning rules from necessity.

**Category 3 — Evidence Dilemmas (15+):** Science contradicts a sacred story; archaeology challenges tradition-history; psychology explains away a sacred experience; a miracle has a natural explanation; the tradition's founding narrative has historical problems; a prophecy fails; a sacred text's authorship is questioned; evolution vs. creation; other traditions have identical myths; the tradition borrowed from earlier traditions; a sacred site is proven misidentified; a saint's biography is hagiographic; prayer studies show no effect; near-death experiences have neurological explanations; the tradition's cosmology is factually wrong.

**Category 4 — Other-Tradition Dilemmas (20+):** A wise person from another tradition offers compelling truth; a friend converts away; an interfaith marriage; a child wants to explore other paths; another tradition handles suffering better; another tradition's mystics describe the same experience; a tradition-enemy shows genuine holiness; syncretism tempts; a universal truth appears in all traditions; your tradition persecuted theirs; their tradition has something yours lacks; competing truth-claims about the afterlife; another tradition's ethics seem more compassionate; a tradition you dismissed shows unexpected depth; your tradition's uniqueness claims are challenged; a secular person lives more ethically; another tradition's art moves you; a convert from your tradition seems happier; interfaith dialogue reveals uncomfortable similarities; a shared sacred site creates conflict.

**Category 5 — Deepening Dilemmas (15+):** A mystic within your tradition challenges the orthodox reading; doubt arises and won't leave; a dark night of the soul; the tradition's God seems silent; faith feels like habit not living truth; a teacher says "go deeper" but deeper is frightening; the tradition's esoteric branch beckons; a crisis reveals faith was shallower than believed; the tradition asks you to grow past comfort; a paradox demands holding not resolving; the tradition's shadow becomes visible; a reformer is compelling; the letter of the law kills the spirit; a sacred experience contradicts doctrine; maturity requires releasing certainty.

## 5. Technical Requirements

**Input Types:** Multiple-choice selection (4 options per dilemma). Optional free-text "why" after choice (feeds language-reflective cross-validation).

**Timing:** No time pressure on choice. Consequence plays out over 15–30 seconds. Total session paced by player.

**NPC/AI:** Narrative characters who present dilemmas with emotional authenticity. Not interactive — they present and receive.

**LLM:** Generates dilemma variations based on player's tradition-stream. Adapts emotional weight and specificity. Scores optional "why" responses.

**State Persistence:** Choice-pattern history (which shadow-quadrant choices cluster in), dilemma-difficulty level, tradition-stream, time-to-choose as difficulty proxy, cross-session trajectory.
