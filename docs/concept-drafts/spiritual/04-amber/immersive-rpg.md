# Spiritual / Amber — Immersive-RPG Game Concept

> **Axis:** Ecological faith — whether mythic-membership faith appears spontaneously in free-play.  **Why this axis for this module:** The truest measure of integrated faith is whether it emerges *unprompted* — when the player is free to do anything, do they spontaneously enact tradition, seek sacred meaning, maintain communal belonging, or does faith only appear when structured games demand it?

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Title:** The Living Tradition

**Core Mechanic:** Open-world RPG segment where the player inhabits a tradition-rich world with sacred sites, communal gatherings, ritual opportunities, and competing worldviews. No explicit faith-tasks are assigned — the player's spontaneous choices reveal their relationship to mythic-membership faith ecologically.

**Duration:** 10–20 minutes per session (player-determined).

**Internal Progression:** (1) World familiarisation → (2) Tradition-opportunities appear naturally → (3) Competing worldviews present themselves → (4) Faith-crises emerge from narrative → (5) Integration tested through sustained free-choice.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Unique Presentation:** A living world — village, temple, marketplace, wilderness — where tradition is woven into the environment. Sacred bells ring at prayer-times. Community gathers for festivals. Elders tell stories. Other traditions exist across the river. Nothing is forced.

**Differs From Others:** No structured measurement (deterministic), no prompted reflection (language-reflective), no presented dilemmas (scenario-choice), no guided body-practice (embodied-somatic), no planning interface (strategic), no cooperative tasks (social). Pure ecological emergence.

**Uniquely Surfaces:** DA through spontaneous policing of NPCs' faith, avoiding other-tradition areas, spending all time in temple. DAll through ignoring all sacred opportunities, treating tradition-NPCs dismissively, never entering sacred spaces. GA through seeking "higher" spiritual content (the hermit on the mountain, the forbidden library) while ignoring communal practice. GAll through rigid routine (always at temple, never exploring) combined with avoidance of the other-tradition quarter.

**Successful Integration:** Player engages tradition naturally — visits sacred spaces, participates in communal life, AND explores freely, encounters other traditions with curiosity, allows doubt without crisis.

## 3. Game Design

**Setup:** Player's avatar wakes in a tradition-village. The day unfolds with natural rhythms — morning bells, market activity, evening gathering. Sacred and secular opportunities coexist. No quest-markers for faith-content.

**Interaction Phases:**
1. **Orientation** — explore the world, discover what exists (sacred sites, community, wilderness, other-tradition areas)
2. **Rhythm** — daily cycle repeats; does the player develop sacred rhythm spontaneously?
3. **Encounter** — NPCs from other traditions appear naturally; how does the player respond?
4. **Crisis** — a narrative event challenges the tradition (drought, invasion, schism); what does the player do?
5. **Sustained Choice** — over multiple sessions, what pattern emerges in the player's free behaviour?

**Feedback Examples:**
1. Natural tradition-engagement + curiosity about others → world feels alive, NPCs warm
2. Obsessive temple-attendance + hostility to outsiders → world narrows, NPCs become fearful
3. Complete avoidance of sacred spaces → world feels empty, NPCs distant
4. Seeking only esoteric/advanced content → ground becomes unstable, community NPCs confused
5. Rigid routine without exploration → world becomes repetitive, stagnation indicators

**Difficulty Adaptation:** World complexity increases — more traditions present, more ambiguous situations, more compelling alternatives to tradition, more severe crises. The "easy" path (pure conformity or pure avoidance) becomes less rewarding.

**Internal Progression Table:**

| Level | World State | Shadow Probed | Drive Engaged |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Simple tradition-village | Baseline (what does player do freely?) | All (ecological) |
| 2 | Other-tradition presence | DA/DAll (approach/avoidance) | Communion/Agency |
| 3 | Internal tradition-diversity | GAll (rigidity within tradition) | Eros (curiosity) |
| 4 | Crisis/disruption | All four (stress-response) | All four |
| 5 | Sustained multi-session | Integration pattern | All four (ecological confirmation) |

## 4. Item Pool

**Category 1 — Sacred Spaces (20+):** Village temple, forest shrine, river-blessing site, ancestor-hall, meditation cave, hilltop altar, community prayer-room, sacred tree, holy well, tradition-school, elder's teaching-seat, festival ground, burial/cremation site, initiation chamber, pilgrimage waypoint, dawn-watching spot, sacred garden, tradition-library, bell-tower, healing-spring.

**Category 2 — Tradition-NPCs (15+):** Village priest/priestess, teaching elder, devout grandmother, questioning youth, tradition-keeper, ritual-musician, sacred-artist, community cook (feast-preparer), tradition-healer, story-teller, pilgrim-guide, novice-in-training, lapsed-but-returning member, tradition-scholar, festival-organiser.

**Category 3 — Other-Tradition Elements (15+):** Traders from across the river with different prayers, a wandering monk of another path, books in another tradition's script, music from another sacred tradition drifting over the wall, an interfaith marketplace, a convert who left, a convert who arrived, shared sacred symbols with different meanings, a neutral sacred site both traditions claim, a philosopher who questions all traditions, a secular humanist village nearby, art depicting another tradition's saints, a child asking "why is their God different?", a festival that overlaps with another tradition's holy day, a healer who uses another tradition's methods.

**Category 4 — Free-Choice Opportunities (20+):** Attend morning prayer or sleep in, join festival or explore wilderness, help tradition-elder or help secular merchant, enter other-tradition quarter or stay home, read sacred text or read adventure story, fast on fast-day or eat, wear tradition-markers or dress neutrally, teach a child the tradition or let them choose, repair the temple or repair the bridge, attend the funeral rite or grieve alone, join the pilgrimage or stay and work, observe the sabbath or finish the project, welcome the stranger or protect the boundary, question the elder or accept the teaching, seek the hermit or stay with community.

**Category 5 — Ecological Crisis Events (15+):** Drought (is it divine punishment?), invasion (does tradition provide courage?), plague (does faith hold?), schism (which faction?), prophet-figure arrives (follow or reject?), sacred site damaged (rebuild or relocate?), youth rebellion (adapt or hold firm?), miracle-claim (believe or investigate?), tradition-leader falls (faith in institution shaken?), other-tradition offers alliance (accept or refuse?), sacred text lost (memory or despair?), convert wants to join (welcome or test?), tradition declared illegal (practice secretly or comply?), abundance (gratitude or entitlement?), peace (deepen practice or become complacent?).

## 5. Technical Requirements

**Input Types:** Standard RPG movement/interaction (walk, talk, use, enter, observe). No faith-specific inputs — all measurement is behavioural tracking.

**Timing:** Real-time world with day/night cycle (compressed). Sessions are player-terminated. Multiple sessions required for ecological pattern-detection.

**NPC/AI:** Full NPC ecosystem with schedules, personalities, and reactive dialogue. NPCs remember player's choices across sessions. Community-mood system reflects player's faith-behaviour.

**LLM:** Powers NPC dialogue, generates contextual narrative events, adapts world-state to player's emerging pattern. Does NOT prompt faith-behaviour — only creates opportunities.

**State Persistence:** Behavioural heat-map (where player spends time), sacred-engagement frequency, other-tradition approach/avoidance ratio, crisis-response patterns, spontaneous-ritual frequency, NPC-relationship states, multi-session trajectory, ecological shadow-convergence scores.
