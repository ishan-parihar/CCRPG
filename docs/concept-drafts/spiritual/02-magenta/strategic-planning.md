# Spiritual / Magenta — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** The strategic-planning axis probes spiritual intelligence through SACRED PLANNING — can the player plan around sacred obligations? At Magenta, this is: "The spirits need an offering tomorrow. How will you prepare? What steps will you take to honour the sacred?"
>
> **Why this axis for Spiritual/Magenta:** Before systematic religious practice (Amber+), sacred planning is PROTO-RITUAL — "I need to bring the offering, find the special place, do the thing at the right time." This modality tests whether the player can organise behaviour around sacred commitments — the executive dimension of spiritual intelligence.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Sacred Preparation"
- **Core mechanic:** The player must PLAN and EXECUTE sacred actions — gathering offerings, finding sacred places, timing rituals. Not spontaneous sacred feeling but ORGANISED sacred behaviour. "The spirits need three things. How will you get them? In what order? When?"
- **Duration:** 3-5 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** The First Offering → The Sacred Sequence → The Many Preparations → The Changing Ritual → The Living Keeper

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst:** "The sacred needs something from you. Not just feeling — DOING. An offering. A preparation. A ritual. Can you plan for the sacred? Can you organise your actions around what matters most?" The contact boundary is: "Can you plan around sacred obligations?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* How does the player plan for the sacred? The Spirit-Addict over-plans ("I'll bring EVERYTHING! Ten offerings! A hundred preparations!"). The Spirit-Denier won't plan ("Why would I plan for nothing? There's nothing to prepare for"). The Premature Priest plans EFFICIENTLY ("Fastest route to the offering. Minimum effort, maximum power"). The Sacred-Refuser begins planning then abandons ("I was going to prepare but… it doesn't matter").
- *Emergent:* Can they plan for the sacred with appropriate care — neither obsessive nor dismissive?

**Integration path:** Rewards APPROPRIATE SACRED PLANNING — organising behaviour around the numinous with care and proportion. Not obsessive, not dismissive, not efficient-only, not abandoned.

**Successful integration:** The player can plan around sacred obligations — the executive foundation of spiritual intelligence.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Sacred Preparation: a ritual-planning space. The player must organise steps to complete a sacred action — gather offerings, find the right place, time the ritual. The aesthetic: Magenta-stage preparation-altar — a workspace where sacred items are gathered and arranged. Steps glow when completed correctly.

### Interaction
- **The First Offering (1-5):** One-step sacred plan. "Bring one thing to the sacred place." Single action.
- **The Sacred Sequence (5-15):** Multi-step: gather → carry → place → invoke. Correct order matters.
- **The Many Preparations (15-30):** Multiple sacred obligations; prioritise and sequence.
- **The Changing Ritual (30-50):** Sacred requirements CHANGE; adapt the plan mid-execution.
- **The Living Keeper (50+):** Full sacred planning: proportionate + reverent + adaptive + spontaneous.

### Feedback
- Proportionate plan → "Perfect. Just enough. The spirits are pleased. You prepared with care."
- Over-planning → "Enough! The spirits don't need all that. Just the offering. With presence. That's enough."
- Won't plan → "Just one thing. Pick something up. Bring it here. That's sacred planning. Simple."
- Efficient-only → "Slower. With care. Not fast — REVERENT. The sacred wants your attention, not your speed."
- Abandons → "Just the first step. You started! That counts. One step toward the sacred. Enough for now."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Steps: 1 → 2-3 → 4-5 → adaptive sequences
- Constraints: unlimited time → time-awareness → competing demands
- Ambiguity: clear requirements → unclear → must discern what sacred needs
- Social: solo → collaborative → competing sacred obligations
- Adaptation: fixed plan → plan must change mid-execution

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The First Offering | 1-5 | Single-step sacred plan |
| The Sacred Sequence | 5-15 | Multi-step sequencing |
| The Many Preparations | 15-30 | Multiple sacred obligations |
| The Changing Ritual | 30-50 | Adaptive sacred planning |
| The Living Keeper | 50+ | Full sacred planning integration |

---

## 4. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Simple offerings:** One-step sacred actions
- **Sequenced rituals:** Multi-step sacred actions (order matters)
- **Competing obligations:** Multiple sacred needs; must prioritise
- **Adaptive rituals:** Requirements change mid-plan
- **Collaborative rituals:** Sacred planning with others

### Minimum pool size
- 20+ simple, 20+ sequenced, 15+ competing, 10+ adaptive, 10+ collaborative

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Over-plans → dark-addiction; won't plan → dark-allergy
- Optimises only → golden-addiction; abandons → golden-allergy

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Tap (select items/steps); drag (sequence steps); hold (confirm plan)

### Timing requirements
- Planning time tracked; execution time tracked; no time pressure but efficiency noted

### NPC/AI requirements
- Sacred requirement generation (what the ritual needs)
- Step-sequencing system (correct order tracking)
- Proportionality assessment (too much/too little/just right)
- Difficulty adaptation (steps, constraints, ambiguity)

### LLM requirements
- **Low-Medium:** Proportionality and reverence assessment. Core sequencing algorithmic.

### State persistence
- Planning history; proportionality scores; sequencing accuracy; adaptability quality; reverence ratings; obsession indicators; refusal indicators; efficiency-only indicators; abandonment indicators; drive/shadow signals; fatigue state; checkpoint position
