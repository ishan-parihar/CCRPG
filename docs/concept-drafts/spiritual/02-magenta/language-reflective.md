# Spiritual / Magenta — Language-Reflective Game Concept

> **Axis:** The language-reflective axis probes spiritual intelligence through VERBAL SACRED EXPRESSION — can the player name, describe, and narrate their relationship to the numinous? At Magenta, this is: "What do you call the sacred? How do you talk to the spirits? What words do you use for what matters most?"
>
> **Why this axis for Spiritual/Magenta:** Before systematic theology (Amber+), sacred language is ANIMISTIC — "the spirits," "the glow," "the special feeling." This modality tests whether the player can articulate their numinous experience in magical-narrative language.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Spirit-Words"
- **Core mechanic:** The player names and describes their sacred experience. Not explaining theology — NAMING the numinous. "What do you call it? The thing that glows? The feeling of something more? Give it a name. Tell it something. What would you say to the sacred?"
- **Duration:** 2-4 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** The First Word → The Many Words → The Spirit-Story → The Changing Word → The Living Prayer

---

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst:** "The sacred is here. You can feel it. Now — what do you call it? What name does it have? What would you say to it? Tell me about the spirits." The contact boundary is: "Can you articulate your numinous experience?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* How does the player verbalise the sacred? The Spirit-Addict floods with sacred language ("The spirits are everywhere! They speak to me always! Everything is holy!"). The Spirit-Denier has no sacred vocabulary ("I don't know what you mean. There's nothing to name"). The Premature Priest uses power-language ("The strong spirit. The one that gives power"). The Sacred-Refuser begins naming then stops ("It's like a… no, I can't say it").
- *Emergent:* Can they name the sacred with appropriate specificity — neither flooding nor empty?

**Integration path:** Rewards AUTHENTIC SACRED LANGUAGE — naming the numinous genuinely, with specificity and feeling. Not flooding, not empty, not power-oriented, not avoidant.

**Successful integration:** The player can articulate their numinous experience — the verbal foundation of spiritual intelligence.

---

## 3. Game Design

### Setup
The Spirit-Words: a sacred articulation space. The player encounters numinous content and must NAME it, DESCRIBE it, SPEAK to it. The aesthetic: Magenta-stage word-altar — sacred things that await naming. When named, they respond (glow brighter, move, acknowledge).

### Interaction
- **The First Word (1-5):** "What do you call it?" Name one sacred thing. Single word.
- **The Many Words (5-15):** Name multiple sacred things. Build sacred vocabulary.
- **The Spirit-Story (15-30):** Tell a story about the sacred. Narrative sacred language.
- **The Changing Word (30-50):** Name the sacred in DIFFERENT contexts (happy/sad/scared).
- **The Living Prayer (50+):** Full sacred articulation: name + describe + address + narrate + pray.

### Feedback
- Authentic naming → "Yes! That's its name. You named it. The sacred heard you. It glows for you."
- Flooding → "Quieter. Not all of them. Just THIS one. One name. The real one. What's ITS name?"
- Empty → "Just a feeling-word. Warm? Bright? Soft? … Yes! That's a sacred word. Your first one."
- Power-only → "Not the strong one. The gentle one. What do you call the quiet glow? The soft sacred?"
- Approach-withdrawal → "You almost said it! Say it. Quick. Don't think. What was the word? … Yes! That's it!"

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Vocabulary demand: single word → phrase → sentence → narrative
- Specificity: general ("the glow") → specific ("the warm golden feeling in the old tree")
- Context: neutral → emotional → challenging
- Novelty: familiar sacred → novel sacred → ambiguous sacred
- Social: private naming → shared naming → mutual sacred vocabulary

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The First Word | 1-5 | Single sacred naming |
| The Many Words | 5-15 | Multiple sacred names; vocabulary building |
| The Spirit-Story | 15-30 | Narrative sacred language |
| The Changing Word | 30-50 | Context-sensitive sacred vocabulary |
| The Living Prayer | 50+ | Full sacred articulation integration |

---

## 4. Item Pool

### Item types
- **Naming prompts:** "What do you call this?" (sacred stimuli requiring names)
- **Description prompts:** "What is it like?" (sacred stimuli requiring description)
- **Address prompts:** "What would you say to it?" (sacred stimuli requiring speech-to)
- **Narrative prompts:** "Tell me about it" (sacred stimuli requiring story)
- **Context prompts:** "What do you call it when you're [emotion]?" (context-sensitive naming)

### Minimum pool size
- 25+ naming, 20+ description, 15+ address, 15+ narrative, 10+ context

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Floods with sacred words → dark-addiction; no sacred vocabulary → dark-allergy
- Power-language only → golden-addiction; begins then stops → golden-allergy

---

## 5. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Voice/text (sacred naming); tap-to-select (from word-options for younger players); gesture (pointing while naming)

### Timing requirements
- Response latency tracked (hesitation = uncertainty); no time pressure; verbal fluency noted

### NPC/AI requirements
- Sacred stimulus generation (numinous content requiring naming)
- Vocabulary tracking system (sacred words used, consistency, growth)
- Authenticity assessment (genuine vs. performative sacred language)
- Difficulty adaptation (vocabulary demand, specificity, context)

### LLM requirements
- **Very High:** Core assessment is linguistic. LLM evaluates sacred vocabulary authenticity, specificity, emotional grounding, inflation patterns, and growth quality. Generates responsive sacred content based on player's vocabulary.

### State persistence
- Sacred vocabulary inventory; naming accuracy; specificity scores; authenticity ratings; range metrics; consistency quality; flooding indicators; emptiness indicators; power-bias indicators; avoidance indicators; drive/shadow signals; fatigue state; checkpoint position
