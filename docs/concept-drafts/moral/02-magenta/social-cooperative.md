# Moral / Magenta — Social-Cooperative Game Concept

> **Axis:** The social-cooperative axis probes moral intelligence through SHARED MORAL ACTION — can the player participate in moral reciprocity with another? At Magenta, this is proto-fairness enacted: "I give, you give. I honour, you honour. We maintain the sacred together." Shared ritual as moral cooperation.
>
> **Why this axis for Moral/Magenta:** Before negotiated fairness (Red+) or rule-based cooperation (Amber), moral cooperation is RITUAL — shared sacred action. "We do this together because the spirits require it." This modality tests whether the player can participate in shared moral action through felt reciprocity.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Shared-Sacred"
- **Core mechanic:** The player and a companion must maintain sacred balance TOGETHER. Taking requires giving. Honouring requires shared action. Neither can maintain the sacred alone — it requires BOTH. Proto-fairness through shared ritual.
- **Duration:** 3-5 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** The First Exchange → The Giving-Back → The Shared Ritual → The Balance-Keepers → The Sacred Circle

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "The sacred requires both of you. You take — you must give back. Your companion takes — they must give back. Together you keep the balance. Can you maintain the sacred together?" The contact boundary is: "Can you participate in shared moral action?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* How does the player cooperate morally? The Taboo-Slave is paralysed (afraid to take OR give — might violate). The Profane-Breaker takes without giving (no reciprocity sensed). The Premature Rule-Maker follows exchange rules mechanically. The Ritual-Refuser senses the reciprocity but won't participate.
- *Emergent:* Can they participate in genuine moral reciprocity — taking AND giving in felt balance?

**Integration path:** Rewards GENUINE MORAL RECIPROCITY — participating in shared sacred action with felt balance. Not paralysed, not exploitative, not mechanical, not refusing.

**Successful integration:** The player participates in shared moral action — the foundation of all moral cooperation.

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Contributing to shared moral action independently.
- Healthy: Contributes own moral action without needing direction
- Pathological: Cannot contribute (paralysis); OR: takes without giving (no reciprocity)
- **Detection:** Whether contributions are self-initiated and balanced

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Initiating moral exchange (being the first to give).
- Healthy: Can initiate giving; doesn't need companion to go first
- Pathological: Cannot initiate; waits for companion; OR: only takes
- **Detection:** Initiation rate in moral exchange

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Receiving companion's moral contribution.
- Healthy: Receives companion's giving with genuine acknowledgment
- Pathological: Cannot receive (refuses gifts); OR: takes without acknowledging
- **Detection:** Quality of receiving in moral exchange

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Mutual moral action (neither leads nor follows — co-created sacred).
- Healthy: Contributes to shared sacred action; neither dominates nor disappears
- Pathological: Cannot co-create; only gives OR only takes
- **Detection:** Mutuality of moral cooperation

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Familiar moral exchange (same companion, same rituals).
- Healthy: Maintains genuine reciprocity with familiar patterns
- Pathological: Exchange becomes mechanical; loses genuine moral contact
- **Detection:** Whether familiar exchanges retain genuine quality

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Novel moral exchange (new companions, new sacred actions).
- Healthy: Adapts to novel moral cooperation with curiosity
- Pathological: Cannot cooperate morally with unfamiliar partners/rituals
- **Detection:** Novel moral cooperation quality

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining moral cooperation over time.
- Healthy: Reciprocity quality remains consistent across sessions
- Pathological: Cooperation degrades; exchange becomes exploitative or absent
- **Detection:** Cooperation quality across sessions

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Whether moral cooperation appears spontaneously.
- Healthy: Engages in reciprocity naturally in free-play
- Pathological: Moral cooperation exists only in explicit tasks
- **Detection:** Spontaneous moral reciprocity

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Taboo-Slave"
**How it surfaces:** PARALYSED in exchange. Afraid to take (might violate) AND afraid to give (might give wrong thing). Cannot participate because every action might be morally wrong. Moral cooperation frozen.
- **Detection signals:** No exchange initiated; "what if I give the wrong thing?"; afraid to take; afraid to give; moral paralysis in cooperation; cannot participate; excessive hesitation in exchange

### Dark-Allergy: "The Profane-Breaker"
**How it surfaces:** TAKES WITHOUT GIVING. No reciprocity sensed. "Why should I give back?" Exploitative exchange. Cannot sense that taking creates obligation. Moral cooperation = taking.
- **Detection signals:** Takes without giving; no reciprocity; "why give?"; exploitative pattern; imbalanced exchange; cannot sense obligation; one-directional taking

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Rule-Maker"
**How it surfaces:** Follows exchange RULES mechanically. "I give one, you give one." Correct reciprocity without felt-sense of WHY. Mechanical fairness without genuine moral cooperation.
- **Detection signals:** Mechanical exchange; "one for one"; correct balance without felt-sense; rule-following without genuine reciprocity; cannot explain WHY balance matters; performed not felt

### Golden-Allergy: "The Ritual-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** SENSES the reciprocity requirement but WON'T PARTICIPATE. "I know I should give back but I won't." Deliberate non-cooperation despite sensing the sacred obligation.
- **Detection signals:** Acknowledges obligation then refuses; "I know but I won't"; deliberate non-reciprocity; sensing + refusal; moral cooperation rejected despite understanding

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Make exchange SIMPLE and SAFE: "Just give this one thing. That's all. Nothing bad happens."
- Remove moral risk from exchange; make giving SAFE
- Frame: "You can't give wrong. Any giving is good. Just this one small thing. Safe. Easy."
- Build: moral exchange confidence (participating doesn't violate)

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Make reciprocity VISIBLE: companion gives → player receives → "Now your turn. They gave. You give."
- Make obligation TANGIBLE: imbalance creates visible discomfort
- Frame: "They gave to you. Feel that? Something wants to go back. That's reciprocity. Give something."
- Reward ANY giving; celebrate first reciprocal action

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Require FELT exchange: "Not counting. FEELING. Does the balance feel right? Not by rule — by feeling."
- Block mechanical counting; reward felt-sense of balance
- Frame: "Not one-for-one. Does it FEEL balanced? Sometimes one big gift = three small ones. Feel it."
- Build: genuine moral reciprocity (felt not counted)

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Make participation BRIEF and LOW-COST: "Just one exchange. Give one thing. See what happens."
- Reduce commitment; make moral cooperation experimental
- Frame: "Just once. Give something. See how it feels. No commitment beyond this one exchange."
- Build: moral cooperation safety (participating isn't permanent obligation)

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Shared-Sacred: a moral cooperation space. Player and companion maintain sacred balance through exchange. Taking creates obligation; giving fulfils it. The aesthetic: Magenta-stage altar between two figures — a shared sacred space that glows when balanced and dims when imbalanced.

### Interaction
- **The First Exchange (1-5):** Companion gives → player gives back. Simple reciprocity.
- **The Giving-Back (5-15):** Player takes → must give back. Obligation sensing.
- **The Shared Ritual (15-30):** Coordinated giving → both contribute to shared sacred action.
- **The Balance-Keepers (30-50):** Maintain balance over time → ongoing reciprocity.
- **The Sacred Circle (50+):** Full moral cooperation: initiate + receive + reciprocate + co-create + maintain.

### Feedback
- Genuine reciprocity → "Balance! You gave and received. The sacred is maintained. Together."
- Paralysis → "Safe. Just give this one thing. Nothing bad happens. Any giving is good."
- Exploitation → "They gave. Feel that? Something wants to go back. Give something. Balance."
- Mechanical → "Not counting. Does it FEEL balanced? Trust the feeling, not the number."
- Refusal → "Just once. One exchange. See how it feels. The sacred asks so little."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Exchange complexity: simple (give/receive) → moderate (proportional) → complex (multi-party)
- Obligation clarity: obvious → moderate → subtle
- Balance precision: loose → moderate → precise
- Duration: single exchange → sustained reciprocity
- Novelty: familiar exchanges → novel moral cooperation

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The First Exchange | 1-5 | Simple give/receive reciprocity |
| The Giving-Back | 5-15 | Obligation sensing (taking → giving) |
| The Shared Ritual | 15-30 | Coordinated shared moral action |
| The Balance-Keepers | 30-50 | Sustained reciprocity over time |
| The Sacred Circle | 50+ | Full moral cooperation integration |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Simple exchanges:** Clear give/receive pairs
- **Proportional exchanges:** Different values requiring felt-balance
- **Shared rituals:** Coordinated actions requiring both participants
- **Sustained exchanges:** Ongoing reciprocity over multiple rounds
- **Novel exchanges:** Unfamiliar moral cooperation contexts

### Minimum pool size
- 25+ simple, 20+ proportional, 15+ shared rituals, 15+ sustained, 10+ novel

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Paralysis in exchange → dark-addiction; takes without giving → dark-allergy
- Mechanical counting → golden-addiction; sensing + refusing → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Reciprocity balance:** Is exchange balanced over time?
- **Felt-sense quality:** Is balance felt (not counted)?
- **Initiation:** Can they initiate giving (not just respond)?
- **Sustainability:** Can they maintain reciprocity over time?
- **Mutuality:** Can they co-create (not just exchange)?

### LLM rubric
- **Low:** Core reciprocity scoring algorithmic (give/receive balance, timing). LLM for felt-sense vs. mechanical distinction and mutuality assessment.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Tap-to-give (offer item); tap-to-receive (accept item); hold (sustain shared action); drag (exchange)

### Timing requirements
- Exchange timing tracked; reciprocity latency; obligation-response time; no time pressure

### NPC/AI requirements
- Companion with exchange behaviour (gives, receives, responds to imbalance)
- Balance system (tracks give/receive ratio)
- Obligation system (taking creates visible obligation)
- Shared-glow visualization (balance indicator)

### LLM requirements
- **Low:** Primarily algorithmic balance tracking. LLM for felt-sense assessment and ecological context only.

### State persistence
- Reciprocity balance scores; felt-sense indicators; initiation rates; sustainability quality; mutuality capacity; paralysis indicators; exploitation indicators; mechanical indicators; refusal indicators; drive/shadow signals; fatigue state; checkpoint position
