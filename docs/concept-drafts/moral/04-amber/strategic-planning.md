# Moral / Amber — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** Planning moral action — structuring vow-keeping, sequencing moral obligations, maintaining the code across time and competing demands.  **Why this axis for this module:** Conventional morality requires SUSTAINED commitment — the capacity to plan how to keep vows, sequence duties, and maintain moral integrity across complex life-demands.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Title:** The Keeper's Ledger
**Core Mechanic:** Player must plan and sequence moral obligations across time — allocating limited resources (time, energy, attention) to maintain multiple vows simultaneously. Planning reveals shadow through over-structuring, abandonment, bypassing, or calcification.
**Duration:** 5-9 minutes per session, infinite checkpoint.
**Internal Progression:** Single-vow maintenance → multi-vow sequencing → competing-obligation triage → moral planning under scarcity → adaptive planning (code-revision without collapse).

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Unique Presentation:** A planning interface where moral obligations are resources to be allocated across time. Not rule-application (deterministic), not verbal (language-reflective), not dilemma-choice (scenario), not body (embodied), not group (social), not free-play (immersive). Pure moral-temporal planning.
**Differs From Others:** The PLANNING HORIZON is the catalyst. How does the player structure moral commitment across time? What gets prioritised, what gets dropped, what gets over-planned?
**Uniquely Surfaces:** DA through over-structured moral plans (every obligation maximised, no flexibility, no rest); DAll through absent moral planning (no vow-maintenance, no obligation-tracking); GA through planning "ethical frameworks" rather than concrete vow-keeping; GAll through rote planning (same structure always, no adaptation to new circumstances).
**Successful Integration:** Player can plan moral commitment realistically — maintains vows, sequences duties, adapts when circumstances change, without rigidity or abandonment.

## 3. Game Design

**Setup:** Player enters "The Keeper's Study" — a planning space where moral obligations appear as commitments requiring time, energy, and attention across a timeline.

**Interaction Phases:**
1. **Single Vow** — Maintain one moral commitment across time (baseline planning)
2. **Multi-Vow** — Maintain 3-5 commitments simultaneously (sequencing capacity)
3. **Competing Obligations** — Obligations conflict in timing — triage required (priority)
4. **Scarcity Planning** — Not enough resources for all obligations — what gives? (moral realism)
5. **Adaptive Planning** — Circumstances change mid-plan — restructure without collapse (flexibility)

**Feedback Examples:**
1. Realistic, sustainable moral plan → ledger glows with steady light
2. Over-structured plan (no margin, no rest) → ledger cracks under weight
3. Absent planning (obligations untracked) → ledger pages go blank
4. Abstract "framework" without concrete actions → ledger floats, unanchored
5. Rigid plan that cannot adapt → ledger becomes brittle when circumstances shift

**Difficulty Adaptation:** Obligation count and complexity scale with demonstrated planning capacity. Scarcity and adaptation challenges only appear after baseline multi-vow competence. Resource constraints tighten gradually.

**Internal Progression Table:**

| Level | Obligations | Resources | Disruptions | Shadow Probed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 vow | Abundant | None | Baseline |
| 2 | 3-5 vows | Adequate | Minor | DAll |
| 3 | 5-7 competing | Limited | Moderate | DA/GAll |
| 4 | 7+ under scarcity | Scarce | Significant | All |
| 5 | Adaptive replanning | Variable | Major shifts | GA/DA |

## 4. Item Pool

**Vow Types (20+):** Daily practice commitment, weekly service obligation, truth-telling maintenance, promise to specific person, group attendance duty, elder-care responsibility, tradition-keeping, resource-sharing schedule, protection duty, mentoring commitment, ritual maintenance, debt-repayment plan, boundary-holding, community service, witnessing obligation, loyalty maintenance, honour-keeping, purity practice, order-maintenance, forgiveness process.

**Resource Types (15+):** Time blocks, energy units, attention capacity, emotional bandwidth, physical presence, financial resources, social capital, willpower reserves, rest requirements, creative energy, patience stores, courage reserves, compassion capacity, focus duration, relational availability.

**Disruption Types (20+):** Unexpected demand on time, energy depletion, competing emergency, relationship crisis, health challenge, opportunity that conflicts with duty, authority changes the rules, group shifts expectations, new obligation appears, old obligation intensifies, resource loss, support withdrawal, context change, priority conflict, deadline compression, capacity reduction, temptation to abandon, external pressure to over-commit, circumstances that invalidate a vow, new information that changes moral landscape.

**Planning Strategies (15+):** Sequential scheduling, priority ranking, buffer allocation, delegation, renegotiation, minimum-viable-commitment, batch processing, ritual anchoring, accountability structures, contingency planning, graceful degradation, sustainable pacing, periodic review, adaptive restructuring, moral triage.

**Adaptation Scenarios (15+):** Vow becomes impossible — restructure or release? New obligation more important — reprioritise? Resources halved — what survives? Context changed — does the vow still apply? Group changed the code — adapt your plan? You grew — old vow feels small — honour or evolve? Failure occurred — repair plan? Burnout approaching — sustainable restructure? Competing goods — which gets more? Someone needs you to break schedule — flex or hold? The plan worked but feels dead — revitalise? External crisis — emergency moral triage? Success created new obligations — expand or hold? The plan serves others but depletes you — adjust? Long-term vow meets short-term crisis — navigate?

## 5. Technical Requirements

**Input Types:** Drag-and-drop scheduling, priority sliders, resource allocation bars, timeline manipulation, tap-to-commit/release.
**Timing:** Planning phase (untimed, reflective) + execution phase (time-pressured as obligations come due). Cross-session plan persistence.
**NPC/AI:** The Keeper — an archivist figure who presents obligations and tracks plan integrity. Neutral, not judgmental.
**LLM:** Generates novel disruption scenarios based on player's planning patterns. Adapts obligation complexity. Evaluates plan realism (not moral judgment — structural assessment).
**State Persistence:** Active vow registry, plan history, resource allocation patterns, disruption-response patterns, planning-style trajectory, shadow-signal accumulation, cross-session obligation tracking.
