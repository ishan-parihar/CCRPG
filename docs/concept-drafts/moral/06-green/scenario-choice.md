# Moral / Green — Scenario-Choice Game Concept

> **Axis:** Moral dilemmas where care conflicts with justice, and contextual sensitivity conflicts with ethical commitment.  **Why this axis for this module:** Green moral capacity is tested most directly when the player must choose under genuine tension between valid moral orientations — choice reveals shadow.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Name:** The Crossroads of Care
**Core loop:** Player navigates branching moral scenarios where each choice-point pits care against justice, context against principle, or commitment against openness. No "correct" answer — but choice patterns reveal shadow structures. Consequences unfold across linked scenarios.
**Session length:** 6–12 minutes (1–2 full scenarios per session).
**Infinite checkpoint:** Save between any choice-point; resume mid-scenario.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst frequency:** Green moral — committed contextual ethics under tension.
**Shadow provocation:** DA surfaces as choice-avoidance (selecting "both are valid" options, delaying, or choosing the least-committal path). DAll surfaces as consistent justice-over-care choices regardless of context. GA surfaces as meta-ethical choices ("I'd step back and see the bigger picture") that avoid genuine engagement. GAll surfaces as refusal to acknowledge that some choices lead to better developmental outcomes.
**Drive probing:** Eros — scenarios where growth requires choosing the harder moral path. Agape — scenarios where care demands returning to relational ground. Agency — scenarios requiring sovereign moral commitment. Communion — scenarios requiring genuine joining with the other's moral world.
**Integration path:** Diagnosis (baseline choice patterns across care-justice spectrum) → Healing (scenarios that make shadow-choices feel their consequences) → Evolution (scenarios requiring genuine care-justice synthesis in a single choice).

## 3. Game Design

**Mechanic:** Narrative scenarios with 3–5 choice-points each. Each choice-point offers 2–4 options that map onto care/justice/avoidance/bypass dimensions. Choices have narrative consequences that unfold in subsequent scenes — the player experiences the downstream effects of their moral orientation.
**Adaptive difficulty:** Early scenarios have clear care-justice tension; later scenarios introduce: (a) multiple stakeholders with conflicting care-claims, (b) justice principles that conflict with each other, (c) cultural contexts that reframe the dilemma, (d) time pressure that forces commitment.
**Consequence engine:** Choices propagate — a justice-heavy choice in scenario A affects relationships in scenario B. Player sees how their moral orientation shapes a moral ecology over time.
**Shadow confrontation:** After 5+ scenarios, the system presents a "mirror scenario" — structurally identical to an earlier one but with reversed contextual cues. Consistent shadow shows as identical choice despite changed context.

## 4. Item Pool

**Pool structure:** 80+ branching scenarios across relational domains (family, friendship, civic, professional, ecological, stranger, institutional, existential). Each scenario has 3–5 choice-points with mapped dimensions.
**Scenario types:** (a) Care-justice direct conflict — helping one person harms the principle. (b) Context-shift dilemmas — same act, different cultural meaning. (c) Commitment tests — must choose NOW despite incomplete information. (d) Ranking provocations — one moral framework clearly produces better outcomes.
**Consequence trees:** Each scenario has 4–8 possible endings based on choice-path. Endings are not "good/bad" but reveal different shadow ecologies.
**Rotation:** Scenarios cycle; no full repeat within 60 days. Mirror-scenarios appear 10–20 sessions after their original.

## 5. Technical Requirements

**Input:** Tap/click choice selection. Minimum 44px touch targets. Choices presented as brief text cards (≤25 words each).
**Timing:** Optional soft time-pressure on later scenarios (visible timer, no forced timeout). Time-to-choose recorded as secondary metric.
**Telemetry:** Per-choice dimensional mapping (care/justice/avoidance/bypass), choice latency, revision attempts, consequence-awareness (does player reference prior outcomes?), mirror-scenario consistency score.
**Adaptive engine:** Choice-pattern analysis across 5+ scenarios detects shadow orientation. Scenario selection targets under-probed shadow quadrants. Consequence severity scales with detected shadow rigidity.
**Accessibility:** All scenarios available as text + optional audio narration; high-contrast choice cards; no reliance on colour for moral mapping.
