# Moral / Orange — Immersive-RPG Game Concept

> **Axis:** Ecological moral reasoning — whether principled ethics appears spontaneously in free-play.  **Why this axis for this module:** The ultimate test of Orange moral capacity is whether principled reasoning emerges naturally in unstructured situations, without prompting or framing — the examined moral life lived, not performed.

---

## 1. Game Identity

**Title:** The Examined World  
**Genre:** Open-world RPG with embedded moral ecology  
**Session length:** 8–15 minutes (infinite checkpoint)  
**Core loop:** Free exploration → moral situations arise organically → player acts → consequences unfold naturally → pattern scored across sessions

The player inhabits a world where moral situations are woven into the ecology rather than presented as explicit dilemmas. NPCs have competing needs, systems have unjust structures, and the player's actions have downstream moral consequences — but nothing is framed as "a moral choice." The game measures whether principled reasoning appears spontaneously.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

**Catalyst frequency:** Orange moral — ecological principled reasoning without scaffolding  
**Shadow surfacing:** All four shadows surface naturally. DA when player optimises world-systems without considering persons. DAll when player follows quest-markers without examining their moral content. GA when player performs moral sophistication for NPC approval. GAll when player applies rigid rules regardless of NPC circumstances.  
**Progression:** Diagnosis (sessions 1–5) → Healing (6–12) → Evolution (13+)

Healing catalyst: world consequences that make shadows visible — the calculator's optimised system harms specific NPCs; the conformist's rule-following perpetuates injustice; the relativist's non-commitment allows harm; the fortress's rigidity breaks relationships. Evolution catalyst: world-states where principled reasoning + contextual sensitivity yields flourishing.

## 3. Game Design

**Mechanics:**
- Open world with 3 interconnected communities, each with internal moral tensions
- NPCs with persistent needs, relationships, and moral positions
- Player actions have delayed consequences (2–5 sessions later)
- No explicit moral framing — situations arise from world-state, not prompts
- Scoring: pattern analysis across 10+ sessions identifies moral reasoning style

**World ecology:**
- Community A: unjust law benefits majority, harms minority (social-contract reasoning test)
- Community B: competing rights claims between groups (universal-rights application test)
- Community C: efficient system that dehumanises individuals (utility vs. dignity test)
- Cross-community: player's actions in one community affect others (systemic moral reasoning)

**Shadow-specific mechanics:**
- DA: world offers optimisation tools that treat NPCs as resources
- DAll: quest-givers offer clear rules that perpetuate injustice
- GA: NPCs reward sophisticated moral language regardless of action
- GAll: situations where player's stated principles conflict with NPC welfare

## 4. Item Pool

- 3 community world-maps with 15 NPCs each (45 total persistent characters)
- 120 organic moral situations (40 per community, arising from world-state)
- 60 delayed-consequence chains (player action → 2–5 session delay → visible outcome)
- NPC relationship matrices: actions toward one NPC affect others
- 30 systemic-injustice structures embedded in world mechanics
- Adaptive world-state: community tensions escalate based on player's moral patterns

## 5. Technical Requirements

- Persistent world-state engine: tracks NPC needs, relationships, and community health across sessions
- Delayed-consequence system: queues outcomes for future sessions
- Pattern-analysis scorer: identifies moral reasoning style from unstructured behaviour over 10+ sessions
- NPC relationship graph: models how actions propagate through social networks
- Ecological moral-situation generator: creates situations from world-state rather than scripted triggers
- Drive-health mapping: behavioural patterns → DA/DAll/GA/GAll signatures (requires large sample)
- Compound shadow detection: cross-references with other modality data for convergent evidence
