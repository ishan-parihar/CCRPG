# Moral / Red — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** The strategic-planning axis probes moral intelligence through MULTI-STEP CONSEQUENCE PLANNING — sequencing moral actions across time, managing competing self-interests over multiple moves, and anticipating how today's moral choice affects tomorrow's options.
>
> **Why this axis for Moral/Red:** At Red, moral planning is 2-step and SELF-INTERESTED: "If I take now, what happens next? If I wait, do I get more later?" This axis isolates the TEMPORAL component of moral intelligence — can you think ahead in consequence-space?

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Consequence-Planner"
- **Core mechanic:** The player plans moral sequences — which actions to take when, how to position for future advantage, and how to manage competing self-interests across a multi-step campaign of choices.
- **Duration:** 4-7 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** 2-Step Plans → 3-Step Plans → Competing Timelines → Adaptive Plans → The Long Game

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "You can take now. Or wait. Or take some now and some later. Plan your moves." The contact boundary is: "Can you THINK AHEAD in moral-consequence space?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* Do they plan at all (or just react to each consequence)? Do they over-plan (golden-addiction)? Refuse to plan because "consequences are unpredictable" (dark-allergy)? Plan only exploitation sequences (dark-addiction)?
- *Emergent:* Can they hold one more consequence-step in mind? Can they delay gratification for larger reward?

**Integration path:** Rewards EXECUTED moral plans (not just conceived). A simple 2-step plan that works scores higher than a complex 5-step plan that collapses.

**Successful integration:** The player conceives and executes 2-3 step moral-consequence plans appropriate to Red capacity.

---

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Plan suggested by an NPC vs. player's own plan.
- Healthy: Evaluates suggestions; uses good ones
- Pathological: Rejects all moral planning advice; "I plan my own moves"
- **Detection:** Rejection rate independent of suggestion quality

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Open moral planning — "Design your strategy for this sequence of choices."
- Healthy: Generates moral plans with confidence
- Pathological: Cannot plan without template; "Tell me what to do first"
- **Detection:** Self-generated moral plan quality

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Joint planning — player and ally must coordinate moral strategies.
- Healthy: Integrates ally's needs into own plan
- Pathological: Abandons own moral strategy to match ally's; "Whatever you think"
- **Detection:** Whether joint plans reflect player's moral sovereignty

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Teaching an NPC to plan moral sequences.
- Healthy: Scaffolds the NPC's moral planning
- Pathological: Plans for them; cannot let others develop moral strategy
- **Detection:** Genuine scaffolding vs. imposition

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Mastered 2-step plans vs. advancing to 3-step.
- Healthy: Enjoys clean execution; advances when ready
- Pathological: Demands longer moral sequences immediately; "This is too simple"
- **Detection:** Satisfaction at current plan-complexity

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Plans requiring trust of uncertain outcomes.
- Healthy: Attempts with appropriate caution
- Pathological: Claims complex moral foresight without demonstration
- **Detection:** Gap between planned and executed moral complexity

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Simple 1-step moral "plans" (barely planning).
- Healthy: Executes with full commitment
- Pathological: Dismisses; "That's not real planning"
- **Detection:** Engagement quality on below-level plans

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Moral planning appearing in free-play.
- Healthy: Plans consequences spontaneously in other contexts
- Pathological: Moral planning exists only in structured challenges
- **Detection:** Spontaneous moral planning outside task contexts

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Predator"
**How it surfaces:** All moral plans are EXPLOITATION SEQUENCES — "take X now, use it to take Y later, then take Z." Every plan ends with maximum extraction. No plan includes fair exchange or sustainability.
- **Detection signals:** All plans terminate in maximum extraction; cannot plan mutual-benefit outcomes; "sustainability" never appears in plans; every step serves exploitation

### Dark-Allergy: "The Paralysed"
**How it surfaces:** Cannot plan morally because they cannot COMMIT to a first step. "What if the first choice is wrong?" Plans are never executed because the first action feels too risky.
- **Detection signals:** Plans conceived but never executed; first-step avoidance; "What if..." loops; cannot commit to a moral sequence; plans that never begin

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Saint"
**How it surfaces:** Over-complex moral plans for simple situations. Plans a 5-step "ethical strategy" when a simple 2-step consequence-chain would suffice. Elaborate "everyone wins" architectures that collapse under execution.
- **Detection signals:** Over-complex plans for simple problems; elaborate moral architectures that fail; cannot choose the simple path; plans that sound sophisticated but don't execute

### Golden-Allergy: "The Cynic"
**How it surfaces:** Plans that NEVER extend beyond the immediate. "Why plan? Consequences are random." Cannot conceive of moral sequences because they refuse to believe consequences are predictable.
- **Detection signals:** All plans are single-step; refuses multi-step moral planning; "You can't predict what will happen"; no temporal moral reasoning

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Introduce plans where SUSTAINABLE extraction produces more than one-shot exploitation
- Frame: "You took everything. Now there's nothing left to take. The planner who leaves some has more NEXT time."
- Reward sustainability over maximum-extraction plans

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Introduce REVERSIBLE first steps (plans where the first action can be undone if wrong)
- Frame: "Try this. If it doesn't work, you can undo it. No permanent cost."
- Remove commitment-fear from the first step

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Reward MINIMUM-STEP solutions (fewest moral moves needed to achieve the goal)
- Frame: "Two steps. That's all you need. Simpler is stronger."
- Penalise unnecessary moral-planning complexity

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Introduce PREDICTABLE consequence-chains (outcomes are 100% certain; planning is easy)
- Frame: "This ALWAYS happens when you do X. Plan around it. It's reliable."
- Start with perfectly predictable moral sequences; build planning confidence

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Consequence-Planner: a campaign of moral choices across time. The player must plan which actions to take when, managing resources, relationships, and consequences across a multi-step sequence. Each step's consequences affect the next step's options. The aesthetic: Red-stage war-campaign where every raid, every trade, every alliance has consequences that ripple forward.

### Interaction
- **2-Step Plans (1-5):** "Take now → lose access later" OR "wait now → gain more later." Simple delayed gratification.
- **3-Step Plans (5-15):** "Take X → use X to get Y → use Y to get Z." Longer consequence-chains.
- **Competing Timelines (15-30):** "Path A gives more in 2 steps; Path B gives more in 4 steps. Which timeline?"
- **Adaptive Plans (30-50):** Mid-plan, consequences shift. Revise the moral strategy.
- **The Long Game (50+):** Complex multi-step moral campaigns with competing timelines, uncertain outcomes, and adaptive demands.

### Feedback
- Plan executed → "Your foresight paid off. Each step served the next."
- Plan collapsed → "The chain broke. What consequence did you miss?"
- Over-complex → "Two steps would have done it. You planned six. Simpler wins."
- Exploitation-only → "You planned to take everything. But the source is gone now. What's your plan for NEXT time?"

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Plan length: 2 → 3 → 4 steps
- Consequence certainty: certain → probable → uncertain
- Competing timelines: none → 2 → 3+
- Adaptation demand: static → changing → adversarial

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| 2-Step Plans | 1-5 | Simple delayed gratification; certain outcomes |
| 3-Step Plans | 5-15 | Longer chains; some uncertainty |
| Competing Timelines | 15-30 | Multiple paths; must choose which timeline |
| Adaptive Plans | 30-50 | Mid-plan changes; must revise |
| The Long Game | 50+ | Full complexity: chains + timelines + adaptation |

---

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Delayed-gratification sequences:** Now vs. later trade-offs across steps
- **Consequence-chain puzzles:** Multi-step cause→effect requiring planning
- **Timeline comparisons:** Competing paths with different payoff curves
- **Disruption scenarios:** Mid-plan changes requiring revision
- **Sustainability puzzles:** Plans that must account for resource renewal

### Minimum pool size
- 30+ 2-step, 25+ 3-step, 20+ competing-timeline, 15+ adaptive, 10+ long-game

### Drive/shadow mapping
- All-exploitation plans → dark-addiction; never-execute plans → dark-allergy
- Over-complex plans → golden-addiction; single-step-only → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Plan completion:** % of moral plans successfully executed to conclusion
- **Consequence-prediction accuracy:** Did planned consequences actually occur?
- **Timeline optimisation:** Quality of path-selection in competing-timeline scenarios
- **Adaptation speed:** How quickly moral plans revise after disruption
- **Sustainability:** Do plans account for future resource availability?

### LLM rubric
- **Medium:** Scenario generation, plan evaluation, consequence-chain narration. Core plan-execution scoring algorithmic.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Drag-and-drop (arrange moral actions in sequence); tap-to-select (choose timeline); sequence input (order moral moves)

### Timing requirements
- No millisecond precision; planning phase untimed; execution phase has generous windows

### NPC/AI requirements
- Environment with consistent consequence-rules (predictable at early levels)
- Disruption events at advanced levels (mid-plan changes)
- Resource systems that deplete and renew (sustainability testing)

### LLM requirements
- **Medium:** Scenario generation, plan evaluation narration, consequence-chain construction. Core scoring algorithmic.

### State persistence
- Plan completion history; consequence-prediction accuracy; timeline optimisation quality; adaptation speed; sustainability patterns; exploitation/paralysis/over-complexity/single-step patterns; drive/shadow signals; fatigue state; checkpoint position
