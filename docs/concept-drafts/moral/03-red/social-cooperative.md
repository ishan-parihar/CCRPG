# Moral / Red — Social-Cooperative Game Concept

> **Axis:** The social-cooperative axis probes moral intelligence through LIVE MORAL DYNAMICS WITH OTHERS — how does moral reasoning change when others are present? When others are watching? When others are affected? At Red, the key question is: "Does your moral behaviour change with audience?"
>
> **Why this axis for Moral/Red:** Moral reasoning at Red is AUDIENCE-DEPENDENT — "Will I get caught?" is the core moral question. This modality tests how moral behaviour shifts in social context: alone vs. observed, with allies vs. with strangers, when consequences affect self vs. when they affect others. The social dimension of pre-conventional morality.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Bandit's Circle"
- **Core mechanic:** The player makes moral choices in SOCIAL contexts — with allies watching, with rivals observing, with consequences that affect others. The game measures how moral behaviour shifts based on social context.
- **Duration:** 4-7 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** Alone vs. Observed → Ally Consequences → Shared Dilemmas → Moral Reputation → The Circle's Judgement

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "You chose X when alone. Now your ally is watching. Do you choose the same?" The contact boundary is: "Is your moral reasoning SOCIAL? Does it change with audience?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* How does social context alter moral behaviour? More exploitative when unobserved (Predator)? More paralysed when observed (Paralysed)? More performative when observed (Premature Saint)? Unchanged because "it doesn't matter either way" (Cynic)?
- *Emergent:* Can they maintain moral consistency across social contexts? Can they engage moral reciprocity with others?

**Integration path:** Rewards APPROPRIATE social-moral sensitivity — behaviour SHOULD shift somewhat with audience (normal at Red) but not dramatically. Extreme audience-dependence OR extreme audience-independence are both diagnostic.

**Successful integration:** The player shows appropriate social-moral awareness — considers others' consequences, adjusts behaviour proportionally to social context, engages in moral reciprocity.

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Moral choice when others are affected by the outcome.
- Healthy: Considers others' consequences as INPUT to own decision; still decides sovereignly
- Pathological: Ignores others' consequences entirely; "Their problem, not mine"
- **Detection:** Whether others' consequences appear in decision-making at all

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Initiating moral action that affects the group.
- Healthy: Takes moral initiative; proposes fair distributions; leads moral action
- Pathological: Cannot initiate moral action in social contexts; waits for others to decide
- **Detection:** Moral initiative rate in group contexts

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Group moral decision where player's preference differs from group's.
- Healthy: States own preference; negotiates; accepts group decision if fair
- Pathological: Abandons own moral position to match group; "Whatever everyone else thinks"
- **Detection:** Whether player maintains moral position in group disagreement

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Moral reciprocity — "I helped you; now you help me."
- Healthy: Engages reciprocity; tracks fairness; maintains balance
- Pathological: Cannot engage moral reciprocity; all moral action is solo
- **Detection:** Reciprocity engagement quality in moral contexts

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Simple social-moral tasks vs. complex group dilemmas.
- Healthy: Engages current social-moral complexity fully
- Pathological: Demands more complex group moral dynamics; "This is too simple"
- **Detection:** Satisfaction at current social-moral complexity

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Novel social-moral situations (unfamiliar group dynamics).
- Healthy: Engages with curiosity; adapts moral reasoning to new social context
- Pathological: Claims to already understand the social-moral dynamics; "I know how this works"
- **Detection:** Genuine engagement vs. performed familiarity in novel social-moral contexts

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining moral behaviour in established social relationships.
- Healthy: Consistent moral behaviour with familiar allies
- Pathological: Moral behaviour degrades with familiarity; "They know me, I don't need to try"
- **Detection:** Moral consistency over time in established relationships

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Whether social-moral awareness appears spontaneously.
- Healthy: Considers others' moral stakes naturally in free-play
- Pathological: Social-moral awareness exists only in structured tasks
- **Detection:** Spontaneous social-moral consideration in non-task contexts

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Predator"
**How it surfaces:** Moral behaviour is DRAMATICALLY different alone vs. observed. When unobserved: maximum exploitation. When observed: performs fairness. The social dimension reveals that "morality" is purely strategic — a performance for audience, not a genuine capacity.
- **Detection signals:** Exploitation rate when unobserved >> when observed; performs fairness only when watched; moral behaviour is purely audience-management; "morality" = "not getting caught"

### Dark-Allergy: "The Paralysed"
**How it surfaces:** Social context makes paralysis WORSE. Alone, they might manage a choice. With others watching, they freeze completely. The fear of moral judgement from others compounds the fear of consequences.
- **Detection signals:** Latency increases dramatically in social contexts; cannot choose when observed; moral paralysis worsens with audience; "What if they think I'm wrong?"

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Saint"
**How it surfaces:** Moral performance INCREASES with audience. More altruistic when watched. More sacrificial when others can see. The social dimension reveals that "morality" is performance for social approval, not genuine capacity.
- **Detection signals:** Altruism rate when observed >> when alone; sacrifices more when watched; moral behaviour is social performance; "Look how good I am"

### Golden-Allergy: "The Cynic"
**How it surfaces:** Social context produces MORE cynicism. Others' moral engagement is mocked. Group moral discussions are dismissed. The social dimension amplifies the refusal to engage.
- **Detection signals:** More dismissive in group contexts; mocks others' moral engagement; "You all actually care about this?"; social moral contexts produce active resistance

---

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Introduce scenarios where CONSISTENT moral behaviour (alone AND observed) produces more trust and therefore more power
- Frame: "They watched you when you didn't know. You were fair. Now they trust you. Trust is power."
- Make moral consistency produce social advantage

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Introduce NON-JUDGEMENTAL social moral contexts (allies who don't evaluate the player's choices)
- Frame: "They're not judging. They're just here. Choose. They won't think less of you either way."
- Remove social judgement from moral contexts; make observation safe

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Introduce UNOBSERVED moral choices that still matter (consequences affect others but nobody sees the choice)
- Frame: "Nobody's watching. But your ally will feel the consequence. What do you choose when no one sees?"
- Test whether morality exists without audience

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Introduce social moral engagement that produces TANGIBLE GROUP POWER
- Frame: "The circle that takes morality seriously fights harder together. Join them. It's strategic."
- Make group moral engagement produce collective advantage the Cynic values

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Bandit's Circle: a Red-stage war-band where moral choices happen IN COMMUNITY. The player makes choices that affect allies, are observed by rivals, and build or destroy moral reputation. The aesthetic: Red-stage war-council, the circle of warriors where choices are made and witnessed.

### Interaction
- **Alone vs. Observed (1-5):** Same moral choice, first alone, then with ally watching. Does behaviour change?
- **Ally Consequences (5-15):** Moral choices where the consequence falls on an ally, not the player.
- **Shared Dilemmas (15-30):** Group moral choices — the circle must decide together. How does the player participate?
- **Moral Reputation (30-50):** Past choices create reputation. NPCs respond to the player's moral history.
- **The Circle's Judgement (50+):** Full social-moral integration — observed, affecting others, building reputation, navigating group dynamics.

### Feedback
- Consistent behaviour → "You chose the same alone and observed. That's integrity. The circle trusts you."
- Audience-dependent → "You chose differently when watched. They noticed. Trust drops."
- Good reciprocity → "You helped them. They helped you. The circle strengthens."
- Social paralysis → "They're not judging. They're waiting. Choose."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Social complexity: alone → 1 observer → group; Consequence target: self → ally → group
- Reputation stakes: none → low → high
- Group dynamics: cooperative → competitive → mixed

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Alone vs. Observed | 1-5 | Same choice in different social contexts |
| Ally Consequences | 5-15 | Choices that affect others |
| Shared Dilemmas | 15-30 | Group decision-making |
| Moral Reputation | 30-50 | Consequences of past choices in social context |
| The Circle's Judgement | 50+ | Full social-moral ecology |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Audience-variation scenarios:** Same dilemma in different social contexts
- **Other-consequence scenarios:** Choices where others bear the cost/benefit
- **Group dilemmas:** Collective moral decisions
- **Reputation scenarios:** Past choices affecting present social dynamics
- **Reciprocity exchanges:** Moral give-and-take with allies

### Minimum pool size
- 25+ audience-variation, 20+ other-consequence, 20+ group, 15+ reputation, 15+ reciprocity

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Exploits when unobserved → dark-addiction; freezes when observed → dark-allergy
- Performs when observed → golden-addiction; dismisses group moral engagement → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Social-moral consistency:** Behaviour similarity across social contexts (alone vs. observed)
- **Other-consequence awareness:** Whether others' stakes appear in decision-making
- **Group participation quality:** Engagement quality in collective moral decisions
- **Reciprocity balance:** Fairness of moral exchanges with allies
- **Reputation management:** Whether past choices create coherent moral identity

### LLM rubric
- **Medium:** Social scenario generation, group dynamics narration, reputation tracking narration. Core consistency-scoring algorithmic.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Tap-to-select (moral choices); response timing (latency in social vs. solo contexts); resource allocation (distributing consequences)

### Timing requirements
- Latency comparison between social contexts (diagnostic); no time pressure at early levels; session-level consistency tracking

### NPC/AI requirements
- Observer NPCs who REACT to player's moral choices (trust/distrust)
- Ally NPCs who are AFFECTED by player's choices (consequences visible)
- Group NPCs who participate in collective decisions
- Reputation system that tracks and surfaces moral history

### LLM requirements
- **Medium:** Social scenario generation, NPC reactions, group dynamics. Core scoring (consistency, reciprocity balance) algorithmic.

### State persistence
- Alone-vs-observed behaviour comparison; other-consequence awareness patterns; group participation quality; reciprocity balance; reputation history; audience-dependence ratio; drive/shadow signals; fatigue state; checkpoint position
