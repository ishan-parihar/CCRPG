# Somatic / Amber — Language-Reflective Game Concept

> **Axis:** The language-reflective axis probes somatic intelligence through VERBAL ARTICULATION of body-experience — can the player NAME what their body is doing? Describe the form? Articulate the felt-sense of discipline? At Amber, this is: "Can you put words to the form your body follows?"
>
> **Why this axis for Somatic/Amber:** The disciplined body at Amber is often PRE-VERBAL — the body knows the form but the person cannot articulate it. This modality tests whether somatic intelligence has reached verbal-metacognitive awareness: "I am holding because... The form requires... My body feels..."

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Body's Testimony"
- **Core mechanic:** The player must ARTICULATE their somatic experience — describe what their body is doing, name the form, explain the discipline, report felt-sense during movement. Not just DO the form but SPEAK the form.
- **Duration:** 3-5 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** The Named Hold → The Described Form → The Spoken Breath → The Witnessed Rhythm → The Living Voice

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "Your body holds the stance. Now: what does it feel like? Where is the effort? What is the form? Can you SPEAK what your body knows?" The contact boundary is: "Can you articulate your somatic experience?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* How does the player relate to body-language? The Rigid Driller describes form MECHANICALLY (no felt-sense, only rules). The Undisciplined Body cannot describe anything (no body-awareness to articulate). The Premature Virtuoso uses TECHNICAL language beyond experience ("proprioceptive engagement" without actual feeling). The Form-Refuser describes familiar forms well but cannot articulate novel body-experience.
- *Emergent:* Can they articulate body-experience genuinely — with both accuracy and felt-sense?

**Integration path:** Rewards GENUINE SOMATIC ARTICULATION — words that match body-experience, descriptions that demonstrate real awareness, language that bridges body and mind.

**Successful integration:** The player can articulate their somatic experience — the verbal-metacognitive dimension of embodied discipline.

---

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Articulating body-experience independently (no prompts/scaffolding).
- Healthy: Generates somatic descriptions independently; owns body-language
- Pathological: Cannot describe without prompts; OR: describes mechanically without feeling
- **Detection:** Whether somatic articulation is self-generated and genuine

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Articulating NOVEL body-experience (unfamiliar forms).
- Healthy: Finds words for new body-sensations; language grows with experience
- Pathological: Cannot articulate novel experience; language frozen in familiar
- **Detection:** Language quality for novel somatic experience

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Hearing others' somatic descriptions and relating.
- Healthy: Recognises shared body-experience in others' words
- Pathological: Cannot relate to others' descriptions; OR: adopts others' language without own experience
- **Detection:** Whether others' somatic language resonates genuinely

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Co-creating somatic language (building shared vocabulary).
- Healthy: Contributes to shared body-language; enriches collective vocabulary
- Pathological: Cannot contribute; only receives or only imposes
- **Detection:** Quality of collaborative somatic articulation

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Articulation consistency over time.
- Healthy: Somatic vocabulary grows steadily; descriptions deepen
- Pathological: Language stagnates; same descriptions repeated
- **Detection:** Vocabulary growth trajectory

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Growing articulation complexity (subtler body-experiences).
- Healthy: Language reaches for subtler sensations with appropriate effort
- Pathological: Cannot grow; OR: uses complex language without matching experience
- **Detection:** Whether language complexity matches somatic complexity

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining articulation capacity across sessions.
- Healthy: Returns with maintained somatic vocabulary
- Pathological: Vocabulary degrades; cannot access previous descriptions
- **Detection:** Cross-session vocabulary stability

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Whether somatic articulation appears spontaneously.
- Healthy: Describes body-experience naturally in non-articulation contexts
- Pathological: Articulation exists only when explicitly prompted
- **Detection:** Spontaneous somatic language

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Rigid Driller"
**How it surfaces:** Describes form MECHANICALLY — rules, steps, positions — but NO felt-sense. "Left foot forward. Right arm up. Hold." No body-awareness in the language. The description is a manual, not a testimony.
- **Detection signals:** Mechanical language only; no feeling-words; describes positions not sensations; "step 1, step 2, step 3"; no interoceptive vocabulary; body-as-machine language

### Dark-Allergy: "The Undisciplined Body"
**How it surfaces:** CANNOT DESCRIBE body-experience. No somatic vocabulary. "I don't know. It just... I can't explain." Body-awareness too low for articulation.
- **Detection signals:** "I don't know"; empty descriptions; cannot name body-parts involved; no felt-sense language; very short responses; "it's just movement"

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Virtuoso"
**How it surfaces:** Uses TECHNICAL/ADVANCED language without matching experience. "I'm engaging my proprioceptive system" (without actual proprioceptive awareness). Language exceeds body-experience.
- **Detection signals:** Technical vocabulary beyond demonstrated capacity; uses terms without experiential grounding; language doesn't match performance; "I feel the subtle energy" (while failing basic form)

### Golden-Allergy: "The Form-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** Articulates FAMILIAR body-experience well but CANNOT describe novel sensations. Language frozen in known territory.
- **Detection signals:** Rich description of familiar forms; empty/frozen language for novel forms; "I can't describe this new one"; vocabulary doesn't extend to new experience; avoids articulating unfamiliar

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Prompt for FEELING: "You described the position. Now: what does it FEEL like? Where is the effort? Is it warm? Heavy? Tight?"
- Reward feeling-language: "Good. 'Heavy in the shoulders.' That's your body speaking."
- Frame: "Not just what the form IS. What it FEELS LIKE. Your body has a voice beyond the manual."
- Build: felt-sense vocabulary (body-as-experience not body-as-machine)

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Start with SIMPLE body-naming: "Where do you feel it? Point. Now name that place."
- Provide vocabulary scaffolding: "Is it: tight? loose? warm? cool? heavy? light?"
- Frame: "Just one word. Where does your body feel this? One word is enough."
- Build: basic somatic vocabulary (any articulation is progress)

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Require EXPERIENTIAL GROUNDING: "You said 'proprioceptive.' Show me. Where exactly? What does it feel like in simple words?"
- Block technical language without experience: "Simpler. What do you ACTUALLY feel?"
- Frame: "Simple words that match real feeling. Not textbook language. YOUR body's words."
- Build: grounded articulation (language matches experience)

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Prompt for NOVEL description: "This is new. What does your body notice? Even one word."
- Reward any attempt at novel articulation
- Frame: "New form, new feeling. Your body is having a new experience. What's one word for it?"
- Build: expanding vocabulary (new experiences get new words)

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Body's Testimony: a reflective space where the player articulates somatic experience. After performing a form (or during a hold), the player is asked to DESCRIBE what their body is experiencing. The aesthetic: Amber-stage confessional/testimony space — quiet, intimate, the body's voice honoured.

### Interaction
- **The Named Hold (1-5):** Hold a stance → "Where do you feel it? Name the place."
- **The Described Form (5-15):** Reproduce a form → "Describe what your body just did. How did it feel?"
- **The Spoken Breath (15-30):** Breath-coupled movement → "What happens in your body when breath and movement join?"
- **The Witnessed Rhythm (30-50):** Synchrony → "What does it feel like to move with the beat? Where does the rhythm live in your body?"
- **The Living Voice (50+):** Full integration: articulate complex somatic experience with genuine felt-sense vocabulary.

### Feedback
- Genuine felt-sense → "Your body speaks. 'Heavy in the legs, warm in the chest.' That's real. That's yours."
- Mechanical only → "You described the position. Good. Now deeper: what does it FEEL like?"
- Cannot describe → "Just one word. Where? What? Even 'tight' or 'warm' is enough."
- Technical without experience → "Simpler. Forget the textbook. What do YOU feel? Right now?"
- Frozen at novel → "New feeling. New word. Even 'strange' or 'different' counts. What's there?"

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Articulation demand: single word → phrase → sentence → paragraph
- Experience complexity: simple hold → form → breath-coupling → synchrony → dual-task
- Scaffolding: multiple-choice → fill-in → open response
- Novelty: familiar forms → slightly novel → entirely new
- Depth: location → sensation → quality → meaning

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The Named Hold | 1-5 | Name where body feels effort |
| The Described Form | 5-15 | Describe form-experience in sentences |
| The Spoken Breath | 15-30 | Articulate breath-body coupling |
| The Witnessed Rhythm | 30-50 | Describe synchrony experience |
| The Living Voice | 50+ | Full somatic articulation integration |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Location prompts:** "Where do you feel it?" (body-mapping)
- **Sensation prompts:** "What does it feel like?" (quality naming)
- **Process prompts:** "What happened in your body?" (movement description)
- **Comparison prompts:** "How is this different from the last form?" (discrimination)
- **Integration prompts:** "What does your body want to say?" (open articulation)

### Minimum pool size
- 20+ location, 20+ sensation, 15+ process, 10+ comparison, 10+ integration

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Mechanical-only language → dark-addiction; cannot describe → dark-allergy
- Technical without experience → golden-addiction; frozen at novel → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Vocabulary range:** Number of distinct somatic terms used
- **Felt-sense depth:** Presence of genuine experiential language (vs. mechanical)
- **Accuracy:** Does description match observed body-behaviour?
- **Novelty articulation:** Can they describe new body-experiences?
- **Spontaneity:** Does somatic language appear without prompting?

### LLM rubric
- **Very High:** Core assessment requires natural language understanding. LLM evaluates: genuineness of felt-sense language, vocabulary depth, accuracy of description vs. observed behaviour, mechanical vs. experiential quality, technical-without-grounding detection.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Voice/text (somatic descriptions); tap (body-map location); selection (scaffolded vocabulary)

### Timing requirements
- Response latency tracked (time to articulate); no time pressure but spontaneity noted

### NPC/AI requirements
- Somatic vocabulary assessment (range, depth, genuineness)
- Body-map system (player indicates location of sensation)
- Scaffolding system (provides vocabulary options when needed)
- Mechanical-vs-experiential classifier
- Technical-without-grounding detector

### LLM requirements
- **Very High:** Evaluates natural language somatic descriptions
- Assesses genuineness (felt-sense vs. mechanical vs. technical-bypass)
- Tracks vocabulary growth over time
- Generates appropriate prompts based on demonstrated capacity
- Detects shadow patterns in language quality

### State persistence
- Somatic vocabulary history; felt-sense depth scores; accuracy metrics; novelty articulation quality; spontaneity indicators; mechanical-language frequency; empty-response frequency; technical-bypass frequency; frozen-vocabulary indicators; drive/shadow signals; checkpoint position
