# Somatic / Amber — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** The strategic-planning axis probes somatic intelligence through MULTI-STEP BODY-PLANNING — can the player plan a sequence of movements, anticipate body-demands, and execute a somatic strategy? At Amber, this is: "The drill has five positions. Plan your body's path. What comes first? Where will fatigue hit? How do you prepare?"
>
> **Why this axis for Somatic/Amber:** Disciplined movement requires PLANNING — the kata has an order, the drill has phases, the body must be prepared for what comes next. This modality tests whether the player can think AHEAD about their body within a rule-governed movement system.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Drill-Master's Path"
- **Core mechanic:** The player must PLAN multi-step body-sequences — anticipate fatigue, sequence movements efficiently, prepare the body for upcoming demands. Not just executing form but STRATEGISING about form.
- **Duration:** 3-5 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** The First Sequence → The Planned Drill → The Fatigue Map → The Changing Drill → The Master Strategist

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "The drill has phases. Your body has limits. Plan: which form first? Where will fatigue hit? How do you sequence to maintain quality throughout? The wise practitioner plans the body's path." The contact boundary is: "Can you plan multi-step somatic sequences strategically?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* How does the player plan body-sequences? The Rigid Driller plans the SAME sequence every time (no adaptation). The Undisciplined Body cannot plan at all (impulsive execution). The Premature Virtuoso plans overly complex sequences. The Form-Refuser plans only familiar sequences.
- *Emergent:* Can they plan body-sequences wisely — anticipating fatigue, sequencing efficiently, adapting to changing demands?

**Integration path:** Rewards WISE SOMATIC PLANNING — sequences that honour body-limits, anticipate fatigue, and maintain quality throughout.

**Successful integration:** The player can plan multi-step body-sequences strategically — the executive dimension of embodied discipline.

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Planning body-sequences independently.
- Healthy: Plans own drill sequence with body-awareness; anticipates own fatigue
- Pathological: Cannot plan independently; OR: plans rigidly without body-awareness
- **Detection:** Whether somatic planning is self-generated and body-aware

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Planning under uncertainty (unknown fatigue, novel forms in sequence).
- Healthy: Makes reasonable body-plans under uncertainty; adapts as information emerges
- Pathological: Cannot plan without certainty; freezes at unknown body-demands
- **Detection:** Planning quality under somatic uncertainty

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Planning when others' sequences affect yours (shared space/timing).
- Healthy: Plans own sequence while accounting for others' movement
- Pathological: Ignores others' sequences; OR: plans entirely around others (loses own)
- **Detection:** Whether own somatic plan accounts for social context

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Collaborative drill-planning (sequencing together).
- Healthy: Contributes to shared drill-plan; accounts for others' body-limits
- Pathological: Cannot collaborate on body-planning; dominates or defers
- **Detection:** Quality of collaborative somatic planning

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Planning consistency over time.
- Healthy: Plans consistently improve; learns from body-feedback
- Pathological: Plans don't improve; same mistakes repeated
- **Detection:** Planning learning trajectory

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Growing planning complexity (longer drills, more constraints).
- Healthy: Plans grow in complexity as capacity grows
- Pathological: Cannot grow plans; OR: plans exceed body-capacity
- **Detection:** Planning growth quality

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining planning capacity across sessions.
- Healthy: Returns with maintained planning ability
- Pathological: Planning capacity degrades; must relearn sequencing
- **Detection:** Cross-session planning stability

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Whether somatic planning appears in daily movement.
- Healthy: Plans body-sequences naturally (stairs, carrying, exercise)
- Pathological: Planning exists only in explicit drill contexts
- **Detection:** Spontaneous somatic planning

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Rigid Driller"
**How it surfaces:** Plans the SAME sequence every time regardless of body-state. Cannot adapt plan to current fatigue, energy, or context. The plan is fixed — the body must conform.
- **Detection signals:** Same sequence regardless of body-state; cannot modify plan; "this is the order"; ignores fatigue in planning; plan never changes; body forced to conform to rigid plan

### Dark-Allergy: "The Undisciplined Body"
**How it surfaces:** CANNOT PLAN. Executes movements impulsively without sequencing. No anticipation of fatigue. No strategy — just impulse.
- **Detection signals:** No plan before execution; random sequence order; surprised by fatigue; "I'll just do whatever"; no anticipation; impulsive movement-selection

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Virtuoso"
**How it surfaces:** Plans OVERLY COMPLEX sequences beyond body-capacity. 8-phase drills when 4-phase is unstable. Elaborate strategies that fail in execution.
- **Detection signals:** Plans exceed capacity; elaborate sequences that collapse; "I can handle a longer drill"; 4-phase accuracy <85% but plans 6-8 phase; strategy exceeds execution

### Golden-Allergy: "The Form-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** Plans only FAMILIAR sequences. Cannot plan novel drills. Strategy frozen in known territory.
- **Detection signals:** Plans only familiar sequences; cannot strategise for novel forms; "I know this drill"; avoids planning new sequences; strategy doesn't extend to unfamiliar

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Present situations where BODY-STATE should change the plan: "You're fatigued. The usual sequence will fail at phase 3. Adapt: what's the wise order TODAY?"
- Reward adaptive planning: "You changed the sequence for today's body. That's strategic wisdom."
- Frame: "The wise drill-master reads the body FIRST, then plans. Today's body, today's plan."
- Build: adaptive somatic planning (plan serves body, not body serves plan)

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Start with 2-STEP planning: "Two forms. Which first? Think: which is harder? Do the harder one while fresh."
- Make planning BRIEF and IMMEDIATELY USEFUL
- Frame: "Just two steps. Which first? That's planning. Simple. Powerful. Your body benefits."
- Build: basic somatic sequencing (any planning is progress)

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- LOCK complexity until current plans succeed: "Your 4-phase plan must execute at >90% before 5-phase unlocks."
- Reward successful execution of current-level plans
- Frame: "A plan that fails isn't strategic. Master this length. Then grow."
- Build: executable planning (plans that actually work)

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Introduce ONE novel element in familiar plan: "Same drill. But: one new form in position 3. Plan around it."
- Reward planning for novelty
- Frame: "Almost the same plan. One new thing. Your strategy still works. Just adapt slightly."
- Build: expanding planning range (novel forms get planned for)

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Drill-Master's Path: a planning space where the player sequences body-movements strategically. Forms are available; the player must ORDER them wisely — considering fatigue, difficulty, body-state, and constraints. The aesthetic: Amber-stage war-room/dojo — the drill laid out, the body's path planned before execution.

### Interaction
- **The First Sequence (1-5):** 2-form sequence. "Which first? The harder form while fresh, or the easier to warm up?"
- **The Planned Drill (5-15):** 3-4 form sequence with fatigue consideration. "Plan the order. Fatigue hits at form 3. Sequence wisely."
- **The Fatigue Map (15-30):** Plan around known body-limits. "Your hold weakens after 6 seconds. Plan the drill so holds come early."
- **The Changing Drill (30-50):** Plan must adapt mid-execution. "Fatigue hit earlier than expected. Replan the remaining forms."
- **The Master Strategist (50+):** Full integration: complex drill-planning with fatigue-mapping, adaptation, and novel forms.

### Feedback
- Wise plan → "Well sequenced. Hard forms while fresh. Rest before the hold. Strategic body."
- Rigid plan → "Same plan as yesterday. But today you're fatigued. The wise plan adapts to TODAY's body."
- No plan → "You jumped in without planning. Fatigue hit at form 2. Next time: think first, then move."
- Overcomplex → "Too many forms. Your body can't execute this plan. Simpler. Shorter. Executable."
- Frozen → "Same familiar drill. But your body is ready for more. Plan one new form into the sequence."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Sequence length: 2-form → 3-form → 4-form → 4-form with constraints
- Fatigue complexity: predictable → variable → surprising
- Constraints: none → timing → social → multiple
- Adaptation demand: fixed plan → mid-execution replan
- Novelty: familiar forms only → mixed → novel forms included

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The First Sequence | 1-5 | 2-form basic sequencing |
| The Planned Drill | 5-15 | 3-4 form with fatigue awareness |
| The Fatigue Map | 15-30 | Planning around body-limits |
| The Changing Drill | 30-50 | Mid-execution replanning |
| The Master Strategist | 50+ | Full strategic somatic planning |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Simple sequences:** 2-3 form ordering decisions
- **Fatigue-aware sequences:** Plans that must account for body-limits
- **Constrained sequences:** Plans with timing/social/resource constraints
- **Adaptive sequences:** Plans that must change mid-execution
- **Novel-form sequences:** Plans incorporating unfamiliar movements

### Minimum pool size
- 20+ simple, 15+ fatigue-aware, 15+ constrained, 10+ adaptive, 10+ novel

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Same plan regardless of body-state → dark-addiction; no plan → dark-allergy
- Plans exceed capacity → golden-addiction; plans only familiar → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Sequencing quality:** Does the plan order forms wisely (hard-while-fresh, rest-before-holds)?
- **Fatigue anticipation:** Does the plan account for body-limits?
- **Execution success:** Does the planned sequence actually execute successfully?
- **Adaptation quality:** Can the plan change when body-state changes?
- **Novel planning:** Can they plan for unfamiliar forms?

### LLM rubric
- **Low:** Core sequencing is algorithmic (optimal order calculable). LLM for nuanced fatigue-anticipation assessment and adaptive planning evaluation.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Drag (sequence forms in order); tap (confirm plan); reorder (mid-execution replan)

### Timing requirements
- Planning time tracked; execution success measured; fatigue-point prediction accuracy

### NPC/AI requirements
- Form library (available movements with difficulty/fatigue ratings)
- Fatigue simulation (predictable body-limits for planning)
- Optimal-sequence calculator (for scoring plan quality)
- Adaptation triggers (mid-execution body-state changes)
- Plan-execution tracker (did the plan work?)

### LLM requirements
- **Low:** Generates contextual planning scenarios; evaluates adaptive planning quality. Core sequencing assessment is algorithmic.

### State persistence
- Planning history; sequencing quality scores; fatigue-anticipation accuracy; execution success rates; adaptation quality; novel-planning metrics; rigidity indicators; impulsivity indicators; overreach indicators; frozen-range indicators; drive/shadow signals; checkpoint position
