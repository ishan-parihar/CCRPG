# Somatic / Amber — Social-Cooperative Game Concept

> **Axis:** The social-cooperative axis probes somatic intelligence through SHARED BODY-DISCIPLINE — can the player move WITH others? Synchronise? Coordinate forms? At Amber, this is the GROUP BODY: the congregation moving together, the martial arts class in unison, the coordinated ritual.
>
> **Why this axis for Somatic/Amber:** Amber is the stage of GROUP BELONGING. Somatic capacity at Amber includes moving WITH the group — shared rhythm, coordinated forms, synchronised breath. This modality tests whether the player's body can be disciplined TOGETHER with others.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** "The Shared Form"
- **Core mechanic:** The player and companions must MOVE TOGETHER — synchronised forms, shared rhythm, coordinated sequences. Not solo discipline but GROUP discipline. "The form is: hold together. Move together. Breathe together. One body, many limbs."
- **Duration:** 3-5 minutes per session (infinite checkpoints; fatigue-aware)
- **Internal progression:** The First Unison → The Shared Beat → The Coordinated Form → The Changing Together → The Living Congregation

---

## 2. The Catalyst→Experience→Integration Flow

**Catalyst:** "You are not alone in this form. Others move with you. Can your body join theirs? Can you maintain YOUR form while moving WITH them? Can the group body emerge without losing the individual body?" The contact boundary is: "Can your body be disciplined together with others?"

**Unconscious response:**
- *Submergent:* How does the player's body relate to group movement? The Rigid Driller maintains own form perfectly but CANNOT SYNCHRONISE (too locked in own pattern). The Undisciplined Body cannot maintain form even solo, let alone in group. The Premature Virtuoso tries to LEAD the group (beyond their role). The Form-Refuser synchronises with familiar group-forms but freezes at novel coordinated movement.
- *Emergent:* Can they move with others genuinely — maintaining own form while synchronising with the group?

**Integration path:** Rewards GENUINE GROUP EMBODIMENT — individual form maintained within group synchrony. Not fusion (losing self), not isolation (ignoring group), not domination (controlling group), not rigidity (refusing new group-forms).

**Successful integration:** The player's body can be disciplined together with others — the social dimension of embodied discipline.

## 3. Drive Probing (All 4 × Both Domains)

### Agency Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining own form within group movement.
- Healthy: Maintains own form quality while synchronising; doesn't sacrifice accuracy for group
- Pathological: Loses own form in group (fusion); OR: ignores group entirely (isolation)
- **Detection:** Whether own form survives group context

### Agency Golden-Domain
**Context:** Contributing to group movement (leading a beat, setting pace).
- Healthy: Leads when appropriate; contributes to group rhythm with confidence
- Pathological: Cannot lead; OR: dominates group movement
- **Detection:** Leadership quality in group movement

### Communion Dark-Domain
**Context:** Adapting to group rhythm (matching others' timing).
- Healthy: Adapts own timing to match group without losing form quality
- Pathological: Cannot adapt (too rigid); OR: over-adapts (loses own form)
- **Detection:** Adaptation quality — timing match without form loss

### Communion Golden-Domain
**Context:** Genuine group-body emergence (moving as one).
- Healthy: Experiences genuine synchrony; group movement exceeds individual capacity
- Pathological: Cannot achieve genuine synchrony; always slightly separate
- **Detection:** Whether true group-body emerges (synchrony quality)

### Eros Dark-Domain
**Context:** Sustained group synchrony over time.
- Healthy: Maintains group synchrony across extended practice
- Pathological: Synchrony degrades over time; OR: compulsive synchrony (cannot stop)
- **Detection:** Group synchrony sustainability

### Eros Golden-Domain
**Context:** Growing group complexity (harder coordinated forms).
- Healthy: Group coordination grows in complexity as capacity grows
- Pathological: Cannot grow together; OR: pushes group beyond capacity
- **Detection:** Collaborative growth quality

### Agape Dark-Domain
**Context:** Maintaining group capacity across sessions.
- Healthy: Returns to group with maintained coordination
- Pathological: Group coordination degrades between sessions
- **Detection:** Cross-session group stability

### Agape Golden-Domain
**Context:** Whether group-body awareness appears in daily life.
- Healthy: Coordinates movement with others naturally (walking pace, door-holding)
- Pathological: Group coordination exists only in explicit practice
- **Detection:** Spontaneous social-somatic coordination

## 4. Shadow Surfacing (All 4 Quadrants)

### Dark-Addiction: "The Rigid Driller"
**How it surfaces:** Maintains own form PERFECTLY but CANNOT SYNCHRONISE. Too locked in own pattern to adapt to group timing. The body is disciplined but ALONE — even in a group.
- **Detection signals:** High solo accuracy + low group synchrony; own timing rigid regardless of group; cannot adapt pace; "I'm doing the form correctly" (but out of sync); isolated within group

### Dark-Allergy: "The Undisciplined Body"
**How it surfaces:** Cannot maintain form even in group support. The group moves together but this body is chaotic. Cannot benefit from group rhythm.
- **Detection signals:** Low accuracy even with group support; cannot match group timing; chaotic within coordinated context; group rhythm doesn't help; "I can't keep up"

### Golden-Addiction: "The Premature Virtuoso"
**How it surfaces:** Tries to LEAD or DIRECT the group beyond their role. Attempts to set pace, correct others, manage the group-body. Domination disguised as contribution.
- **Detection signals:** Attempts to lead when not designated; corrects others' form; sets own pace expecting group to follow; "everyone should do it this way"; manages rather than participates

### Golden-Allergy: "The Form-Refuser"
**How it surfaces:** Synchronises well with FAMILIAR group-forms but FREEZES at novel coordinated movement. Cannot adapt to new group patterns.
- **Detection signals:** Good synchrony on familiar group-forms; failure at novel coordination; "we always do it this way"; cannot adapt to new group patterns; anxiety at novel group-forms

## 5. Healing/Evolution Mechanics

### Heal/Evolve Vector (Agape + Agency for dark shadows)

**When Dark-Addiction detected:**
- Present group-forms where SYNCHRONY is the measure (not solo accuracy): "The form is correct only when you're WITH the group. Timing matters more than perfection."
- Reward synchrony over solo accuracy: "You matched the group. That's the form. Together."
- Frame: "Discipline includes joining. Your form is correct when it's WITH others. Not alone."
- Build: group-synchrony capacity (discipline includes coordination)

**When Dark-Allergy detected:**
- Start with SIMPLE group-matching: "Just match the beat. One tap. Together. That's group movement."
- Use group rhythm as SUPPORT (not demand): "The group holds the beat FOR you. Let it carry you."
- Frame: "The group helps. Their rhythm supports yours. Let them carry the beat. You just join."
- Build: group-supported discipline (others help, not hinder)

### Evolve/Heal Vector (Eros + Communion for golden shadows)

**When Golden-Addiction detected:**
- Present tasks where EQUAL PARTICIPATION is required: "Not leading. Participating. Your role is to match, not direct."
- Block leadership attempts; reward equal contribution
- Frame: "The group-body has no head. All limbs. Equal. Your role: participate. Not manage."
- Build: equal participation (contributor not director)

**When Golden-Allergy detected:**
- Introduce SLIGHT novelty in group-form: "Same group. Same rhythm. One new movement. Together."
- Reward group-adaptation to novelty
- Frame: "The group is learning together. You're not alone in the new form. Everyone's adapting."
- Build: group-supported novelty (new forms are less threatening together)

## 6. Game Design

### Setup
The Shared Form: a group movement space. Player and companions move together — synchronised forms, shared rhythm, coordinated sequences. The aesthetic: Amber-stage monastery courtyard — practitioners moving in unison, the group-body as sacred form.

### Interaction
- **The First Unison (1-5):** Simple beat-matching with companion. "Tap together. Same beat. One rhythm."
- **The Shared Beat (5-15):** Sustained group rhythm. "Hold the beat together. 120 BPM. Stay with the group."
- **The Coordinated Form (15-30):** Shared form-execution. "The form together. Same sequence. Same timing."
- **The Changing Together (30-50):** Group adapts to new form. "New form. Learn it together. Synchronise the new."
- **The Living Congregation (50+):** Full group-body integration: complex coordinated forms with role-flexibility.

### Feedback
- Good synchrony → "Together. One body, many limbs. The group-form holds. Beautiful."
- Isolated → "You're accurate but alone. The group is HERE. Match their timing. Join."
- Chaotic → "Simpler. Just the beat. One tap with the group. That's enough. Join the rhythm."
- Dominating → "Not leading. Joining. Equal. Match THEIR pace. Participate."
- Frozen at novel → "New form for everyone. You're not alone. The group learns together. Join them."

### Difficulty Adaptation
- Group size: dyad → triad → small group
- Synchrony precision: ±50ms → ±40ms → ±30ms
- Form complexity: beat-matching → simple forms → complex sequences
- Role flexibility: fixed roles → occasional swap → frequent rotation
- Novelty: familiar group-forms → slight variations → novel coordinated movement

### Internal Progression
| Phase | Checkpoint range | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| The First Unison | 1-5 | Simple dyad beat-matching |
| The Shared Beat | 5-15 | Sustained group rhythm |
| The Coordinated Form | 15-30 | Shared form-execution |
| The Changing Together | 30-50 | Group adaptation to novelty |
| The Living Congregation | 50+ | Full group-body integration |

## 7. Item Pool Specification

### Item types
- **Beat-matching:** Simple synchrony with companion(s)
- **Sustained rhythm:** Extended group beat-maintenance
- **Coordinated forms:** Shared sequences executed together
- **Role-rotation:** Same form, different positions/roles
- **Novel group-forms:** New coordinated movements learned together

### Minimum pool size
- 20+ beat-matching, 15+ sustained, 15+ coordinated, 10+ role-rotation, 10+ novel

### Drive/shadow mapping
- Accurate but isolated → dark-addiction; chaotic even in group → dark-allergy
- Dominates/leads inappropriately → golden-addiction; freezes at novel group-forms → golden-allergy

---

## 8. Scoring Rubric

### Capacity scoring
- **Synchrony precision:** Mean deviation from group timing (ms)
- **Form-in-group:** Own form accuracy while synchronising
- **Adaptation quality:** How quickly/smoothly does player match group changes?
- **Role-flexibility:** Can they function in different group positions?
- **Group-body emergence:** Does genuine synchrony emerge (beyond mechanical matching)?

### LLM rubric
- **Low:** Core synchrony is algorithmic (ms deviation). LLM for nuanced assessment of genuine-vs-mechanical synchrony, domination detection, and group-body emergence quality.

---

## 9. Technical Requirements

### Input types
- Rhythm-tap (synchrony); sequence-tap (coordinated forms); hold (sustained group posture)

### Timing requirements
- Millisecond precision for synchrony measurement
- Group-timing comparison (player vs. companion(s) vs. target beat)

### NPC/AI requirements
- Companion system (NPCs with own movement patterns and timing)
- Group-rhythm system (shared beat generation and tracking)
- Synchrony measurement (player-to-group deviation)
- Domination detection (player attempting to lead/control)
- Role-rotation system (changing positions within group)

### LLM requirements
- **Low:** Companion behaviour generation; nuanced group-dynamics assessment. Core synchrony metrics algorithmic.

### State persistence
- Synchrony history; form-in-group accuracy; adaptation metrics; role-flexibility scores; group-body emergence indicators; isolation indicators; domination indicators; chaos indicators; rigidity indicators; drive/shadow signals; fatigue state; checkpoint position
