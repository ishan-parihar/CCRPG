# Somatic / Orange — Strategic-Planning Game Concept

> **Axis:** Planning physical training — structuring skill acquisition across time, periodization, managing fatigue and recovery.  **Why this axis for this module:** Orange somatic intelligence includes the RATIONAL DESIGN of training — the hypothetico-deductive approach to physical development that distinguishes scientific training from mere repetition.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** The Architect of Training
- **Core Mechanic:** Player designs, schedules, and adjusts physical training programs for an avatar. Outcomes reveal whether the plan optimizes sustainably or drives toward breakdown/stagnation.
- **Duration:** 5–10 minutes per session, infinite checkpoint.
- **Internal Progression:** Single-session planning → weekly periodization → multi-domain scheduling → fatigue-recovery balancing → long-term program design → adaptive replanning based on body feedback.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

- **Unique Presentation:** A training architect's studio with timeline boards, body-state dashboards, and outcome projections. The player is the coach designing programs for their avatar-athlete.
- **Differs From Others:** No physical execution — pure planning and strategy. Reveals the MIND's relationship to the body's training, not the body's direct performance.
- **Uniquely Surfaces:** DA (over-scheduled, no recovery, volume addiction), GA (plans include "intuitive days" and "body wisdom sessions" without structured skill work), DAll (minimal planning, avoids commitment to physical development), GAll (perfectly periodized but ignores subjective readiness).
- **Successful Integration:** Player designs programs that balance progressive overload with recovery, structure with flexibility, metrics with felt-readiness, ambition with sustainability.

## 3. Game Design

- **Setup:** Avatar-athlete profile with current capacities, fatigue state, goals, and available training time. Player designs the next training block.
- **Interaction Phases:**
  1. Assessment — review avatar's current state, recent performance, recovery status
  2. Goal-setting — select priorities for the training block
  3. Programming — allocate sessions, intensities, domains, and recovery
  4. Execution simulation — watch the plan unfold with emerging complications
  5. Adaptation — adjust the plan based on simulated outcomes and body feedback
- **Feedback Examples:**
  1. "Your program has zero recovery days — the avatar broke down in week 3" (DA)
  2. "Your program is all 'listen to the body' with no progressive structure" (GA)
  3. "You planned one easy session per week — the avatar stagnated" (DAll)
  4. "Your periodization is textbook-perfect but ignores the avatar's reported fatigue" (GAll)
  5. "Progressive overload with built-in deloads and readiness-based adjustments — sustainable growth" (integration)
- **Difficulty Adaptation:** Planning complexity increases with demonstrated capacity. Complications become more ambiguous. Avatar feedback becomes more subtle (requiring interpretation rather than obvious signals).
- **Internal Progression Table:**

| Level | Planning Scope | Shadow Probe |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Single session design | Baseline planning orientation |
| 4–6 | Weekly block with recovery | DA: no rest days; DAll: minimal sessions |
| 7–9 | Multi-domain periodization | GAll: ignores subjective readiness |
| 10–12 | Long-term with complications | GA: abandons structure for "intuition" |
| 13+ | Adaptive replanning under uncertainty | All shadows in tension |

## 4. Item Pool

- **Training Variables (20):** Volume, intensity, frequency, density, specificity, variation, progressive overload, deload timing, domain selection, session duration, warm-up structure, cool-down protocol, skill vs. conditioning ratio, technique vs. capacity work, competition preparation, recovery modalities, cross-training allocation, periodization model, autoregulation rules, fatigue management thresholds
- **Complications (18):** Unexpected fatigue spike, minor injury, sleep disruption, motivation drop, plateau in primary domain, rapid improvement requiring plan adjustment, schedule conflict, recovery slower than expected, new domain interest emerging, competition date moved, training partner unavailable, equipment limitation, weather/environment change, life stress increase, illness onset, travel disruption, overtraining symptoms, boredom with current program
- **Avatar Profiles (15):** The eager beginner (high motivation, low capacity), the experienced athlete (high capacity, injury history), the returning practitioner (past mastery, current deconditioning), the multi-sport athlete (breadth without depth), the specialist (depth without breadth), the over-trainer (high drive, poor recovery), the under-trainer (low drive, high potential), the injured athlete (capacity limited by healing), the aging practitioner (declining capacity, increasing wisdom), the perfectionist (technique obsession, fear of failure), the competitor (outcome-focused, process-avoidant), the hobbyist (enjoyment-focused, growth-avoidant), the intellectual athlete (plans perfectly, executes poorly), the intuitive mover (executes well, plans poorly), the balanced practitioner (integration model)
- **Periodization Models (15):** Linear progression, undulating periodization, block periodization, conjugate method, autoregulated training, intuitive periodization, hybrid models, competition peaking, off-season development, rehabilitation progression, skill-acquisition phases, capacity-building blocks, maintenance phases, exploratory phases, integration blocks
- **Outcome Scenarios (12):** Sustainable improvement, burnout and breakdown, stagnation through under-challenge, injury through over-load, breakthrough after deload, regression after over-reach, plateau requiring program change, successful peak for competition, failed peak from poor timing, recovery-driven improvement, cross-domain transfer success, integration of structure and flexibility

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input Types:** Drag-and-drop scheduling, slider-based intensity selection, dropdown domain selection, timeline manipulation, parameter adjustment.
- **Timing:** No time pressure on planning phases. Execution simulation runs at accelerated time. Adaptation decisions may have mild time pressure.
- **NPC/AI:** Avatar-athlete with simulated physiology responding to training stimuli. Complication generator introduces realistic disruptions.
- **LLM:** Evaluates plan quality, generates complication narratives, provides coaching feedback on program design, assesses shadow patterns in planning style.
- **State Persistence:** Planning history, outcome patterns, shadow-indicator trajectories from planning style, avatar progression across sessions, program-design evolution, adaptation quality scores.
