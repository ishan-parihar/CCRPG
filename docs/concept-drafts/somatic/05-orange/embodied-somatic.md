# Somatic / Orange — Embodied-Somatic Game Concept

> **Axis:** The body training itself — progressive skill acquisition, technique mastery, the felt-sense of physical excellence.  **Why this axis for this module:** This is the PRIMARY modality for Somatic/Orange — the body directly practicing and mastering multi-domain technique through rational, progressive training.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** The Forge of Mastery
- **Core Mechanic:** Progressive physical skill acquisition through technique trees. Player trains, drills, and masters increasingly complex movement patterns with real-time biofeedback on execution quality.
- **Duration:** 5–12 minutes per session, infinite checkpoint.
- **Internal Progression:** Single technique acquisition → technique chaining → cross-domain transfer → fatigue-managed execution → technique-switching under load → mastery with felt-sense integration.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

- **Unique Presentation:** A training dojo that evolves with the player — bare floor becomes equipped space becomes multi-domain facility. The environment reflects accumulated mastery.
- **Differs From Others:** The body IS the game. Not measuring (deterministic), not talking about (language-reflective), not choosing between (scenario-choice) — actually DOING the physical practice.
- **Uniquely Surfaces:** DA (trains compulsively without rest, pushes past pain), DAll (avoids skill progression, stays in comfort zone of mastered basics), GA (claims mastery without demonstrating it), GAll (executes perfectly but refuses to feel the movement).
- **Successful Integration:** Player demonstrates genuine technique mastery AND maintains embodied awareness throughout execution — the body is both precise and alive.

## 3. Game Design

- **Setup:** Training space with technique tree visible. Player selects a domain and begins progressive skill acquisition.
- **Interaction Phases:**
  1. Introduction — new technique demonstrated, player attempts basic form
  2. Drilling — repetition with progressive refinement, feedback on precision
  3. Chaining — combining techniques into sequences
  4. Pressure — executing under fatigue, time pressure, or complexity load
  5. Integration — executing with awareness, choosing effort level, maintaining felt-sense
- **Feedback Examples:**
  1. "Technique precision: 94% — but your input pattern shows tension accumulating" (DA early warning)
  2. "You've repeated this basic pattern 20 times without attempting the next level" (DAll indicator)
  3. "You skipped the drilling phase and attempted the advanced chain — form collapsed" (GA indicator)
  4. "Perfect execution — but you ignored the recovery prompt three times" (GAll indicator)
  5. "High precision maintained through self-paced effort with voluntary recovery — integrated mastery" (integration)
- **Difficulty Adaptation:** Technique complexity scales with demonstrated capacity. Recovery prompts appear based on fatigue modeling. New domains unlock when current domain reaches threshold. Shadow-specific challenges introduced at appropriate levels.
- **Internal Progression Table:**

| Level | Training Focus | Shadow Probe |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Single-domain basic techniques | Baseline motor capacity |
| 4–6 | Technique chaining and sequencing | DA: refuses rest between chains |
| 7–9 | Cross-domain transfer challenges | DAll: refuses new domains |
| 10–12 | Fatigue-managed complex execution | DA: pushes past breakdown |
| 13+ | Mastery with embodied awareness | GAll: executes without feeling |

## 4. Item Pool

- **Technique Domains (6):** Precision (fine motor, threading, targeting), Power (explosive movement, force generation, impact), Flow (continuous movement, rhythm, transitions), Balance (stability, recovery, single-point control), Coordination (bilateral, cross-limb, multi-point), Speed (reaction, acceleration, rapid sequencing)
- **Technique Trees per Domain (15+ each):** Basic form → refined form → chained form → pressured form → integrated form. Each domain has 3 branches of 5 levels. Example Precision: steady-hand hold → moving-target track → multi-target sequence → fatigue-precision maintenance → precision-with-awareness
- **Fatigue Challenges (18):** Maintain precision at 70% capacity, execute new technique while fatigued, chain familiar techniques under load, cross-domain switch while tired, recovery-gated progression (must rest to unlock next), endurance sequences with precision gates, progressive overload with form maintenance, deload challenges (can you reduce effort?), fatigue-awareness probes (identify your current state), pacing challenges (distribute effort across time), strategic rest placement, active recovery techniques, fatigue-differentiation (tired vs. injured vs. bored), effort-calibration (match prescribed intensity), sustainable-pace finding, peak-then-recover cycles, fatigue-masked skill tests, recovery-as-skill challenges
- **Cross-Domain Transfers (15):** Precision→Power (controlled force), Power→Flow (explosive into continuous), Flow→Balance (movement into stillness), Balance→Coordination (stability into complexity), Coordination→Speed (multi-limb into rapid), Speed→Precision (fast into accurate), novel combinations, three-domain chains, domain-switching under time pressure, unfamiliar domain onboarding, schema abstraction challenges, technique generalization tests, creative recombination, interference resistance, consolidation after transfer
- **Integration Challenges (12):** Execute with eyes closed (felt-sense only), describe what you feel while executing, choose your own intensity for this set, design your own technique chain, teach this technique to an NPC, execute at 70% effort (not maximum), find the minimum effort for this precision, notice where tension lives during execution, execute the same technique three different ways, find flow within structure, let the body choose the next technique, integrate rest as part of the practice

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input Types:** Tap sequences, hold-and-release timing, swipe patterns, multi-touch coordination, rhythm input, pressure modulation, gesture recognition.
- **Timing:** Real-time input with frame-accurate feedback. Technique execution windows scale with difficulty. Rest periods tracked and valued.
- **NPC/AI:** Training partner NPC demonstrates techniques. Adaptive coach provides form feedback. Fatigue model gates progression.
- **LLM:** Secondary. Generates technique descriptions, provides coaching language, interprets integration challenge responses.
- **State Persistence:** Technique tree progress per domain, precision scores over time, fatigue-management patterns, cross-domain transfer rates, shadow-indicator trajectories, drive-health evolution, session-to-session improvement curves.
