# Somatic / Orange — Social-Cooperative Game Concept

> **Axis:** Collaborative physical practice — synchronized movement with NPC partners, shared physical challenges, teaching technique.  **Why this axis for this module:** Orange somatic mastery is typically solitary and self-authored; the social axis reveals whether mastery can extend into relational physical practice without collapsing into either isolation or conformity.

---

## 1. Game Identity

- **Title:** The Training Circle
- **Core Mechanic:** Player engages in synchronized physical challenges with NPC partners — matching rhythms, coordinating sequences, teaching techniques, and adapting to others' capacities while maintaining personal mastery.
- **Duration:** 5–10 minutes per session, infinite checkpoint.
- **Internal Progression:** Simple rhythm-matching with one partner → complex coordination with multiple NPCs → teaching technique to less-skilled NPCs → adapting personal technique to complement partners → leading group physical challenges.

## 2. Catalyst Delivery

- **Unique Presentation:** A training hall where NPC practitioners of varying skill levels invite the player into shared physical practice. The social dynamic IS the challenge — not just the movement.
- **Differs From Others:** Physical skill is assumed (embodied-somatic establishes it). Here the question is: can mastery RELATE? Can technique serve connection without losing precision?
- **Uniquely Surfaces:** GAll (refuses to slow down for partners, maintains technique fortress), GA (over-attunes to partners, loses personal form), DA (dominates the group, uses others as metrics), DAll (avoids group practice entirely).
- **Successful Integration:** Player synchronizes with partners while maintaining personal excellence, teaches without condescension, learns from others without losing self, leads without dominating.

## 3. Game Design

- **Setup:** Training circle with 1–4 NPC partners of varying skill levels. Shared physical challenge presented requiring coordination.
- **Interaction Phases:**
  1. Attunement — match partner's rhythm and energy level
  2. Coordination — execute synchronized sequences together
  3. Adaptation — adjust personal technique to complement partner's style
  4. Teaching — transmit technique knowledge to less-skilled NPC
  5. Integration — lead group practice that honors all skill levels
- **Feedback Examples:**
  1. "You maintained perfect personal form but your partner couldn't follow — technique without communion" (GAll)
  2. "You matched your partner perfectly but lost your own technique quality" (GA)
  3. "You outperformed everyone but trained alone within the group" (DA)
  4. "You avoided the group challenge and practiced in the corner" (DAll)
  5. "You adapted your rhythm to include your partner while maintaining your own precision — relational mastery" (integration)
- **Difficulty Adaptation:** Partner skill gaps widen, requiring more adaptation. Group size increases. Teaching challenges become more complex. Social-physical dilemmas intensify.
- **Internal Progression Table:**

| Level | Social Challenge | Shadow Probe |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Simple rhythm-matching with skilled partner | Baseline social-physical capacity |
| 4–6 | Coordination with less-skilled partner | GAll: refuses to adapt; GA: over-adapts |
| 7–9 | Teaching technique to novice NPC | DA: dominates; DAll: avoids |
| 10–12 | Leading multi-level group practice | All shadows in social tension |
| 13+ | Integrated relational mastery | Shadow-free collaborative practice |

## 4. Item Pool

- **Synchronization Challenges (20):** Mirror-rhythm matching, complementary-rhythm weaving, leader-follower alternation, simultaneous precision sequences, call-and-response patterns, tempo negotiation, dynamic speed matching, asymmetric coordination, group pulse maintenance, partner-dependent timing, shared acceleration curves, synchronized deceleration, mutual recovery pacing, energy-level matching, intensity negotiation, flow-state co-creation, rhythm repair after disruption, graceful re-entry after error, shared fatigue management, collective peak timing
- **Teaching Scenarios (18):** Demonstrate basic technique to novice, break complex movement into learnable steps, adapt instruction to partner's learning style, provide feedback without discouraging, scaffold difficulty for struggling learner, challenge advanced learner appropriately, teach through demonstration vs. verbal instruction, correct form without creating tension, encourage risk-taking in cautious learner, slow down impatient learner, teach recovery and rest as skills, transmit felt-sense (not just mechanics), teach adaptation rather than rigid form, model sustainable practice, teach self-correction, transmit confidence through physical presence, teach technique-switching, model integration of effort and ease
- **Adaptation Challenges (15):** Slow personal tempo to match partner, increase intensity to challenge partner, modify technique for partner's body type, find shared rhythm between different styles, maintain quality while accommodating limitation, lead without imposing, follow without losing self, negotiate shared goals, balance personal growth with group service, adapt to unexpected partner behavior, recover synchronization after disruption, maintain personal standards in group context, find excellence within constraint, create space for partner's expression, integrate personal and relational goals
- **Group Dynamics (15):** Multi-level group with skill gaps, competitive partner who pushes too hard, passive partner who under-contributes, partner who needs encouragement, partner who resists instruction, group that moves too fast, group that moves too slow, leadership vacuum requiring initiative, over-led group needing space, conflict between group members, celebration of shared achievement, group fatigue management, collective decision-making about intensity, shared goal negotiation, group recovery practice
- **Integration Probes (12):** Lead and follow in the same session, teach while learning, maintain personal excellence within group constraint, find flow with a difficult partner, create group coherence without domination, accept help without losing agency, offer help without condescension, compete and cooperate simultaneously, maintain boundaries while connecting, express individual style within group form, find personal growth through service, experience communion without losing agency

## 5. Technical Requirements

- **Input Types:** Rhythm-matching taps, timing coordination with NPC cues, selection of teaching approaches, intensity/tempo sliders for adaptation, leadership/following mode toggles.
- **Timing:** Synchronization windows scale with partner skill level. Teaching feedback is real-time. Group coordination requires sustained attention.
- **NPC/AI:** Multiple NPC partners with distinct skill profiles, learning curves, personalities, and physical styles. NPCs respond to player's teaching quality and social approach.
- **LLM:** Generates NPC dialogue and social responses, evaluates teaching quality, interprets player's relational style, adapts NPC behavior to surface specific shadows.
- **State Persistence:** Relational patterns with each NPC partner, teaching effectiveness scores, synchronization quality over time, social-physical shadow indicators, group leadership style evolution, communion-agency balance trajectories.
